How Much Concrete Do I Need?

A simple guide with a worked example, a slab table and answers to the questions people actually ask.

Short answer

A 10 ft × 10 ft slab, 4 inches thick, needs 1.23 cubic yards of concrete, and 1.36 cubic yards with 10% extra for waste. Use the formula below with your own slab size and thickness, or try the Concrete Slab Calculator for an instant result.

The formula

Volume in cubic feet = length × width × thickness, with all three in feet. Cubic yards = cubic feet ÷ 27, because one cubic yard is 27 cubic feet. Add 10% for waste and round up, because spillage, uneven ground and formwork always take a little extra.

Worked example: 10 ft × 10 ft slab, 4 inches thick

Step 1: convert the thickness to feet: 4 inches ÷ 12 = 0.333 feet. Step 2: the volume is 10 × 10 × 0.333 = 33.33 cubic feet. Step 3: convert to cubic yards: 33.33 ÷ 27 = 1.2346 cubic yards (about 1.23). Step 4: add 10% for waste: 1.2346 × 1.10 = 1.36 cubic yards to order. That is the same result the Concrete Slab Calculator gives for these numbers.

How to measure your slab

Measure the length and width along the inside of the formwork, and measure the thickness from the base to the top of the form. If the slab has more than one section, measure each section separately and add the volumes together. Always convert inches to feet before multiplying: divide inches by 12.

What changes the number

Thickness changes it fastest, because volume is directly proportional to thickness: doubling the thickness doubles the concrete. Waste matters too, because uneven ground, spillage and formwork gaps all consume extra mix. For footings, driveways and walls, the shape is different, so use the calculator made for that shape.

Common mistakes

The most common mistake is multiplying inches directly with feet, which inflates the answer by 12 times. The next is ordering with no waste allowance and running short mid-pour. The third is measuring only one section of a multi-section slab and forgetting the rest.

Frequently asked questions

What is the concrete volume formula?

Volume in cubic feet = length x width x thickness, with all three in feet. Cubic yards = cubic feet / 27. Add 10% for waste and round up.

How much concrete for a 10 ft x 10 ft slab, 4 inches thick?

1.23 cubic yards, and 1.36 cubic yards with 10% extra for waste.

How many bags of concrete do I need?

It depends on the bag size and the mix, so bags are counted separately from cubic yards. Use the concrete bags calculator, enter your volume and your bag size, and it gives the bag count.

Do I need extra concrete for waste?

Yes. Add 10% for spillage, uneven ground and formwork gaps. Ordering exactly the calculated volume almost always runs short mid-pour.

What if my slab is not a rectangle?

Split it into rectangles, work out each rectangle's volume with the same formula, and add the volumes together. Then convert the total to cubic yards and add waste.
