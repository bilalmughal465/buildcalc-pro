How to calculate drywall sheets

Drywall is sold by the sheet, and the standard sheet is 4 ft by 8 ft, which is 32 sq ft. Find the wall area, add waste for cuts, then divide by 32 and round up.

Formula: sheets = wall area (sq ft) × (1 + waste %) ÷ 32, rounded up

Worked example: 12 ft × 8 ft wall

Wall area = 12 × 8 = 96 sq ft. Add 10% waste: 105.6 sq ft. Divide by 32: 3.3, rounded up to 4 sheets.

Whole rooms

For a whole room, add up every wall plus the ceiling. You can run the total through this calculator once, or do each wall separately and add the sheets.

Which thickness?

Half-inch drywall is standard for walls. Five-eighths inch is used for ceilings and fire-rated walls. Quarter-inch is for curved surfaces and overlays.

Cost tips

Frequently asked questions

How many drywall sheets do I need for a 12x8 wall?

A 12 ft by 8 ft wall needs 4 sheets of 4x8 drywall, with 10% waste.

How big is a sheet of drywall?

The standard sheet is 4 ft by 8 ft, which is 32 sq ft. Longer 10 ft and 12 ft sheets exist for tall walls.

How do I count drywall for a whole room?

Add up all wall areas plus the ceiling, then run them through this calculator as one total area, or do each wall separately.

How much waste should I add?

Add 10% for straight walls and 15% for rooms with many corners, windows and doors.

What screws do I need?

Use drywall screws, about 32 per sheet, spaced 12 inches on ceilings and 16 inches on walls. This calculator counts sheets only.

Are the prices accurate?

Prices are editable estimates. The defaults are typical US figures, and your local price can be higher or lower.
