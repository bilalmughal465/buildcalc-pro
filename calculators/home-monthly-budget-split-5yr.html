Home Monthly Budget Split Calculator (5 Years)

Split monthly income into needs, wants and savings. This home version uses a 5-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Needs are 50 percent, wants are 30 percent and savings are 20 percent of income.

How it works

This 50-30-20 split is a planning guide, not a rule. Adjust it to your real costs.

Worked example

Monthly home income $4,000, term 5 years. Using the page formula, needs are 50 percent of $4,000, which is $2,000 a month; wants are 30 percent, which is $1,200 a month; and savings are 20 percent, which is $800 a month. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults, and it is the same on every page in this group because the term is not an input.

As an illustration only, not the output of this page, keeping that same $4,000 a month for 5 years (60 months) would mean total income of $240,000, split into $120,000 needs, $72,000 wants and $48,000 savings. In real life home income, rent or mortgage payments, and prices will not stay the same for 5 years, so these totals are a planning picture, not a promise.

Who this term suits for a home household: a home household getting its spending under control on a short deadline, for example a family saving a home deposit, clearing a small home improvement loan, or cutting grocery and utility waste before a planned move in about 5 years, where the $800 a month of savings only works if needs are really kept inside $2,000.

FAQs

What is the 50-30-20 rule?
It is a simple budget guide: 50 percent needs, 30 percent wants and 20 percent savings.

Is this home calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is $800 a month of savings realistic for a home household on $4,000?
It depends on the home's fixed costs. If rent or a mortgage, utilities, groceries and transport already pass $2,000 a month, the 50 percent needs share is too small and the split must be adjusted before the $800 savings figure is trusted.

What home costs count as needs in the $2,000 figure?
Needs are the costs a home cannot skip: rent or mortgage payments, utilities, groceries, basic transport, insurance and minimum loan payments. Eating out, streaming subscriptions and new furniture are wants, not needs, even when they feel regular.

What if home rent rises during the 5 years and $2,000 no longer covers needs?
Then the split must be reworked, not stretched. Either wants fall below $1,200, savings fall below $800 for a while, or income must rise, because a budget that pretends needs still fit in $2,000 will quietly fail.
