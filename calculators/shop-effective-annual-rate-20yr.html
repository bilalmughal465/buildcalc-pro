Shop Effective Annual Rate Calculator (20 Years)

Convert a nominal annual rate to an effective annual rate. This shop version uses a 20-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Effective rate equals one plus nominal rate divided by periods, to the power of periods, minus one.

How it works

More frequent compounding raises the effective rate slightly above the nominal rate.

Worked example

Nominal rate 6 percent, compounding periods 12 a year, term 20 years. The monthly rate is 6 percent divided by 12, which is 0.5 percent a month. Using the page formula, the effective rate equals 1.005 to the power of 12, minus one, which is 6.17 percent a year. That is the calculator's stated-formula result for these defaults. On $10,000 for one year, interest at the effective rate is $616.78, while interest at the nominal 6 percent alone would be $600.00, so monthly compounding adds $16.78 in the first year.

As an illustration only, not the output of this page, $10,000 grown at a steady 6.17 percent a year for 20 years would reach $33,102.04, with interest of $23,102.04. That illustration only shows what the same yearly rate would do over the term if it stayed fixed, which no real shop loan or account rate does.

Who this term suits for a shop owner: a shop owner taking a longer premises loan, where twenty years of monthly compounding makes the gap between the nominal rate and the effective rate worth checking before signing.

FAQs

What is an effective rate?
The effective rate is the true yearly rate after allowing for compounding during the year.

Is this shop calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is 6.17% a realistic true rate for a 20-year shop loan?
It is the correct effective rate for a 6% nominal rate compounded monthly, but only while those terms hold. Over 20 years rates, fees, and shop takings will change, so it is a planning figure, not a forecast.

What if a 20-year shop loan compounds quarterly instead of monthly?
Quarterly compounding gives a lower effective rate than monthly compounding: (1 + 0.06/4)^4 - 1 is about 6.14%, against 6.17% monthly. The less often interest compounds, the closer the effective rate sits to the nominal rate.

Should a 20-year shop loan be judged on the effective rate alone?
No. The effective rate shows the true yearly cost, but a shop should also weigh fees, the total amount repaid, early-repayment charges, and whether the loan can be refinanced if rates fall.
