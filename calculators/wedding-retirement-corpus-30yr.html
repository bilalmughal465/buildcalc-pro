Wedding Retirement Corpus Calculator (30 Years)

Estimate a retirement corpus from monthly savings. This wedding version uses a 30-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The Future value result shows the total retirement fund your regular monthly savings could build up over 30 years at the rate you entered, combining every monthly payment with the growth earned on those payments.

Formula

Corpus uses the monthly rate and number of monthly savings to grow each saving with interest.

How it works

Starting earlier matters more than saving a little more later, because interest has longer to compound.

Worked example

Monthly saving $500, annual rate 7 percent, term 30 years. The monthly rate is 7 percent divided by 12, which is about 0.5833 percent a month, and the number of savings is 30 times 12, which is 360 months. Using the page formula, the future value is $500 times one plus the monthly rate to the power of 360, minus one, divided by the monthly rate, which gives $609,985.50. That is the calculator output for these defaults. The couple pays in $180,000 in total, so $429,985.50 of the corpus is interest. That is the same monthly saving and rate as on every page in this group; only the time input changes, and that alone produces the different corpus figures across the years.

Who this term suits for a wedding: a wedding fund that is really a retirement plan in disguise, started a generation ahead, where the wedding itself is long past by the time the fund matures.

FAQs

When should I start saving for retirement?
As early as possible. Time in the market allows compounding to do more of the work.

Is this wedding calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does the 30-year figure pass $600,000?
Because 360 savings of $500 compound for up to 30 years. The couple pays in $180,000 and interest adds $429,985.50, giving a corpus of $609,985.50. Interest is more than double the amount paid in.

Can a $609,985.50 corpus still fund a wedding after 30 years of price rises?
Only if the wedding budget is judged in future prices. Thirty years of price rises will make a wedding far more expensive than today, so the corpus should be compared with the future cost, not today's cost.

What should a couple do if the money is needed early?
Taking money out early costs more than the amount withdrawn, because that money stops compounding. A wedding fund meant for 30 years should not be treated as an emergency fund.
