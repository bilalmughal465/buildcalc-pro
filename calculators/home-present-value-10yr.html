Home Present Value Calculator (10 Years)

Find how much a future amount is worth today. This home version uses a 10-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Present value equals future amount divided by one plus rate to the power of years.

How it works

A future amount is worth less today because money today can earn interest.

Worked example

Future amount $50,000, annual rate 5%, term 10 years. First, one plus the rate is 1.05, and 1.05 to the power of 10 is about 1.62889. Using the page formula, present value is $50,000 divided by 1.62889, which is $30,695.66. That is the calculator's own output for the page defaults. The discount is $50,000 minus $30,695.66, which is $19,304.34, so waiting 10 years removes $19,304.34 of value in today's money at 5% a year. For a home buyer this is a medium-term check: a house move, an extension, or a new heating system due in about 10 years looks much smaller in today's money than its future price suggests.

Who this term suits for a home buyer: a homeowner saving toward a house move or an extension in about 10 years, or a family planning a new boiler and heating system before the old one fails, checking what today's money must be set aside while there is still time to save steadily.

FAQs

What is present value?
Present value is what a future sum is worth today after allowing for interest over time.

Is this home calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does $50,000 in 10 years fall to $30,695.66 today?
Because $30,695.66 growing at 5% a year for 10 years reaches $50,000, so the future sum is divided by 1.05 to the power of 10, which is about 1.62889.

Is the 10-year figure useful for a home extension?
Yes, as a planning guide: it puts a future extension budget in today's money, but the final build cost will still depend on quotes, materials, and planning permission at the time.

What if interest rates change over 10 years?
Then the present value changes too: a lower average rate would raise the present value, and a higher average rate would lower it, so the 5% figure is only an assumption, not a forecast.
