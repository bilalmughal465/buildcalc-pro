Personal Effective Annual Rate Calculator (20 Years)

Convert a nominal annual rate to an effective annual rate. This personal version uses a 20-year planning term as its starting point.

—

In real terms, the Effective annual rate is the true yearly return or cost after compounding is taken into account, so it is the fair figure to use when comparing two accounts or loans with the same nominal rate but different compounding frequencies. A higher effective annual rate means more growth on savings, or a higher true cost on borrowing, than the headline nominal rate suggests. Over a 20-year horizon, even a small gap between the nominal rate and the effective annual rate compounds year after year, so the account with the higher effective annual rate pulls further ahead over time.

Formula

Effective rate equals one plus nominal rate divided by periods, to the power of periods, minus one.

How it works

More frequent compounding raises the effective rate slightly above the nominal rate.

Worked example

Nominal rate 6%, compounded monthly (12 periods per year), term 20 years. First, the effective annual rate is (1 + 0.06 / 12) to the power of 12, minus one, which is 6.17%. That is the calculator's own formula: the effective rate is slightly above the 6% nominal rate because interest compounds each month. On $10,000, the effective rate gives $33,102.04 after 20 years, a gain of $23,102.04. At a flat 6% with no monthly compounding, the same $10,000 would give $32,071.35, so monthly compounding adds $1,030.69 over 20 years. The effective rate itself does not change with the term; what changes is how long that 6.17% has to compound, which is why the 20-year total is so much larger than a short-term total.

Who this term suits for a person: A 20-year term suits a person building a mid-career retirement seed or a long home fund who can leave money alone but cannot leave the plan unchecked. Twenty years turns $10,000 into $33,102.04 at the effective rate, yet that figure assumes the same rate holds for two decades, so the plan needs a review whenever rates, fees, or income change.

FAQs

What is an effective rate?
The effective rate is the true yearly rate after allowing for compounding during the year.

Is this personal calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

How does $10,000 reach $33,102.04 in 20 years at 6.17%?
By compounding the effective rate for 20 years: $10,000 times 1.061678 to the power of 20 gives $33,102.04, a gain of $23,102.04. The same $10,000 at a flat 6% gives $32,071.35, so monthly compounding adds $1,030.69 over 20 years.

Does EAR include fees or tax?
No. The effective rate on this page allows only for compounding during the year. It does not include account fees, tax, inflation, or withdrawals. A real 20-year result will be lower after those costs, so compare accounts on their effective rate after fees where the bank states one.

Should I review a 20-year plan if rates fall?
Yes. A 20-year illustration at 6.17% breaks as soon as the rate changes. If the rate falls, the final amount falls too, and the saver may need to save more each month, extend the goal, or accept a smaller pot. Review the plan at least when a fixed deal ends or income changes.
