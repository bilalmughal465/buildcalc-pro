Education Compound Annual Growth Rate Calculator (5 Years)

Calculate compound annual growth over a period. This education version uses a 5-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

CAGR equals ending value divided by starting value, to the power of one over years, minus one.

How it works

CAGR smooths growth into a single yearly rate, even if actual yearly growth varied.

Worked example

Starting value $10,000, ending value $20,000, term 5 years. First, the growth ratio is $20,000 divided by $10,000, which is 2.00. Using the page formula, CAGR is 2.00 to the power of one over 5, minus one, which is 14.87% a year. That is the steady yearly rate that would take $10,000 to $20,000 in 5 years, if growth were perfectly smooth every year.

After only 5 years the yearly rate looks high, because the whole doubling is squeezed into a short window. For education planning this is a short, sharp check: a fund that doubled this fast may have had one exceptional year, not five steady ones. Remember that CAGR smooths the path: the fund may have grown unevenly, with flat or negative years in between, and still show 14.87% a year. It describes the past window, it does not promise the next 5 years.

Who this term suits for an education saver: a family whose child starts university in about 5 years, for example parents checking whether an education fund that doubled in 5 years grew steadily or jumped in one good year, before counting on the same pace for fee bills ahead.

FAQs

What does CAGR mean?
CAGR is the steady yearly growth rate that would take a starting value to an ending value.

Is this education calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is 14.87% a year realistic for an education fund?
Not as a steady expectation. A fund can double in 5 years after one strong year, but planning the next 5 years on 14.87% every year would be optimistic; check what actually drove the growth before assuming it repeats.

What does doubling in 5 years mean for a family saving for university?
It means the fund kept pace with, or beat, fee rises for that short window. It does not guarantee the next 5 years: fees, returns and top-ups all change, so the figure should be re-checked each year.

Should a 5-year education plan rely on a 14.87% yearly rate?
No. Use it as a backward-looking summary of the last 5 years, not as a forecast. A prudent plan tests lower rates too, so a flat year does not break the fee budget.
