Car Effective Annual Rate Calculator (25 Years)

Convert a nominal annual rate to an effective annual rate. This car version uses a 25-year planning term as its starting point.

—

This result shows the true yearly rate after compounding, so it is the rate to compare with other car finance offers rather than the headline nominal rate alone.

Formula

Effective rate equals one plus nominal rate divided by periods, to the power of periods, minus one.

How it works

More frequent compounding raises the effective rate slightly above the nominal rate.

Worked example

Nominal rate 6%, compounding 12 times a year, term 25 years. Using the formula shown on this page, the effective annual rate is one plus 6 divided by 100 and then by 12, which is 1.005, raised to the power of 12, which is about 1.061678, minus one, which is 6.17%. That 6.17% is the same on every page in this group, because the term in years is a planning horizon, not an input in the formula. As a term illustration only, not a separate output of this page, every $10,000 borrowed for a car grows to about $44,649.70 over 25 years at the 6.17% effective rate, compared with about $42,918.71 at a flat 6% with no compounding. The difference, $1,730.99, is the cost of monthly compounding alone over 25 years, and the totals are rounded to the nearest cent.

Who this term suits for a car: a long-term owner thinking about a car kept for a quarter of a century, for example a well-maintained car passed within a family, where the effective rate is a planning check on what a loan tied to that car would truly have cost, not a reason to borrow for that long.

FAQs

What is an effective rate?
The effective rate is the true yearly rate after allowing for compounding during the year.

Is this car calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does the bonus reach $1,730.99 over 25 years?
Because 25 years is 300 months of compounding. The monthly bonus is tiny at first, but after hundreds of months of interest earning interest, the total bonus on $10,000 reaches $1,730.99, which is more than 17% of the original sum.

Is a 25-year effective rate comparison still fair if rates change?
Only as a snapshot. The 6.17% figure assumes 6% nominal and monthly compounding stay fixed for the whole period, which will not happen over 25 years of car ownership, so it should be reviewed whenever a rate, loan, or car changes.

How does the effective rate help a car buyer choose between a loan and a lease?
By putting both deals on the same yearly basis. A lease and a loan may advertise different kinds of rates, but converting each to an effective yearly cost lets the buyer compare what each one truly costs per year for the car.
