Car Loan Total Interest Calculator (30 Years)

Estimate total interest paid over a loan. This car version uses a 30-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Total interest equals monthly EMI times number of payments minus the principal.

How it works

A longer term lowers the monthly payment but raises total interest.

Worked example

Loan amount $200,000, annual rate 6.5%, term 30 years. First, the monthly rate is 6.5% divided by 12, which is 0.0054167, and the number of payments is 30 times 12, which is 360. Using the EMI formula, the monthly payment is $1,264.14. Over 360 payments the total paid is $455,088.98, calculated from the unrounded EMI exactly as the calculator does. Total interest is $455,088.98 minus $200,000, which is $255,088.98. That is 127.5% of the amount borrowed.

Who this term suits for a car buyer: A 30-year term is mortgage-length, not car-length, and it does not suit a car buyer looking for a good deal. The payment of $1,264.14 a month looks manageable, but the total paid is $455,088.98, so the buyer pays for the car more than twice over. Its only honest use is to show the trade clearly: a much lower payment bought with $255,088.98 of interest, while the original car will long since have been replaced.

FAQs

Why does a longer loan cost more interest?
More payments mean interest is charged for longer, so total interest rises even if each payment is smaller.

Is this car calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is a 30-year term normal for a car loan?
No. Thirty years is a mortgage-length term, not a car-loan term, and normal car loans are far shorter. A 30-year car loan means 360 payments of $1,264.14 a month, a total of $455,088.98, for a car that will have been replaced several times. It is included here as a comparison, not as a normal choice for a car buyer.

How can the $255,088.98 interest bill be cut?
The most direct ways are a shorter term, a smaller loan, or a larger deposit, because each of those cuts the time interest is charged or the balance it is charged on. Extra payments can also help, if the lender allows them without a fee, but this calculator assumes a fixed 6.5% rate and no fees, so any real alternative must be compared on its own rate, fees and term, not on the monthly payment alone.

What is a car buyer trading when it chooses 30 years over 5 years?
The buyer trades a lower monthly payment for a much larger total cost. The payment falls by $2,649.09, from $3,913.23 to $1,264.14, but total interest rises by $220,295.20, from $34,793.78 to $255,088.98. That trade is only worth it if the lower payment is genuinely the only way to afford a car, and even then a cheaper car or a larger deposit is usually a better answer than 30 years.
