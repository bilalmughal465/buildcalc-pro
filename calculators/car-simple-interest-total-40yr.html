Car Simple Interest Total Calculator (40 Years)

Calculate simple interest and the total amount to repay. This car version uses a 40-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The Interest result is the extra cost of borrowing on top of the principal, and the Total result is the full amount to repay, so together they show how much more than the original amount the loan costs over 40 years.

Formula

Simple interest equals principal times rate times time, divided by 100.

How it works

Interest is charged only on the original principal, not on interest already added.

Worked example

Principal $10,000, annual rate 5%, term 40 years. Simple interest is $10,000 times 5 times 40, divided by 100, which is $20,000.00. The total to repay is $10,000 plus $20,000.00, which is $30,000.00. After 40 years the interest is twice the amount borrowed, so the car buyer pays back three times the original sum. That is 200% growth on the original amount, because simple interest grows by the same $500 every year, and after 40 years it has added $20,000.00.

Who this term suits for a car buyer: no car buyer as a real borrowing plan, and that is the point of this page: a 40-year term is a lifetime warning, showing a debt outliving the car, and probably several cars after it, by decades.

FAQs

What is simple interest?
Simple interest is interest charged only on the original amount, not on accumulated interest.

Is this car calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is a 40-year car loan a good idea?
No. No ordinary car loan should run for 40 years, because the car will be scrapped long before the debt ends, while the $500 yearly charge keeps running.

How can $10,000 turn into $30,000.00 of total payments?
By adding $500 a year for 40 years. The interest alone is $20,000.00, so the total is the original $10,000 plus $20,000.00 of interest.

What alternatives should a buyer compare before a 40-year term?
Saving for a cheaper car, buying used with cash, or taking a short loan that ends while the car is still on the road. Any of these avoids paying $20,000.00 of interest on a $10,000 car.
