Education Loan EMI Calculator (10 Years)

Estimate the monthly EMI for a loan from amount, rate and term. This education version uses a 10-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

EMI equals principal times monthly rate times one plus monthly rate to the power of payments, divided by that power minus one.

How it works

This assumes a fixed rate and equal monthly payments with no extra fees.

Worked example

Loan amount $200,000, annual rate 6.5%, term 10 years. The monthly rate is 6.5% divided by 12, which is 0.0054167, and there are 120 monthly payments. Using the page formula, the EMI is $2,270.96 a month. Over 120 payments the total paid is $272,515.15, so interest is $72,515.15. Stretching from 5 to 10 years cuts the EMI by about $1,642 a month, but more than doubles the interest, because the balance stays high for twice as long.

Who this term suits for an education borrower: a typical undergraduate or professional graduate repaying over the first decade of a career, where the salary is expected to rise steadily and the loan should be gone well before mid-career costs such as a mortgage and family expenses peak.

FAQs

What is an EMI?
An EMI is a fixed monthly payment that covers both interest and principal over the loan term.

Is this education calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does doubling the term from 5 to 10 years more than double the interest?
Because the balance falls more slowly with a smaller EMI, so interest is charged on a larger amount for more months. The EMI falls by about $1,642 a month, but the extra 60 months of interest add $37,721.37 on top of the 5-year interest.

Is 10 years the usual horizon for an education loan?
It is a common middle choice for graduates: long enough to keep the EMI near a starter salary, short enough that the loan does not follow the borrower into later life stages. Lenders and borrowers often compare 10 years against 5 and 15 before deciding.

What if income drops in year 3 of a 10-year education loan?
The EMI stays $2,270.96 under this fixed-rate example, so a pay cut hurts. The honest check before signing is whether the EMI is still payable on a lower income, not only on the expected starting salary.
