Home Credit Card Monthly Interest Calculator (40 Years)

Estimate monthly interest on a card balance. This home version uses a 40-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The result, labelled Monthly interest, is the interest charged on the balance in a single month at the entered annual rate, so paying only this amount leaves the balance unchanged, while paying more than this amount reduces the balance.

Formula

Monthly interest equals balance times annual rate divided by 100 divided by 12.

How it works

Paying only interest leaves the balance unchanged. Paying more than interest reduces the balance.

Worked example

Balance $5,000, annual rate 18 percent, term 40 years. Using the page formula, monthly interest equals $5,000 times 18 divided by 100 divided by 12, which is $75.00 per month. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults, and it is the same on every page in this group because the term is not an input.

As an illustration only, if only the $75 interest is paid every month and the balance never falls, then over 40 years the interest paid totals $75 times 12 times 40, which is $36,000. At the end of that term the balance would still be $5,000, because paying only interest leaves the balance unchanged. That total is an illustration, not the output of this page: it assumes the balance stays at $5,000 and the rate stays at 18 percent for the whole term, which will not happen if new home spending is added to the card or the rate changes.

Who this term suits for a home household: nobody as an ordinary buying plan. Forty years of card interest is a lifetime warning: it shows a home household paying $36,000 in interest alone and still owing $5,000 at the end, which is why a card balance must never be left to run for decades.

FAQs

Why is card interest high?
Card rates are usually higher than loan rates, so balances grow quickly if only minimum payments are made.

Is this home calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is $75 over 40 years actually a lifetime of card interest?
In effect, yes: $75 times 480 months is $36,000 of interest, with the $5,000 balance still owed at the end. Forty years of interest-only payments is a lifetime cost for no reduction in debt.

What does 40 years mean for home costs and buying power?
Over 40 years, home costs, prices, and incomes all change many times, so a fixed $75 illustration cannot predict the real outcome. What it does show is that leaving a card balance for decades is the most expensive way to pay for a home purchase.

What should a home household check before trusting a 40-year card figure?
That the balance stays at $5,000, the rate stays at 18 percent, and no new spending is added, because the illustration assumes all three. If any of them changes, the real total changes too, and usually not in the household's favour.
