Shop Loan Total Interest Calculator (40 Years)

Estimate total interest paid over a loan. This shop version uses a 40-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Total interest equals monthly EMI times number of payments minus the principal.

How it works

A longer term lowers the monthly payment but raises total interest.

Worked example

Loan amount $200,000, annual rate 6.5 percent, term 40 years. The monthly rate is 6.5 percent divided by 12, which is about 0.5417 percent a month, and the number of monthly payments is 40 times 12, which is 480 payments. Using the page formula, the monthly EMI is $1,170.91, total paid is $1,170.91 times 480, which is $562,038.54, and total interest is $562,038.54 minus $200,000, which is $362,038.54. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults.

Who this term suits for a shop borrower: nobody as an ordinary shop plan, because a 40-year horizon is a lifetime warning, showing interest of nearly twice the amount borrowed, with the loan possibly still running long after the shop has changed hands or closed.

FAQs

Why does a longer loan cost more interest?
More payments mean interest is charged for longer, so total interest rises even if each payment is smaller.

Is this shop calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is $1,170.91 over 40 years actually a lifetime shop loan?
In practice, yes. Forty years is a whole working life, and the shop pays $562,038.54 in total, with $362,038.54 of interest, nearly twice the amount borrowed. Few shops trade unchanged for 40 years, so this figure is a warning, not a plan.

What does 40 years mean for shop costs and buying power?
Rent, rates, wages, and stock prices will all rise many times over 40 years, while this loan's payment stays fixed only if the rate is fixed. A shop comparing a 40-year loan should ask what the payment will really be worth, and cost, in later decades.

What should a shop borrower check before trusting a 40-year loan figure?
Whether the rate is fixed or variable, what security the lender takes, what happens on sale or closure of the shop, and whether extra payments are allowed. A 40-year commitment deserves advice from a qualified adviser before signing.
