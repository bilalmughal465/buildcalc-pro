Personal Savings Goal Monthly Deposit Calculator (5 Years)

Find the monthly deposit needed to reach a savings goal. This personal version uses a 5-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Monthly deposit equals goal times monthly rate divided by one plus monthly rate to the power of months minus one.

How it works

Saving a fixed amount each month with interest reaches the goal faster than saving without interest.

Worked example

Goal $50,000, annual rate 5%, term 5 years. First, the monthly rate is 5% divided by 12, which is 0.0041667, and the number of payments is 5 times 12, which is 60. Using the monthly deposit formula, the monthly deposit is $735.23. Over 60 payments the deposits add up to $44,113.70, calculated from the unrounded deposit exactly as the calculator does, and interest adds $5,886.30 to reach the $50,000 goal. That interest is 11.8% of the goal.

Who this term suits for a person: A 5-year term suits a person saving for a near, fixed personal goal who can afford $735.23 even in a tight month, for example topping up a house deposit, paying for a wedding, or replacing an old car before repair bills grow. The trade-off is clear: the monthly amount is high, but the goal is reached quickly and the plan is easy to keep track of.

FAQs

How much should I save each month?
Divide the goal by the growth of regular deposits at the assumed rate to get the monthly amount.

Is this personal calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why is the 5-year monthly deposit $735.23 so high for a personal goal?
Because only 60 payments have to reach $50,000, so each payment must carry a large share of the goal. Interest adds only $5,886.30 over 5 years, which is 11.8% of the goal, so most of the fund comes from your own deposits. A shorter term always means a higher monthly amount, and a longer term spreads the same goal over more payments.

What personal goal fits a 5-year savings plan?
A near, fixed goal fits best, such as a house deposit top-up, a wedding, a first car, or a planned career break. The goal should be clear and dated, because $735.23 a month is a serious household commitment. If the goal is vague or can wait, a longer term with a lower payment is usually easier to keep.

What happens if I miss a month in a 5-year plan?
Missing one month does not cancel the plan, but it matters more over 5 years than over 30 years, because there are only 60 payments to catch up. This calculator assumes every payment is made on time at a fixed 5% rate, so a missed month would need a slightly higher later payment or a later finish date to still reach $50,000. Keep a small buffer so a tight month does not break the habit.
