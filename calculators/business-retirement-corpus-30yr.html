Business Retirement Corpus Calculator (30 Years)

Estimate a retirement corpus from monthly savings. This business version uses a 30-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The Future value shown is the total retirement pot from your monthly savings plus interest over 30 years, so a larger figure means more money available at retirement at the end of that term.

Formula

Corpus uses the monthly rate and number of monthly savings to grow each saving with interest.

How it works

Starting earlier matters more than saving a little more later, because interest has longer to compound.

Worked example

A business owner sets aside $500 every month for 30 years at 7% a year. The monthly rate is 7% divided by 12, which is 0.0058333, and there are 360 monthly savings. Using the page formula, the corpus is $500 times ((1.0058333 to the power of 360 minus 1) divided by 0.0058333), which is $609,985.50. The owner paid in $180,000, so interest added $429,985.50, which is 70.5% of the corpus. The pot passes $600,000 on only $180,000 of deposits, but only after 30 years of uninterrupted saving at a fixed 7%.

Who this term suits for a business: a long-serving owner planning a full retirement — for example a builder, accountant, or shop owner who started saving in their 30s and wants the pot to fund retirement without having to sell the business at any price.

FAQs

When should I start saving for retirement?
As early as possible. Time in the market allows compounding to do more of the work.

Is this business calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is $609,985.50 a realistic result or just maths?
It is maths on fixed assumptions: $500 every month, 7% every year, no fees, tax, gaps, or market falls for 30 years. Real life will differ, usually lower in bad years and sometimes higher in good ones. Use it as a scale guide, then check the real pot regularly.

What most often breaks a 30-year business savings plan?
Gaps and withdrawals. A few years missed in the middle, or money taken out for business emergencies, removes both the deposit and all its future compounding. Keeping the pot separate from business cash, and rebuilding after any gap, matters more than the rate.

How often should a 30-year business retirement plan be reviewed?
At least every 2 to 3 years, and at every big business change — a new partner, a premises move, a sale, or a health change. A 30-year plan set once and never reviewed is a guess, not a plan.
