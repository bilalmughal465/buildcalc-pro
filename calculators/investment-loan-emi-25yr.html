Investment Loan EMI Calculator (25 Years)

Estimate the monthly EMI for a loan from amount, rate and term. This investment version uses a 25-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The Monthly payment is the fixed amount due every month for 300 months, and the Total paid is that monthly amount multiplied by 300, so the gap between the Total paid and the loan amount is the interest cost of borrowing over 25 years.

Formula

EMI equals principal times monthly rate times one plus monthly rate to the power of payments, divided by that power minus one.

How it works

This assumes a fixed rate and equal monthly payments with no extra fees.

Worked example

Loan amount $200,000, annual rate 6.5%, term 25 years. The monthly rate is 6.5% divided by 12, which is about 0.5417% a month, and the number of payments is 25 times 12, which is 300 months. Using the page formula, the monthly EMI is $1350.41. Total paid over the term is $1350.41 times 300, which is $405124.30, so total interest is $405124.30 minus $200,000, which is $205124.30. That is the calculator's own output for these defaults.

Who this term suits for an investor: an investor planning a retirement income property, where twenty-five years of rent can carry the loan while the property is held for income rather than a quick sale.

FAQs

What is an EMI?
An EMI is a fixed monthly payment that covers both interest and principal over the loan term.

Is this investment calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why is the 25-year interest of $205,124.30 more than the amount borrowed?
Because interest is charged on the remaining balance every month for 300 months, so a low EMI hides a very large total cost; the investor pays back $405,124.30 in all.

How should an investor review a 25-year investment loan?
At least once a year: check rent against the EMI, check the remaining balance, and check whether extra payments would cut total interest enough to be worth it.

What investment risks grow over 25 years?
Rate changes, long empty periods, major repairs, tax rule changes and falling rents; a 25-year plan must survive several of these, not just a good first few years.
