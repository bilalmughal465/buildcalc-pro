Emergency Savings Goal Monthly Deposit Calculator (20 Years)

Find the monthly deposit needed to reach a savings goal. This emergency version uses a 20-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Monthly deposit equals goal times monthly rate divided by one plus monthly rate to the power of months minus one.

How it works

Saving a fixed amount each month with interest reaches the goal faster than saving without interest.

Worked example

A household wants a $50,000 emergency fund in 20 years, at 5% a year. The monthly rate is 5% divided by 12, which is 0.0041667, and there are 240 monthly deposits. Using the page formula, the monthly deposit is $50,000 times 0.0041667, divided by 1.0041667 to the power of 240 minus 1, which is $121.64 a month. Over 240 deposits the household pays in $29,194.69, and interest adds $20,805.31, which is 41.6% of the goal.

At twenty years interest passes $20,000, more than 40% of the goal, and the monthly deposit is $121.64. For an emergency fund this long horizon only makes sense as a lifetime habit: a household that saves a modest amount every month for decades will almost always have something available, but the figure assumes no withdrawals at all for twenty years, and real emergency funds are used, so the honest reading is that $121.64 a month is the habit that keeps a fund alive, not a promise that $50,000 will be sitting untouched in year twenty.

Who this term suits for an emergency saver: a household that has already built a basic fund and now keeps a small monthly habit going for twenty years, so that every repair, bill or gap in work is met from savings rather than from a card.

FAQs

How much should I save each month?
Divide the goal by the growth of regular deposits at the assumed rate to get the monthly amount.

Is this emergency calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does interest pass $20,000 at 20 years?
Because early deposits earn interest for up to twenty years, and interest itself starts earning interest. The $29,194.69 paid in by the saver is less than 60% of the goal, and the rest is the reward for leaving the money alone.

Is 20 years too long to forecast an emergency fund?
Yes, as a single untouched pot it is. Jobs, homes, health and family all change over twenty years. Treat the $50,000 as a moving target that should be reviewed whenever essentials change, not as a fixed finish line.

Should emergency saving come before clearing other debts?
A small fund should come first, even while debts are being paid, because without it every emergency becomes new debt. Once a basic fund exists, extra money is usually better used clearing high-rate debt, then deepening the fund.
