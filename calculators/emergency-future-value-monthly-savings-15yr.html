Emergency Future Value Monthly Savings Calculator (15 Years)

Estimate the future value of regular monthly savings. This emergency version uses a 15-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The future value is the total amount your regular monthly deposits are worth at the end of the 15-year term, including both the money paid in and the interest those deposits have earned.

Formula

Future value uses the monthly rate and the number of monthly deposits to grow each deposit with interest.

How it works

Each monthly deposit earns interest from the month it is made until the end of the term.

Worked example

A household saves $500 every month for 15 years at 6% a year for emergencies. The monthly rate is 6% divided by 12, which is 0.005, and there are 180 monthly deposits. Using the page formula, the future value is $500 times ((1.005 to the power of 180 minus 1) divided by 0.005), which is $145,409.36. The household paid in $90,000.00, so interest added $55,409.36, which is 38.1% of the future value. After 15 years interest is more than half of the fund, so time is doing a large share of the work. For emergency planning this is a mid-career cushion: enough to cover a long illness, a period of caring for a relative, or a home rebuild after storm or flood damage, while still leaving something for the next crisis.

Who this term suits for an emergency saver: a mid-career worker who wants a cushion for a long illness or a period of caring for a relative, for example someone whose income could stop for months while medical and household bills keep coming.

FAQs

How does monthly saving grow?
Regular deposits grow because each deposit earns interest, and that interest then earns more interest.

Is this emergency calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

How does a 15-year term balance the monthly amount and the interest?
At 15 years interest is $55,409.36, or 38.1% of the fund, so time is doing a large share of the work while the monthly $500 stays affordable. Shorter terms need a much larger monthly deposit for the same fund; longer terms lean even harder on the fixed-rate assumption.

What if the rate falls below 6% over 15 years?
The fund falls below $145,409.36, and the shortfall grows with every year the lower rate applies. That is why an emergency fund should be reviewed when rates change, rather than trusted to a single 15-year figure.

Can a 15-year emergency fund also cover a second crisis, like a home repair after a medical bill?
That is exactly what a fund of this size is for: one crisis does not have to empty it. But if the first crisis takes most of it, the fund must be rebuilt before the next one, or the household is back to borrowing.
