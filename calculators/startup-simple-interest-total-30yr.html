Startup Simple Interest Total Calculator (30 Years)

Calculate simple interest and the total amount to repay. This startup version uses a 30-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Simple interest equals principal times rate times time, divided by 100.

How it works

Interest is charged only on the original principal, not on interest already added.

Worked example

Principal $10,000, annual rate 5 percent, term 30 years. Using the page formula, simple interest equals $10,000 times 5 times 30, divided by 100, which is $15,000.00. The total to repay is $10,000 plus $15,000.00, which is $25,000.00. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults.

At $500 a year, 30 years of interest is $15,000, which is one and a half times the amount borrowed, and the total to repay is $25,000.

Who this term suits for a startup founder: a premises purchase on a mortgage-like term only, never for stock or fittings. Stock turns over in months and fittings wear out in years, so a 30-year term only fits a very long-life asset.

FAQs

What is simple interest?
Simple interest is interest charged only on the original amount, not on accumulated interest.

Is this startup calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is $15,000 of interest over 30 years a fair price for a startup loan?
It is $500 a year times 30, with a total of $25,000. It is fair only if the asset, such as premises, still earns after 30 years. For stock or short-life fittings it is not.

What happens if startup revenue falls for several years in a 30-year plan?
The $500 a year of interest does not fall with revenue, so a lean patch still costs the same. A startup on a term this long needs a cash buffer for slow years.

Should a startup borrow for 30 years for stock or fittings?
No. Stock turns over in months and fittings wear out in years, so a 30-year loan would be repaid long after the things it paid for are gone.
