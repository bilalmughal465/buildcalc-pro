Business Debt Payoff Months Calculator (15 Years)

Estimate months to pay off a debt with fixed payments. This business version uses a 15-year planning term as its starting point.

—

In real terms, the Months to pay off figure shows how many months the debt lasts at the entered monthly payment, so a larger payment shortens that time while a smaller payment lengthens it.

Formula

Months are found from the balance, monthly rate and monthly payment using the loan payoff formula.

How it works

If the payment is less than monthly interest, the balance will not fall.

Worked example

Balance $10,000, annual rate 12%, monthly payment $300. The monthly rate is 12 divided by 100 and then by 12, which is 1% a month, so interest in the first month is $100. The $300 payment covers that $100 and cuts $200 off the balance. Using the page formula, the payoff time is 40.7 months, about 3.4 years. That means 40 full payments of $300 and one smaller final payment of $224.95, so the total paid is $12,224.95 and the interest paid is $2,224.95. The last payment is smaller because only a small balance is left by then.

A 15-year horizon is 180 months, so 40.7 months uses 22.6% of it and leaves 139.3 months spare. Over such a long window the danger is not the payoff itself but complacency: a business that forgets the loan is gone may drift into new borrowing it does not need.

Who this term suits for a business: A growing business that borrowed $10,000 for a fit-out or a first expansion. The debt is cleared early in the plan, so later years can be judged on real trading profit instead of on loan repayments.

FAQs

What if my payment is too low?
If the payment does not cover monthly interest, the debt will grow instead of shrinking.

Is this business calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is a 15-year plan too long for a $10,000 business loan?
As a planning window it is fine, but the loan itself does not last 15 years on these figures. The debt clears in 40.7 months, so the remaining years should be planned as debt-free years.

How should a business review the loan across 15 years?
Check the payoff once a year against actual sales. If trade is stronger than expected, an extra payment shortens the 40.7 months further; if trade is weaker, the lender should be told before a payment is missed.

Does the interest stay at $2,224.95 if rates change?
No, that total assumes a fixed 12% for the whole payoff. If the rate rises, monthly interest rises and the payoff takes longer, so a variable-rate loan needs a fresh calculation.
