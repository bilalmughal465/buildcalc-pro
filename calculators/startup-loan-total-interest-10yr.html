Startup Loan Total Interest Calculator (10 Years)

Estimate total interest paid over a loan. This startup version uses a 10-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Total interest equals monthly EMI times number of payments minus the principal.

How it works

A longer term lowers the monthly payment but raises total interest.

Worked example

Loan amount $200,000, annual rate 6.5 percent, term 10 years. The monthly rate is 6.5 percent divided by 12, which is about 0.5417 percent a month, and the number of payments is 10 times 12, which is 120 months. Using the page formula, the monthly EMI is $200,000 times the monthly rate times one plus the monthly rate to the power of 120, divided by one plus the monthly rate to the power of 120 minus one, which gives $2,270.96 a month. That is the calculator output for these defaults.

Over the full term, 120 payments of $2,270.96 give total paid of $272,515.15. Total interest is total paid minus the principal, so $272,515.15 minus $200,000 gives $72,515.15 of interest. This headline is the same calculation on every page in this group, because the loan amount and rate are identical; only the term changes, and a longer term lowers the monthly payment while raising total interest.

A startup planning past its first few years: for example a business moving from a home setup to rented premises, hiring its first small team, or buying delivery vehicles, where a 10-year term keeps monthly payments manageable while the business grows.

FAQs

Why does a longer loan cost more interest?
More payments mean interest is charged for longer, so total interest rises even if each payment is smaller.

Is this startup calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is $2,270.96 a month realistic for a growing startup?
It can be, once the business has regular sales: 120 payments of $2,270.96 give $272,515.15 in total, so the startup pays $72,515.15 of interest for the lower monthly figure.

Why does 10-year interest more than double the 5-year figure?
Because interest is charged for twice as long: the 5-year loan costs $34,793.78 of interest, while the 10-year loan costs $72,515.15, even though each monthly payment is smaller.

Should a startup choose 10 years instead of 5?
Only if the lower payment matters more than the extra interest: 10 years costs $37,721.37 more interest than 5 years ($72,515.15 minus $34,793.78), so the startup should check that the freed cash is really earning more than that.
