Farm Simple Interest Total Calculator (20 Years)

Calculate simple interest and the total amount to repay. This farm version uses a 20-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The Interest figure is the extra cost of borrowing on top of the principal, and the Total figure is the full amount to repay over 20 years, so the gap between the two figures shows how much borrowing adds to the original amount.

Formula

Simple interest equals principal times rate times time, divided by 100.

How it works

Interest is charged only on the original principal, not on interest already added.

Worked example

Principal $10,000, annual rate 5%, term 20 years. Using the page formula, simple interest is $10,000 times 5 times 20, divided by 100, which is $10,000. The total to repay is $10,000 plus $10,000, which is $20,000. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults.

At 20 years the interest equals the amount borrowed, so the total to repay is exactly double the principal. That is the point where the cost of borrowing catches up with the sum itself, even though the yearly charge never rose above $500.

Who this term suits on a farm: a farm funding a long-lived asset, such as a new barn, an irrigation system, or land improvement, where the borrower should notice that interest will equal the amount borrowed before the loan ends and should check that the asset will still be earning at that point.

FAQs

What is simple interest?
Simple interest is interest charged only on the original amount, not on accumulated interest.

Is this farm calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does interest equal the $10,000 borrowed after 20 years?
Because $500 a year for 20 years is $10,000. At that point the total to repay is $20,000, exactly double the principal, even though the rate never rose above 5% and the yearly charge never rose above $500.

Is 20 years too long for a simple-interest farm loan?
It can be too long for equipment, which rarely lasts 20 years, but it can fit a long-lived asset such as a barn or land improvement. The test is whether the asset will still be earning when interest catches up with the amount borrowed.

What farm purchase fits a 20-year simple-interest plan?
A purchase with a working life of about 20 years, such as a well-built barn, an irrigation system, or drainage work. A tractor, pump, or shed roof bought on a 20-year loan would likely need replacing while the loan was still being repaid.
