Medical Retirement Corpus Calculator (20 Years)

Estimate a retirement corpus from monthly savings. This medical version uses a 20-year planning term as its starting point.

—

In plain terms, the Future value result is the retirement fund your monthly savings are expected to build by retirement over 20 years, including growth at the annual rate you entered; it is larger than the total of your monthly deposits alone because interest earned along the way also grows over this term.

Formula

Corpus uses the monthly rate and number of monthly savings to grow each saving with interest.

How it works

Starting earlier matters more than saving a little more later, because interest has longer to compound.

Worked example

Monthly saving $500, annual rate 7%, term 20 years. The monthly rate is 7% divided by 12, which is about 0.5833% a month, and the number of monthly deposits is 20 times 12, which is 240 deposits. Using the page formula, the future value is $500 times one plus the monthly rate to the power of 240, minus one, all divided by the monthly rate, which is $260,463.33. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults. Total deposits are $500 times 240, which is $120,000, so interest adds $260,463.33 minus $120,000, which is $140,463.33. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults, before fees, taxes and inflation.

Who this term suits for a medical saver: a doctor in mid-career planning an exit from full-time practice, saving steadily for twenty years so that retirement income does not depend only on selling the practice.

FAQs

When should I start saving for retirement?
As early as possible. Time in the market allows compounding to do more of the work.

Is this medical calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

How can $500 a month become $260,463.33 in 20 years?
Through compounding at 7%. Twenty years is 240 deposits totalling $120,000, and interest adds $140,463.33 on top, more than the deposits themselves. That crossover, where interest passes deposits, is why twenty years changes the picture so much.

Is a 20-year fund suitable for a clinic premises plan?
It can be part of one. A doctor planning to sell or hand over a practice in twenty years can use this fund alongside the sale value, but the fund alone should not be counted as the whole retirement income.

Does a 20-year medical plan need reviewing?
Yes, at least every few years. Rates, tax rules, practice income and retirement age all change over twenty years, and a plan set once and never reviewed will drift away from the $260,463.33 illustration.
