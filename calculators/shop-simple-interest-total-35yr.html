Shop Simple Interest Total Calculator (35 Years)

Calculate simple interest and the total amount to repay. This shop version uses a 35-year planning term as its starting point.

—

This result means the Interest is the extra amount charged on the original principal over 35 years, and the Total is the principal plus that Interest, which is the full amount to repay at the end of the term.

Formula

Simple interest equals principal times rate times time, divided by 100.

How it works

Interest is charged only on the original principal, not on interest already added.

Worked example

Principal $10,000, annual rate 5 percent, term 35 years. Using the page formula, simple interest is $10,000 times 5 divided by 100 times 35, which is $17,500.00. The total to repay is $10,000 plus $17,500.00, which is $27,500.00. That is the calculator output for these defaults. Because time is an input on this page, each year page gives a genuinely different answer: interest grows by exactly $500 for every extra year, since 5 percent of $10,000 is $500 a year and simple interest never compounds. Interest passed the $10,000 borrowed at 20 years, and by 35 years it has grown to $17,500.00, so the total of $27,500.00 is well above double the original loan.

Who this term suits for a shop owner: nobody as an ordinary shop loan, because a 35-year horizon outlives most shop fittings, leases, and even owners, so its use is as a warning that a small yearly interest figure still grows into $17,500 over 35 years.

FAQs

What is simple interest?
Simple interest is interest charged only on the original amount, not on accumulated interest.

Is this shop calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does the total matter more than the $500 yearly figure at 35 years?
Because $500 a year over 35 years is $17,500 of interest, with a total of $27,500. The yearly figure looks small, but the total is nearly three times the amount borrowed, and that total is what the shop actually pays.

Who repays a 35-year shop loan if the owner retires or sells?
Whoever takes over the loan, which is usually the buyer or the owner's family, unless the lender agrees otherwise. A loan that outlives its original owner should be discussed with a solicitor before it is signed.

Is 35 years too long to judge from one fixed rate?
Yes. A single 5 percent rate held for 35 years is a planning simplification, not a forecast. Real rates, rents, and shop costs will move many times over 35 years, so the figure should be rechecked regularly.
