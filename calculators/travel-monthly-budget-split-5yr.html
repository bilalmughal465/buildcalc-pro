Travel Monthly Budget Split Calculator (5 Years)

Split monthly income into needs, wants and savings. This travel version uses a 5-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Needs are 50 percent, wants are 30 percent and savings are 20 percent of income.

How it works

This 50-30-20 split is a planning guide, not a rule. Adjust it to your real costs.

Worked example

Monthly income $4,000, term 5 years. Using the page formula, needs are 50 percent of $4,000, which is $2,000 a month; wants are 30 percent, which is $1,200 a month; and savings are 20 percent, which is $800 a month. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults, and it is the same on every page in this group because the term is not an input: only the monthly income decides the split.

Over 5 years, which is 60 months, saving $800 a month adds up to $48,000 in total. That total is an illustration only, not a second output of this page: it assumes the same $4,000 income every month, with no interest earned, no fees, no taxes and no change in travel costs. Over the same period, needs would total $120,000 and wants would total $72,000 on the same income.

Who this term suits for a traveller: a traveller saving for a near-term, known trip, such as a round-the-world trip, a sabbatical year, a honeymoon, or a campervan deposit due in about 5 years, where the monthly split must leave enough room for the trip itself.

FAQs

What is the 50-30-20 rule?
It is a simple budget guide: 50 percent needs, 30 percent wants and 20 percent savings.

Is this travel calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is $800 a month of savings realistic for a new traveller?
It depends on the $4,000 income holding up. Needs take $2,000 and wants $1,200, so $800 is left only if real travel and living costs fit inside those two shares. If a slow month drops income below $4,000, the $800 is the first figure to shrink.

What travel costs count as needs in the $2,000 figure?
Needs are the costs a traveller cannot skip: rent or a base at home, food, transport to work, insurance and phone. Flights, hotels and eating out on a trip are wants, not needs, unless the trip itself is the reason for the budget.

What if travel income falls below $4,000 in a slow month?
The split scales with income. On $3,000 a month the same percentages give $1,500 for needs, $900 for wants and $600 for savings, so a slow month cuts the travel fund by $200 before anything else is touched.
