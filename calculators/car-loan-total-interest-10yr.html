Car Loan Total Interest Calculator (10 Years)

Estimate total interest paid over a loan. This car version uses a 10-year planning term as its starting point.

—

This means the Total interest is the extra cost of borrowing beyond the loan amount, and the Total paid is the loan amount plus that interest over the full 10-year term.

Formula

Total interest equals monthly EMI times number of payments minus the principal.

How it works

A longer term lowers the monthly payment but raises total interest.

Worked example

Loan amount $200,000, annual rate 6.5%, term 10 years. First, the monthly rate is 6.5% divided by 12, which is 0.0054167, and the number of payments is 10 times 12, which is 120. Using the EMI formula, the monthly payment is $2,270.96. Over 120 payments the total paid is $272,515.15, calculated from the unrounded EMI exactly as the calculator does. Total interest is $272,515.15 minus $200,000, which is $72,515.15. That is 36.3% of the amount borrowed.

Who this term suits for a car buyer: A 10-year term suits a car buyer who needs a lower payment than a 5-year loan but still plans to keep the same car for most of the term, for example a buyer choosing a larger family car or a work vehicle they expect to run for a decade. The payment of $2,270.96 a month is easier to carry, but the buyer must budget for rising repairs as the car ages, because the payment continues long after the warranty ends and the car's value has fallen.

FAQs

Why does a longer loan cost more interest?
More payments mean interest is charged for longer, so total interest rises even if each payment is smaller.

Is this car calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why is a 10-year car loan already long for a car?
Because a car ages, loses value and needs more repairs, while the payment of $2,270.96 a month continues for 120 months. By the later years the car may be out of warranty and worth far less than the amount still owed at that point, so the buyer is paying a new-car-sized payment for an old car. A 10-year term should only be chosen if the buyer genuinely plans to keep and maintain the same car for a decade.

How much more interest does 10 years cost than 5 years?
On $200,000 at 6.5%, a 10-year loan costs $72,515.15 in interest and a 5-year loan costs $34,793.78, so the 10-year term costs $37,721.37 more. The monthly payment falls by $1,642.27, from $3,913.23 to $2,270.96, but that lower payment is bought with more than double the total interest.

How should a car buyer test whether $2,270.96 a month is affordable?
Test the payment against the lowest recent monthly income, not the best month, and after insurance, fuel, tax and a repair allowance are included. Keep a cash buffer for a slow month and for an unexpected repair, because a car loan payment does not pause when the car needs work. If $2,270.96 a month strains that test, a cheaper car or a larger deposit is a better answer than a longer term.
