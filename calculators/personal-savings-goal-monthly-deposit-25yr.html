Personal Savings Goal Monthly Deposit Calculator (25 Years)

Find the monthly deposit needed to reach a savings goal. This personal version uses a 25-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Monthly deposit equals goal times monthly rate divided by one plus monthly rate to the power of months minus one.

How it works

Saving a fixed amount each month with interest reaches the goal faster than saving without interest.

Worked example

Goal $50,000, annual rate 5%, term 25 years. First, the monthly rate is 5% divided by 12, which is 0.0041667, and the number of payments is 25 times 12, which is 300. Using the monthly deposit formula, the monthly deposit is $83.96. Over 300 payments the deposits add up to $25,188.51, calculated from the unrounded deposit exactly as the calculator does, and interest adds $24,811.49 to reach the $50,000 goal. That interest is 49.6% of the goal.

Who this term suits for a person: A 25-year term suits a person in their thirties starting a retirement pot who can keep $83.96 a month going through job changes, a mortgage and family years. The monthly amount is low, but the plan only works if the saver keeps paying through slow years and reviews the goal as income and prices rise.

FAQs

How much should I save each month?
Divide the goal by the growth of regular deposits at the assumed rate to get the monthly amount.

Is this personal calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does interest almost equal my deposits at 25 years?
Because 300 payments over 25 years give compounding a long time to work. Total deposits are $25,188.51 and interest adds $24,811.49, which is 49.6% of the goal, at $83.96 a month. Interest is almost half the fund, so the plan depends heavily on the rate holding near 5% for the whole period.

How do I keep saving for 25 years through job changes?
By keeping the monthly amount low enough to survive a job change, which $83.96 a month usually is, and by restarting quickly after any break. This calculator assumes all 300 payments are made on time. A long break would need higher later payments or a later finish date to still reach $50,000, so treat the habit as the main goal, not just the amount.

Should the $50,000 goal rise with inflation over 25 years?
In real life, yes, it probably should, because $50,000 in 25 years will buy much less than $50,000 today. This calculator keeps the goal fixed at $50,000 and does not adjust for inflation. If the goal is retirement or a house purchase, review the target every few years and raise it as prices and income rise.
