Personal Down Payment Amount Calculator (35 Years)

Calculate a down payment from price and percentage. This personal version uses a 35-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Down payment equals price times down payment percentage divided by 100.

How it works

A larger down payment usually means a smaller loan and lower monthly payments.

Worked example

Price $300,000, down payment 20 percent, term 35 years. First, the down payment is $300,000 times 20 divided by 100, which is $60,000. The loan needed is $300,000 minus $60,000, which is $240,000. That $60,000 does not depend on the term, because the page formula uses only price and percentage. Saving illustration only, not the output of this calculator: to save $60,000 in 35 years, with no interest counted, takes about $142.86 a month for 420 months. The step from 30 years to 35 years saves only about $23.81 a month, for 60 more months of waiting, so a 35-year plan should be judged on whether the saver can keep the habit, not on the small monthly saving it buys.

Who this term suits for a person: A 35-year term does not suit a person looking for a quick route to buying. Its only honest use is as a long habit with regular reviews, for example a saver who starts at about $142.86 a month young and raises the amount over time. Judged as a fixed $60,000 target, it fails, because the gain over 30 years is only about $23.81 a month, while the wait grows by 5 years.

FAQs

How much down payment do I need?
It depends on the lender and the purchase. A common planning figure is 20 percent, but requirements vary.

Is this personal calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does the monthly saving fall only about $23.81 from 30 years to 35 years?
Because $60,000 divided by 360 months is about $166.67, and $60,000 divided by 420 months is about $142.86, a difference of about $23.81. The longer the term already is, the less each extra 5 years reduces the monthly amount, while the wait still grows by a full 5 years. At this end of the scale, stretching the plan is a poor trade: a small monthly saving bought with 60 more months of waiting and price rises.

Is a 35-year down-payment plan realistic?
Only as a habit that is reviewed and raised over time, not as a fixed $60,000 target. A saver who starts at about $142.86 a month young and raises the amount as pay grows can build a real deposit, but a saver who fixes $142.86 for 35 years will find the home they wanted has moved far beyond $300,000. The realistic version of a 35-year plan is a rising savings habit, not a flat monthly amount.

When should a 35-year saver stop saving and buy instead?
When the fund covers 20 percent of a home they actually want, and the monthly loan payment fits their income after other debts. Waiting for a perfect fund while prices rise can leave the saver worse off than buying with the deposit they have, if the loan payment is affordable. This calculator shows the down payment amount only, so the buying decision also needs a loan-payment check and a lender check, not just a fund total.
