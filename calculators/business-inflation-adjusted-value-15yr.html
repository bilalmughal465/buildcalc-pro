Business Inflation Adjusted Value Calculator (15 Years)

Estimate what an amount will be worth after inflation. This business version uses a 15-year planning term as its starting point.

—

In real terms, the Future amount is the sum you would need after 15 years at the entered inflation rate to buy the same things that the original amount buys today.

Formula

Future amount equals amount times one plus inflation rate to the power of years.

How it works

Inflation means the same amount buys less over time, so future prices are higher.

Worked example

Amount $10,000, inflation rate 3%, term 15 years. The growth factor is 1.03 to the power of 15, which is 1.557967. The future amount is $10,000 times 1.557967, which is $15,579.67. That is $5,579.67 more than today, a rise of 55.8%. A basket of business costs that is $10,000 today would need $15,579.67 after 15 years at a steady 3% inflation to buy the same things. After 15 years, more than half as much money again is needed for the same basket.

Who this term suits for a business: a manufacturer or workshop planning equipment replacement, where machines bought today will need replacing in about 15 years at prices more than half as high again, so the replacement reserve must grow, not stay flat.

FAQs

What does inflation do to money?
Inflation reduces what money can buy, so a sum today will need to be larger in future to buy the same things.

Is this business calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does the rise pass $5,000 at 15 years even at only 3%?
Because 3% compounds for 15 years. Small yearly rises stack up: after 10 years the amount is $13,439.16, and five more years at 3% add another $2,140.51. The longer the term, the more the compounding effect dominates the yearly rate.

How should a business plan equipment replacement over 15 years with inflation?
Do not save the today's price of the machine. If a machine costs $10,000 today, budget around $15,579.67 in 15 years at 3%, and review the reserve every few years. If the reserve earns interest, that interest offsets part of the rise, but the target itself keeps moving up.

Can a business just raise prices by 55.8% over 15 years?
Only gradually and only if customers accept it. A 55.8% rise spread over 15 years is about 3% a year, which customers usually absorb in small steps. A single large rise after years of frozen prices is far harder to sell, so plan small annual increases instead.
