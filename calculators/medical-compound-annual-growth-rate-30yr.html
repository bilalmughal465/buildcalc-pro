Medical Compound Annual Growth Rate Calculator (30 Years)

Calculate compound annual growth over a period. This medical version uses a 30-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

CAGR equals ending value divided by starting value, to the power of one over years, minus one.

How it works

CAGR smooths growth into a single yearly rate, even if actual yearly growth varied.

Worked example

Starting value $10,000, ending value $20,000, term 30 years. Using the page formula, CAGR equals $20,000 divided by $10,000, to the power of one over 30, minus one, which is 2.34% a year. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults, and it is the steady yearly rate that would grow $10,000 to $20,000 over 30 years if growth were perfectly even every year.

For comparison, the simple average is 100 divided by 30, which is 3.33% a year. The simple average ignores compounding, so it overstates the steady rate. The CAGR of 2.34% a year is the honest yearly figure, because it is the rate that, compounded every year, actually doubles the value.

Who this term suits for a medical borrower: a clinic owner planning succession, checking whether the practice value has grown enough over 30 years to fund retirement or a handover.

FAQs

What does CAGR mean?
CAGR is the steady yearly growth rate that would take a starting value to an ending value.

Is this medical calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

What does 2.34% a year over 30 years tell a clinic owner?
It tells you the smoothed pace of a doubling spread over 30 years. It is a very small yearly rate, and it may be below medical cost inflation, so the real gain could be much smaller.

Why compare CAGR with the simple average of 3.33% a year?
The simple average overstates the steady rate because it ignores compounding. The CAGR of 2.34% is the rate that actually doubles $10,000 to $20,000 over 30 years.

Should a 30-year medical growth rate guide a new clinic purchase?
No. A 30-year smoothed rate describes the past, not the next 30 years. Judge a purchase on current patient demand, income, and costs, not on this figure alone.
