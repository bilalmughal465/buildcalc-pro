Car Credit Card Monthly Interest Calculator (35 Years)

Estimate monthly interest on a card balance. This car version uses a 35-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Monthly interest equals balance times annual rate divided by 100 divided by 12.

How it works

Paying only interest leaves the balance unchanged. Paying more than interest reduces the balance.

Worked example

Balance $5,000, annual rate 18%, term 35 years. Monthly interest is $5,000 times 18, divided by 100, divided by 12, which is $75.00 a month. That is the calculator's own output, and it is the same every month because the balance and rate do not change. If the car owner pays only interest, after 35 years they have paid $31,500 in interest, which is 630% of the balance, and they still owe the original $5,000. Paying more than $75.00 in any month is what finally reduces that balance.

Who this term suits for a car owner: a lifetime car-cost check for an owner nearing the end of a working life, showing what 35 years of interest-only card payments cost, and why a car bought on a card in youth should never still be owed for in later life.

FAQs

Why is card interest high?
Card rates are usually higher than loan rates, so balances grow quickly if only minimum payments are made.

Is this car calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

How can $31,500 of interest be paid on a $5,000 car balance?
By paying $75.00 a month for 420 months and never paying extra. The interest is $75.00 times 420, which is $31,500, or 630% of the balance, and because every payment only covers interest, the $5,000 balance never moves. The total is large precisely because the balance never falls.

What does 35 years of card interest say about a car's running costs?
It says the running costs were never matched to income. A car whose repairs, tyres and servicing stay on a card for 35 years is costing far more than its purchase price, and the owner would have been better served by a cheaper car or a planned savings pot for repairs.

At 35 years, is consolidation better than continuing interest-only payments?
Usually, yes, if the new loan has a lower rate and a fixed end date, and the card is not used again for car spending. Consolidation only helps if the $75.00 a month becomes a payment that actually reduces the balance, rather than another interest-only arrangement.
