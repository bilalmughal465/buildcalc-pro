Business Present Value Calculator (35 Years)

Find how much a future amount is worth today. This business version uses a 35-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The Present value figure is what the future amount is worth in today's money at the rate and term entered, so it is the amount to compare with cash available today rather than with the future amount itself.

Formula

Present value equals future amount divided by one plus rate to the power of years.

How it works

A future amount is worth less today because money today can earn interest.

Worked example

Future amount $50,000, annual rate 5%, term 35 years. First, the discount factor is 1.05 to the power of 35, which is 5.516015. Present value is $50,000 divided by 5.516015, which is $9,064.51. The discount is $50,000 minus $9,064.51, which is $40,935.49, or 81.9% of the future amount. After 35 years only 18.1% remains in today's money, so the extra 5 years from 30 to 35 alone remove a further $2,504.36 of present value.

Who this term suits for a business: A 35-year term suits a family business planning a very long handover, where a $50,000 sum is expected in 35 years. At $9,064.51 today, the sum is a minor present asset, so the handover plan must rest on the business itself and on nearer cash flows, not on that distant receipt.

FAQs

What is present value?
Present value is what a future sum is worth today after allowing for interest over time.

Is this business calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does the value fall only $2,504.36 from 30 years to 35 years?
Because present value falls in dollars more slowly as it gets smaller, even though the percentage discount keeps rising. From 30 years ($11,568.87) to 35 years ($9,064.51) the drop is $2,504.36, while the discount rises from 76.9% to 81.9%. The later years remove a smaller dollar amount because there is less value left to remove.

Is a 35-year present value useful for a family business?
Yes, mainly as a reality check for a very long handover: a $50,000 sum in 35 years is only $9,064.51 today at 5%, so the handover cannot rest on that sum alone. The business value, nearer income, and the next generation's own plans matter far more than this distant receipt.

Should inflation be added on top of the 5% discount?
No, not separately in this calculator, because the 5% rate is the only discount applied here and no inflation, tax, or fees are included. If the business wants a real-terms figure, it must choose a rate that already reflects its view of inflation and risk, and state that choice with the result.
