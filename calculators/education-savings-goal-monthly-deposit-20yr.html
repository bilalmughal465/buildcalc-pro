Education Savings Goal Monthly Deposit Calculator (20 Years)

Find the monthly deposit needed to reach a savings goal. This education version uses a 20-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The result is the monthly deposit to save every month for 20 years to reach the goal amount at the entered annual rate, with interest helping the deposits grow toward that goal.

Formula

Monthly deposit equals goal times monthly rate divided by one plus monthly rate to the power of months minus one.

How it works

Saving a fixed amount each month with interest reaches the goal faster than saving without interest.

Worked example

A family wants $50,000 for university fees in 20 years, at 5% a year. The monthly rate is 5% divided by 12, which is 0.0041667, and there are 240 monthly deposits. Using the page formula, the monthly deposit is $50,000 times 0.0041667, divided by 1.0041667 to the power of 240 minus 1, which is $121.64 a month. Over 240 deposits the family pays in $29,194.69, and interest adds $20,805.31, which is 41.6% of the goal. Interest now passes $20,000, so the family pays in well under three-fifths of the goal. For an education saver this is a birth-to-university plan: a small monthly amount, but one that assumes 20 years of uninterrupted saving at a fixed 5%, with no fees, taxes, or gaps counted.

Who this term suits for an education saver: a family planning from a child's birth or earliest years, for example parents or grandparents who open an education fund when a baby is born and want a small monthly amount that can run for the whole childhood.

FAQs

How much should I save each month?
Divide the goal by the growth of regular deposits at the assumed rate to get the monthly amount.

Is this education calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does interest pass $20,000 at 20 years?
Because compounding has 20 years to work. Early deposits earn interest for nearly 20 years, and that interest itself earns interest, so the interest share rises from 11.8% at 5 years to 41.6% at 20 years.

Is 20 years too long to forecast university fees?
Yes, as a single fixed figure. Courses, countries, and fee systems change over 20 years, so $50,000 is a planning target, not a price promise. The family should revisit the goal at each school stage.

Should education saving come before clearing other debts?
Usually not expensive debt first. Clearing high-interest debt normally saves more than a 5% education return earns, so a family should compare its own debts before locking in $121.64 a month for 20 years.
