Wedding Loan Total Interest Calculator (30 Years)

Estimate total interest paid over a loan. This wedding version uses a 30-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The Total interest is the extra cost of borrowing on top of the original loan amount over 30 years, and the Total paid is the loan amount plus that interest, meaning the full amount handed over by the final payment.

Formula

Total interest equals monthly EMI times number of payments minus the principal.

How it works

A longer term lowers the monthly payment but raises total interest.

Worked example

Loan $200,000, annual rate 6.5%, term 30 years. The monthly rate is 6.5% divided by 12, which is about 0.5417% a month, and the number of payments is 30 times 12, which is 360. Using the EMI formula, the monthly payment is $1,264.14. Over 360 payments the total paid is $455,088.98, so the total interest is $455,088.98 minus $200,000, which is $255,088.98. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults.

Who this term suits for a wedding: A couple treating wedding debt as a thirty-year household obligation, similar to a home loan, where $1,264.14 a month is chosen for affordability even though the interest, $255,088.98, ends up larger than the loan itself.

FAQs

Why does a longer loan cost more interest?
More payments mean interest is charged for longer, so total interest rises even if each payment is smaller.

Is this wedding calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is a 30-year wedding loan the same as a mortgage?
Only in length: a mortgage buys a home that usually keeps value, while a wedding loan pays for a past event. Paying $255,088.98 of interest over 30 years for a celebration is very different from paying interest on a house, so the two should not be judged the same way.

What happens if wedding income falls during a 30-year plan?
The $1,264.14 payment still falls due every month, and missed payments add fees and damage credit. A thirty-year plan needs an emergency buffer, because income over three decades will not stay flat.

What if the wedding loan were only $100,000 instead of $200,000?
Halving the loan halves every figure at the same rate and term: the payment would be about $632.07 a month, the total paid about $227,544.49, and the total interest about $127,544.49.
