Personal Retirement Corpus Calculator (20 Years)

Estimate a retirement corpus from monthly savings. This personal version uses a 20-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Corpus uses the monthly rate and number of monthly savings to grow each saving with interest.

How it works

Starting earlier matters more than saving a little more later, because interest has longer to compound.

Worked example

Monthly saving $500, annual rate 7%, term 20 years. First, the monthly rate is 7% divided by 12, which is 0.0058333, and the number of payments is 20 times 12, which is 240. Using the corpus formula, the future value is $500 times (4.038739 minus 1) divided by 0.0058333, which is $260,463.33. Over 240 payments the person deposits $120,000.00 in total, and interest adds $140,463.33 to reach the corpus. That interest is 53.9% of the corpus, so interest has now passed the person's own deposits.

Who this term suits for a person: a person in their early 40s who has two decades before retirement and wants a pot that can genuinely supplement a pension. For example, a worker who starts at 40 and saves $500 a month until 60 could build $260,463.33 on paper, which is large enough to matter for yearly retirement income, but only if the saving survives job changes, house moves, and family costs. The trade-off is clear: 20 years rewards patience, but it also means 20 years of discipline, not a one-time decision.

FAQs

When should I start saving for retirement?
As early as possible. Time in the market allows compounding to do more of the work.

Is this personal calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does interest pass my deposits at 20 years?
Because the balance keeps growing and interest is earned on that growing balance every month. Deposits are $120,000.00, yet interest reaches $140,463.33, since later years earn interest not only on deposits but also on all the interest added in earlier years. That crossover is the point where compounding starts doing more work than the saver.

Is 20 years too long to trust a fixed 7% rate?
Yes, as a fixed forecast it is too long to trust blindly. Returns, fees, taxes, and inflation will change over 20 years, so $260,463.33 is a planning illustration, not a promise. Review the plan every year, test it at lower rates, and adjust the monthly saving when pay or costs change, rather than setting it once and forgetting it.

Should retirement saving come before clearing other debts?
High-interest debt usually comes first, because its interest rate is often higher than any retirement return, and it grows while the retirement pot is still small. Keep an emergency buffer, clear costly debts, then protect the $500 retirement saving as a regular habit. For a large or complex case, check with a qualified adviser.
