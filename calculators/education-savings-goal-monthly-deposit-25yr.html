Education Savings Goal Monthly Deposit Calculator (25 Years)

Find the monthly deposit needed to reach a savings goal. This education version uses a 25-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Monthly deposit equals goal times monthly rate divided by one plus monthly rate to the power of months minus one.

How it works

Saving a fixed amount each month with interest reaches the goal faster than saving without interest.

Worked example

A family wants $50,000 for university fees in 25 years, at 5% a year. The monthly rate is 5% divided by 12, which is 0.0041667, and there are 300 monthly deposits. Using the page formula, the monthly deposit is $50,000 times 0.0041667, divided by 1.0041667 to the power of 300 minus 1, which is $83.96 a month. Over 300 deposits the family pays in $25,188.51, and interest adds $24,811.49, which is 49.6% of the goal. Interest now almost equals the family's own deposits, and the monthly amount falls below $100. For an education saver this looks easy, but it rests on 25 years at a fixed 5% with no breaks, and on a $50,000 fee figure that will not stand still for 25 years.

Who this term suits for an education saver: a family that wants the lowest possible monthly burden, for example parents on a tight budget who start very early and prefer under $100 a month, accepting that the goal must be reviewed often as fees change.

FAQs

How much should I save each month?
Divide the goal by the growth of regular deposits at the assumed rate to get the monthly amount.

Is this education calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does the monthly deposit fall below $100 at 25 years?
Because 300 deposits share the goal, and interest contributes $24,811.49, almost half the total. The longer the term, the more work interest does, so the family's own monthly share shrinks.

Does a 25-year plan survive a child changing course or country?
The savings survive, but the goal may not fit. A different course, country, or fee system changes the amount needed, so the family should treat $50,000 as a starting target and adjust it as plans firm up.

Should deposits rise with fee inflation over 25 years?
Yes. Holding the deposit at $83.96 for 25 years while fees rise means the fund falls behind. Raising the deposit with income or with fee expectations is the honest way to keep a 25-year plan on track.
