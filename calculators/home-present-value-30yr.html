Home Present Value Calculator (30 Years)

Find how much a future amount is worth today. This home version uses a 30-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The Present value result shows what a future home sum is worth in today's money after 30 years, so a smaller present value means the future sum has been discounted over time and should be planned from the lower today's-money figure, not the full future price.

Formula

Present value equals future amount divided by one plus rate to the power of years.

How it works

A future amount is worth less today because money today can earn interest.

Worked example

Future amount $50,000, annual rate 5%, term 30 years. First, one plus the rate is 1.05, and 1.05 to the power of 30 is about 4.32194. Using the page formula, present value is $50,000 divided by 4.32194, which is $11,568.87. That is the calculator's own output for the page defaults. The discount is $50,000 minus $11,568.87, which is $38,431.13, so waiting 30 years removes $38,431.13 of value in today's money at 5% a year. For a home buyer this is a full-mortgage check: over 30 years, the standard long mortgage length, a future home sum falls to under a quarter of its future price in today's money.

Who this term suits for a home buyer: a buyer taking a long mortgage, or a homeowner planning retirement in about 30 years, checking what a future home sum is worth today before locking in a plan that will run for most of a working life.

FAQs

What is present value?
Present value is what a future sum is worth today after allowing for interest over time.

Is this home calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

What does $11,568.87 mean over 30 years?
It means a $50,000 home sum due in 30 years is worth $11,568.87 in today's money at 5% a year, because 1.05 to the power of 30 is about 4.32194.

How does the 30-year figure relate to buying a home?
It shows how small a far-future home sum looks in today's money, which is a warning against budgeting a distant purchase at its future price without discounting it first.

Why does present value keep falling even though the future amount is fixed?
Because each extra year of discounting divides the sum by another 1.05, so the present value falls every year even though the $50,000 future amount never changes.
