Car Future Value Monthly Savings Calculator (15 Years)

Estimate the future value of regular monthly savings. This car version uses a 15-year planning term as its starting point.

—

This result is the total amount your regular monthly deposits would grow to by the end of the term, including the interest earned along the way, based on the deposit, rate and years you entered.

Formula

Future value uses the monthly rate and the number of monthly deposits to grow each deposit with interest.

How it works

Each monthly deposit earns interest from the month it is made until the end of the term.

Worked example

A car buyer sets aside $500 every month for 15 years at 6% a year. The monthly rate is 6% divided by 12, which is 0.5%, and there are 180 monthly deposits. Using the page formula, the future value is $500 times ((1.005 to the power of 180 minus 1) divided by 0.005), which is $145,409.36. The buyer paid in $90,000.00, so interest added $55,409.36, which is 38.1% of the final value.

Most of the result after only a few years is still the deposits themselves, because compounding has had little time to work, but as the years pass interest becomes a larger and larger share of the pot. That is why the later years add far more than the early years, even though the monthly deposit never changes. These figures assume a fixed 6% rate, with no fees, taxes, gaps, or changing rates, and they do not include car price rises, insurance, fuel, servicing, or registration, so they are planning estimates, not promises.

Who this term suits for a car buyer: a growing family planning a step up in car, rather than a like-for-like replacement — for example parents moving from a small hatchback to a larger estate or people-carrier as children grow, or a household saving for a second car so two adults no longer have to share one.

FAQs

How does monthly saving grow?
Regular deposits grow because each deposit earns interest, and that interest then earns more interest.

Is this car calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

How does a 15-year term balance the monthly amount and the interest?
At 15 years the balance shifts: you have paid in $90,000.00 and interest has added $55,409.36, which is 38.1% of the $145,409.36 future value. The monthly $500 is still the backbone, but interest is no longer a small bonus. That is why a missed year in the middle costs more than a missed year at the start, when there is less saved to compound.

What if the rate falls below 6% over 15 years?
The future value falls, and the longer the lower rate lasts, the bigger the gap. This page assumes a fixed 6% for all 15 years, which is a planning assumption, not a promise. If you are saving for a family car in 15 years, re-check the rate every few years and adjust the monthly deposit if the return is running below the plan.

Can I use a 15-year fund for a car I need sooner?
Yes, but only the amount saved by then. A 15-year figure of $145,409.36 is not available at year 8 or year 10. If a car is needed sooner, the fund at that point is smaller, and the rest must come from a cheaper car, a trade-in, or finance. Set the term to match when you actually expect to buy.
