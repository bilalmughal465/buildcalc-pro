Personal Credit Card Monthly Interest Calculator (20 Years)

Estimate monthly interest on a card balance. This personal version uses a 20-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Monthly interest equals balance times annual rate divided by 100 divided by 12.

How it works

Paying only interest leaves the balance unchanged. Paying more than interest reduces the balance.

Worked example

Balance $5,000.00, annual rate 18%, term 20 years. First, the monthly interest is $5,000.00 times 18 divided by 100 divided by 12, which is $900.00 divided by 12, which is $75.00. Over 240 months, paying only interest costs $75.00 times 240, which is $18,000.00. That is 360.0% of the balance. If only interest is paid, the balance at the end is still $5,000.00, because no part of the $75.00 payment repays the balance itself.

Who this term suits for a person: A 20-year term suits a person near a retirement or long-term planning point who wants to face a balance that has never been cleared, for example someone who has paid card interest through two decades of work and now asks what those payments bought. Twenty years of $75.00 a month is $18,000.00, and the answer is stark: it bought nothing but time, because the $5,000.00 is still owed.

FAQs

Why is card interest high?
Card rates are usually higher than loan rates, so balances grow quickly if only minimum payments are made.

Is this personal calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does 20 years cost $18,000.00, more than three times the balance?
Because $75.00 a month for 240 months is $18,000.00, which is 360.0% of the $5,000.00 balance. Interest-only payments never touch the principal, so the total keeps growing in a straight line, $900.00 every year, for as long as the balance lasts.

Is paying only interest for 20 years realistic?
It happens, but it is not a plan. Twenty years of minimum or interest-only payments usually means the card has been used again and again, or the balance was never given a repayment target. A 20-year horizon should prompt a clear payoff target, not another year of $75.00 payments.

How does a lower rate change the $75.00 figure?
Directly, because the formula multiplies balance by rate. At 10%, monthly interest on $5,000.00 is $41.67, and 240 months of that is $10,000.00 instead of $18,000.00. The balance would still be $5,000.00 if only interest were paid, so rate alone does not clear a debt.
