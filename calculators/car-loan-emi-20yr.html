Car Loan EMI Calculator (20 Years)

Estimate the monthly EMI for a loan from amount, rate and term. This car version uses a 20-year planning term as its starting point.

—

This means the Monthly payment is the fixed amount due every month, and the Total paid is the sum of all monthly payments over the full 20-year term.

Formula

EMI equals principal times monthly rate times one plus monthly rate to the power of payments, divided by that power minus one.

How it works

This assumes a fixed rate and equal monthly payments with no extra fees.

Worked example

Loan amount $200,000, annual rate 6.5%, term 20 years. First, the monthly rate is 6.5% divided by 12, which is 0.0054167, and the number of payments is 20 times 12, which is 240. Using the EMI formula, the monthly payment is $1,491.15. Over 240 payments the total paid is $357,875.11, calculated from the unrounded EMI exactly as the calculator does. Total interest is $357,875.11 minus $200,000, which is $157,875.11. That is 78.9% of the amount borrowed, so the buyer pays back nearly 1.8 times the loan.

Who this term suits for a car buyer: A 20-year term does not suit a buyer of an ordinary daily car, because no ordinary car stays on the road as the buyer's main car for 20 years. Its only plausible car use is a collector or classic car that holds or gains value and is kept, not driven daily, for decades. Even then, storage, insurance and restoration costs sit on top of the $1,491.15 payment.

FAQs

What is an EMI?
An EMI is a fixed monthly payment that covers both interest and principal over the loan term.

Is this car calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does the monthly payment fall so little after 20 years?
Because most of the early payments go to interest, not to the loan balance, so stretching the term mainly adds more interest payments. Moving from 15 years to 20 years cuts the payment from $1,742.21 to $1,491.15, a saving of $251.06 a month, but adds $44,276.46 in total interest, taking it from $113,598.65 to $157,875.11.

Is a 20-year term realistic for any car, or only for a classic or collector car?
Only for a car that is expected to hold its value and be kept for decades, such as a collector car, and even then it is a stretch. An ordinary car will be replaced several times in 20 years, so the buyer would be paying $1,491.15 a month for cars that are long gone.

What should a car buyer check about repairs and replacement before a 20-year loan?
The buyer should check how long the car is expected to last, what major repairs it will need, and what it will cost to replace it when it wears out. If a replacement car will be needed during the loan, the new car payment would sit on top of the old $1,491.15 payment, which is how a long car loan becomes unaffordable.
