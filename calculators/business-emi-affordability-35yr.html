Business EMI Affordability Calculator (35 Years)

Estimate a safe monthly EMI from income. This business version uses a 35-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Safe EMI equals monthly income times safe share divided by 100.

How it works

Lenders often use a share of income as a guide, but other debts also matter.

Worked example

Monthly business income $10,000, safe EMI share 30%. Using the formula, the safe EMI is $10,000 times 30 divided by 100, which is $3,000 a month. Over a 35-year term that is 420 payments, so the total EMI outflow is $3,000 times 420, which is $1,260,000. As an illustration only, not the output of this page, a $3,000 monthly payment could support a loan of about $496,562 over 35 years at 6.5% a year, using the standard EMI formula. The step from 30 to 35 years adds only about $21,929 to the supportable loan, while adding $180,000 to the total outflow.

Who this term suits for a business: A 35-year term suits almost no ordinary business borrowing except a freehold property held for the whole term, and even then it needs a succession plan. The owner should ask who will still be running the business and paying $3,000 a month in year 30, because the loan will outlast most staff, leases and business plans.

FAQs

How much EMI can I afford?
A common guide is 30 percent of monthly income, but total debts and living costs must also fit.

Is this business calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does the supportable loan rise only about $21,929 from 30 to 35 years?
Because by year 30 most of each $3,000 payment is still going to interest on a slowly falling balance, and the extra 60 payments add far more interest than principal. Extending the term is the least efficient way to borrow more: $180,000 of extra payments buys only about $21,929 of extra loan at 6.5%.

Who should even consider a 35-year business term?
A business buying a freehold property it intends to occupy for the full term, with stable income and a plan for ownership succession. A business that may move, sell or change direction should not take a 35-year loan, because breaking it early can mean fees and a property sale under pressure.

What must a business check before signing a very long term?
The lender's early-repayment fees, whether the rate is fixed or variable, what happens on a missed payment, and whether the property value and the business income are likely to hold for 35 years. This calculator only checks income against a safe share, so rate, fees and property risk must be checked separately.
