Farm Break Even Units Calculator (5 Years)

Find how many units must be sold to break even. This farm version uses a 5-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Break even units is the number of units you must sell for revenue to exactly cover all costs, so selling fewer units means a loss and selling more units means a profit.

Formula

Break even units equal fixed costs divided by price minus variable cost per unit.

How it works

At break even, total revenue exactly covers total costs, with no profit and no loss.

Worked example

Fixed costs $10,000, price per unit $50, variable cost per unit $30, term 5 years. First, the contribution per unit is $50 minus $30, which is $20. Using the page formula, break even units are $10,000 divided by $20, which is 500.00 units. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults, and it is the same on every page in this group because the term is not an input. At break even, revenue is 500 times $50, which is $25,000, variable costs are 500 times $30, which is $15,000, and $25,000 minus $15,000 minus $10,000 of fixed costs leaves zero profit, exactly as break even should.

As an illustration only, not the output of this page, spreading those 500 units evenly over 5 years is about 100.00 units a year. That yearly figure is only a planning average: a farm does not sell evenly, harvests, seasons, and prices vary, and fixed costs, prices, and variable costs will change over a long term.

Who this term suits for a farm household: a farm starting a short, clear project, for example a vegetable grower testing a new crop line, a dairy farmer covering a new milking-equipment loan, or a poultry keeper working out how many birds or crates must sell before a shed extension pays for itself, where the next few harvests or seasons must carry the cost.

FAQs

What is break even?
Break even is the point where revenue covers all costs, so profit is zero.

Is this farm calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is 500 units in 5 years a small target for a farm?
It depends on the product. Spread evenly, the illustration is 100.00 units a year, which is modest for a vegetable grower selling crates weekly but large for a farm selling high-value breeding stock. Judge the 500 units against your own yearly sales, not against another farm.

What farm costs count as fixed costs in this example?
Fixed costs are costs that do not change with each extra unit sold, such as rent, insurance, a shed loan, or salaried labour. Seed, feed, fuel, packaging, and casual picking labour are usually variable costs, because they rise with each extra unit produced.

What if a farm sells fewer than 500 units in 5 years?
Then it has not broken even. For example, selling 400 units gives revenue of $20,000 and variable costs of $12,000, leaving $8,000 of contribution against $10,000 of fixed costs, which is a $2,000 shortfall before any other costs.
