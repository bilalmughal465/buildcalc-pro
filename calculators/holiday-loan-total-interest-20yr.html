Holiday Loan Total Interest Calculator (20 Years)

Estimate total interest paid over a loan. This holiday version uses a 20-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Total interest equals monthly EMI times number of payments minus the principal.

How it works

A longer term lowers the monthly payment but raises total interest.

Worked example

Loan amount $200,000, annual rate 6.5%, term 20 years. The monthly rate is 6.5% divided by 12, which is 0.0054167, and there are 240 monthly payments. Using the page formula, the EMI is $1,491.15 a month, so the total paid is $357,875.11. Total interest is $357,875.11 minus $200,000, equal to $157,875.11, or 78.9% of the amount borrowed. That is about $7,893.76 of interest a year.

By 20 years the interest bill is approaching the amount borrowed itself. Stretching from 15 to 20 years saves only $251.06 a month, but adds $44,276.46 of interest. The monthly saving is now shrinking fast while the extra interest keeps growing, which is why longer terms give less and less monthly relief for more and more total cost.

Who this term suits for a holiday borrower: a household financing a holiday home that the family expects to use for two decades, for example a seaside flat or a countryside cottage visited every school holiday, where the long use may justify a long term, but only if the home stays in the family and in good repair for that whole period.

FAQs

Why does a longer loan cost more interest?
More payments mean interest is charged for longer, so total interest rises even if each payment is smaller.

Is this holiday calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is 20 years too long for a holiday loan?
For a trip, yes, without question. For a holiday home used for 20 years, it can be argued, but the borrower should remember that $157,875.11 of interest is nearly as much again as the $200,000 borrowed.

Why does the EMI fall by only $251.06 from 15 to 20 years?
Because most of the monthly payment at longer terms is interest rather than balance reduction, so adding five more years of payments buys only a small monthly saving while adding $44,276.46 of interest.

What happens if the borrower refinances at year 10 of a 20-year holiday loan?
Refinancing replaces the remaining balance with a new loan, which may carry a different rate, fees, or term, so the total interest to the end could fall or rise: it cannot be judged from this fixed 6.5% example alone, and any fees must be counted.
