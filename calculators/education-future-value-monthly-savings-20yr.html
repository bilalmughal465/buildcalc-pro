Education Future Value Monthly Savings Calculator (20 Years)

Estimate the future value of regular monthly savings. This education version uses a 20-year planning term as its starting point.

—

In real terms, the Future value is the total amount your regular monthly deposits are expected to have grown to by the end of the 20-year term, including both the money you paid in and the interest it earned along the way.

Formula

Future value uses the monthly rate and the number of monthly deposits to grow each deposit with interest.

How it works

Each monthly deposit earns interest from the month it is made until the end of the term.

Worked example

A family saves $500 every month for 20 years at 6% a year for a child's education. The monthly rate is 6% divided by 12, which is 0.005, and there are 240 monthly deposits. Using the page formula, the future value is $500 times ((1.005 to the power of 240 minus 1) divided by 0.005), which is $231,020.45. The family paid in $120,000, so interest added $111,020.45, which is 48.1% of the future value. Interest is now almost as large as everything the family paid in, and the last 5 years alone added $85,611.09.

Who this term suits for an education saver: a family saving from a child's early years for university two decades away, for example parents of a young child who want the fund to cover a degree abroad, where fees and living costs are higher, and who accept that the course and country will change long before the money is spent.

FAQs

How does monthly saving grow?
Regular deposits grow because each deposit earns interest, and that interest then earns more interest.

Is this education calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does interest pass $100,000 at 20 years?
Because the earliest deposits have compounded for 240 months. Interest of $111,020.45 is the reward for leaving the earliest money untouched for two decades, not for any single large deposit.

Is 20 years too long to forecast for education costs?
Yes, as a precise forecast it is. Fees, courses, countries and currencies will all change in 20 years, so $231,020.45 should be read as a scale check for a saving habit, not as a promise that it will match the eventual bill.

Should education saving come before clearing a family loan?
Usually the expensive loan comes first, because loan interest often runs above 6% and is certain, while fund growth is only an estimate. A family carrying high-rate debt should clear that before relying on a 20-year education fund.
