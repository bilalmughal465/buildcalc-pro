Medical Monthly Budget Split Calculator (5 Years)

Split monthly income into needs, wants and savings. This medical version uses a 5-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The result splits your monthly income into three buckets: Needs (50%) covers essentials such as rent, bills and clinic costs, Wants (30%) covers non-essential spending, and Savings (20%) is the amount set aside each month, so if the savings figure looks too small, the wants figure is the first place to trim.

Formula

Needs are 50 percent, wants are 30 percent and savings are 20 percent of income.

How it works

This 50-30-20 split is a planning guide, not a rule. Adjust it to your real costs.

Worked example

Monthly income $4,000, term 5 years. Using the page formula, needs are 50 percent of $4,000, which is $2,000 a month, wants are 30 percent, which is $1,200 a month, and savings are 20 percent, which is $800 a month. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults, and it is the same on every page in this group because the term is not an input: only the monthly income decides the split.

As an illustration only, not the output of this page, holding that same monthly split steady for 5 years (60 months) would total $120,000 for needs, $72,000 for wants and $48,000 for savings. That illustration counts no interest earned, no inflation, no fees and no change in income, so it shows the scale of a steady habit, not a forecast.

Who this term suits for a medical household: a newly qualified doctor or dentist in the first years of practice, setting up a monthly habit while income is still building — for example, a GP registrar or a junior dentist covering rent, loan repayments and clinic costs, and ring-fencing savings for a first piece of equipment such as a dental chair or an ultrasound machine.

FAQs

What is the 50-30-20 rule?
It is a simple budget guide: 50 percent needs, 30 percent wants and 20 percent savings.

Is this medical calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is $800 a month of savings realistic for a new medical practice over 5 years?
Yes, if income really is $4,000 a month after tax and essential costs fit inside $2,000 of needs — but a new practice often has irregular income, so the $800 should be treated as a target month, not a promise every month.

What if clinic costs push needs above $2,000 in the first 5 years?
Then the split has to move: either wants are cut below $1,200, or savings fall below $800 for a while. Needs are not optional, so they take priority and the other two columns absorb the change.

Can 5 years of this split fund a first piece of medical equipment?
At $800 a month, 5 years of saving totals $48,000 before any interest, which covers a basic dental chair or a small ultrasound unit — but prices, fees and taxes are not counted here, so the real purchase needs its own quote.
