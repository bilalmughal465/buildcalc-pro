Business Loan EMI Calculator (10 Years)

Estimate the monthly EMI for a loan from amount, rate and term. This business version uses a 10-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

EMI equals principal times monthly rate times one plus monthly rate to the power of payments, divided by that power minus one.

How it works

This assumes a fixed rate and equal monthly payments with no extra fees.

Worked example

Loan amount $200,000, annual rate 6.5%, term 10 years. The monthly rate is 6.5% divided by 12, which is 0.0054167, and the number of payments is 10 times 12, which is 120. Using the EMI formula, the monthly payment is $2,270.96. Over 120 payments the total paid is $2,270.96 times 120, which is $272,515.15. Total interest is $272,515.15 minus $200,000, which is $72,515.15. That is 36.3% of the amount borrowed, a middle position in this year group: much cheaper per month than 5 years, but with more than twice the interest bill.

Who this term suits for a business: A 10-year term suits a growing business that needs a manageable payment while expanding, for example a restaurant opening a second outlet or a retailer stocking a larger range, where $2,270.96 a month is covered by expected extra sales but $3,913.23 would leave no buffer for a bad quarter. The business keeps room to reinvest, and accepts $72,515.15 in interest for that flexibility.

FAQs

What is an EMI?
An EMI is a fixed monthly payment that covers both interest and principal over the loan term.

Is this business calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why is a 10-year term a common middle choice for a business loan?
Because $2,270.96 a month is far easier on cash flow than the 5-year payment, while the interest bill of $72,515.15 is still well below the longer terms. It balances monthly affordability against total cost, which is why many businesses plan around a decade for equipment or expansion loans.

How much more interest does 10 years cost than 5 years?
It costs $37,721.37 more: $72,515.15 versus $34,793.78 on the same $200,000 at 6.5%. A business should weigh that extra cost against what the freed-up $1,642.27 a month can earn if reinvested in the business.

How should a business test whether $2,270.96 a month is affordable?
Compare it with the business's lowest recent monthly cash flow after rent, wages, stock and tax, not the average month. If the payment still fits in the worst recent month with a buffer left over, the term is affordable on planning numbers. If not, a longer term or a smaller loan should be considered, and a qualified adviser can check the figures.
