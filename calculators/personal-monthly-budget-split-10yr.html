Personal Monthly Budget Split Calculator (10 Years)

Split monthly income into needs, wants and savings. This personal version uses a 10-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Needs are 50 percent, wants are 30 percent and savings are 20 percent of income.

How it works

This 50-30-20 split is a planning guide, not a rule. Adjust it to your real costs.

Worked example

Monthly income $4,000, using the page formula. Needs are 50 percent of $4,000, which is $2,000 a month. Wants are 30 percent of $4,000, which is $1,200 a month. Savings are 20 percent of $4,000, which is $800 a month. Together they add back to $4,000, so the monthly split checks out. Over a 10-year planning term there are 120 months. As a simple illustration only, not an output of this calculator, multiplying the monthly figures by 120 gives needs of $240,000, wants of $144,000 and savings of $96,000. Those totals count no interest, no pay rises and no inflation, because this calculator gives only the monthly split.

Who this term suits for a person: A 10-year term suits a person planning a medium goal that needs steady saving, for example a house deposit, a child's school costs, or clearing a mortgage's first decade. $96,000 of savings over 10 years is close to a six-figure milestone, which makes the habit feel real, but only if the $800 a month is actually moved to savings before wants spending starts.

FAQs

What is the 50-30-20 rule?
It is a simple budget guide: 50 percent needs, 30 percent wants and 20 percent savings.

Is this personal calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does $800 a month reach $96,000 in 10 years without interest?
It is simple multiplication, not a calculator promise: 120 months times $800 is $96,000. The calculator gives only the monthly split, and this total counts no interest, no pay rises and no inflation. Any interest a real savings account paid would be extra, and is not included here.

Is a 10-year budget split realistic with a house move or children?
Only if it is recalculated at each life change. A move can push needs above $2,000, and children add needs that did not exist before. The 50-30-20 percentages can stay as a guide, but the amounts must be re-run with the new income and the new costs.

How do I stop wants from eating the $1,200 wants budget?
Treat wants as a capped monthly amount, not as whatever is left. Moving the $800 savings out on payday, and keeping wants spending in a separate account or card with a $1,200 limit, makes the split a habit instead of a hope.
