Shop Credit Card Monthly Interest Calculator (30 Years)

Estimate monthly interest on a card balance. This shop version uses a 30-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The Monthly interest result is the interest charged on the balance each month at the entered annual rate, so paying only this amount each month covers the interest but leaves the balance unchanged, and over 30 years it would add up to $27,000 if only interest were paid on the default balance.

Formula

Monthly interest equals balance times annual rate divided by 100 divided by 12.

How it works

Paying only interest leaves the balance unchanged. Paying more than interest reduces the balance.

Worked example

Balance $5,000, annual rate 18%, term 30 years. Using the page formula, monthly interest equals $5,000 times 18 divided by 100 divided by 12, which is $75.00 a month. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults, and it is the same on every page in this group because the term is not an input: only the balance and the rate decide the answer.

If only the $75.00 of interest is paid every month for 30 years, the total interest paid over 360 months is $27,000, and the $5,000 balance is still owed at the end, because interest-only payments never reduce the balance. That total is an illustration only, not a second output of this page: it assumes the rate stays at 18% and no new spending is added to the card.

Who this term suits for a shop owner: a shop owner comparing a card with a premises mortgage, where the mortgage ends and the card does not, so the card is the wrong tool for a 30-year plan.

FAQs

Why is card interest high?
Card rates are usually higher than loan rates, so balances grow quickly if only minimum payments are made.

Is this shop calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is a 30-year card balance the same as a shop premises mortgage?
No. A mortgage balance falls with every payment and ends, while an interest-only card balance stays at $5,000 after 30 years and $27,000 of interest.

What happens if shop sales fall for several years in a 30-year plan?
The $75 monthly interest still has to be paid, so it eats a bigger share of smaller takings, and any missed payment can add fees and push the rate higher.

Should I keep using the same card while repaying over 30 years?
No. New spending on the same card raises the balance and the monthly interest, so the debt grows even while interest is being paid.
