Home Credit Card Monthly Interest Calculator (30 Years)

Estimate monthly interest on a card balance. This home version uses a 30-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Monthly interest equals balance times annual rate divided by 100 divided by 12.

How it works

Paying only interest leaves the balance unchanged. Paying more than interest reduces the balance.

Worked example

Balance $5,000, annual rate 18 percent, term 30 years. Using the page formula, monthly interest equals $5,000 times 18 divided by 100 divided by 12, which is $75.00 per month. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults, and it is the same on every page in this group because the term is not an input.

As an illustration only, if only the $75 interest is paid every month and the balance never falls, then over 30 years the interest paid totals $75 times 12 times 30, which is $27,000. At the end of that term the balance would still be $5,000, because paying only interest leaves the balance unchanged. That total is an illustration, not the output of this page: it assumes the balance stays at $5,000 and the rate stays at 18 percent for the whole term, which will not happen if new home spending is added to the card or the rate changes.

Who this term suits for a home household: nobody as a home purchase plan, because 30 years is mortgage length, not card length. This term is a warning: a card balance that is still being serviced after 30 years means the household has paid $27,000 in interest alone and still owes the original $5,000.

FAQs

Why is card interest high?
Card rates are usually higher than loan rates, so balances grow quickly if only minimum payments are made.

Is this home calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is a 30-year card balance the same as a house mortgage?
No. A mortgage is repaid over its term, so the balance falls to zero. A card balance paid at interest only never falls, so after 30 years the household has paid $27,000 in interest and still owes $5,000.

What happens if household income falls for several years in a 30-year plan?
Paying more than the interest becomes harder, so the balance stays high and interest keeps being charged on it. A long card plan with no buffer is fragile when income drops.

Should I keep using the same card while repaying over 30 years?
Using the same card for new home spending while trying to repay it keeps the balance up and the interest high. Separating new spending from repayment is one of the first steps to clearing a long balance.
