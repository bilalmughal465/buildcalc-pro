Personal Inflation Adjusted Value Calculator (15 Years)

Estimate what an amount will be worth after inflation. This personal version uses a 15-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Future amount equals amount times one plus inflation rate to the power of years.

How it works

Inflation means the same amount buys less over time, so future prices are higher.

Worked example

Amount $10,000, inflation rate 3%, term 15 years. First, the factor is 1.03 to the power of 15, which is 1.557967. Future amount is $10,000 times 1.557967, which is $15,579.67. That is $5,579.67 more than today, or 55.8% higher, so the same basket would cost $15,579.67 in 15 years at 3% inflation.

Who this term suits for a person: A 15-year term suits a person saving for a child's university costs or a mid-career move, where the goal is far enough away that today's price is misleading. By 15 years the price has risen by more than half, so a target set at $10,000 today would fall $5,579.67 short if inflation is ignored.

FAQs

What does inflation do to money?
Inflation reduces what money can buy, so a sum today will need to be larger in future to buy the same things.

Is this personal calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why is $15,579.67 more than half as much again as $10,000?
Because 3% a year for 15 years compounds to a factor of 1.557967. Simple interest at 3% for 15 years would give only $14,500, so the extra $1,079.67 is compounding, the interest on earlier price rises, and it grows every year.

What changes most if the rate is not 3% over 15 years?
The whole factor. At a higher rate the $15,579.67 figure rises quickly, and at a lower rate it falls, so a 15-year plan should be re-checked whenever inflation expectations change, rather than locked to the first calculation.

Can a 15-year inflation figure be used for a house or loan decision?
For planning only. It shows what $10,000 today becomes in future prices, but house prices and loan rates do not follow general inflation exactly, so use $15,579.67 as a price guide, not as a house price or a loan total.
