Wedding SIP Future Value Calculator (25 Years)

Estimate the future value of a regular investment plan. This wedding version uses a 25-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Future value uses the monthly rate and number of monthly investments to grow each investment with interest.

How it works

Regular investing smooths timing and lets compounding build the value over time.

Worked example

Monthly investment $300, annual rate 8 percent, term 25 years. The monthly rate is 8 percent divided by 12, which is about 0.6667 percent a month, and the number of investments is 25 times 12, which is 300 months. Using the page formula, the future value is $300 times one plus 0.006667 to the power of 300, minus one, divided by 0.006667, which gives $285,307.92. That is the calculator output for these defaults. The couple pays in $90,000 in total, so $195,307.92 of the future value is interest.

Who this term suits for a wedding: a long-horizon wedding fund, such as parents saving from a child's birth, where a small monthly amount grows into a very large figure but must be reviewed as plans change.

FAQs

What is a SIP?
A SIP is a regular investment of a fixed amount, usually monthly, into a fund or plan.

Is this wedding calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is $300 a month really enough to reach $285,307.92?
Yes, on the stated assumptions: $300 a month for 300 months at 8 percent. The couple pays in $90,000 and interest adds $195,307.92, more than double what was paid in, which is why the figure looks so large.

What is the biggest risk in a 25-year wedding savings plan?
That the plan is forgotten or the rate assumed never happens. A 25-year plan only works if the monthly amount keeps being paid and is reviewed when income, prices or wedding plans change.

Should the monthly amount rise as income grows over 25 years?
Yes, if the couple can afford it. Saving $400 a month instead of $300 would give $380,410.56 over 25 years at 8 percent, and rising with income also protects the fund against 25 years of price rises.
