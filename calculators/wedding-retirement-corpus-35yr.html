Wedding Retirement Corpus Calculator (35 Years)

Estimate a retirement corpus from monthly savings. This wedding version uses a 35-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The Future value result shows the total retirement fund your regular monthly savings could build up over 35 years at the rate you entered, combining every monthly payment with the growth earned on those payments.

Formula

Corpus uses the monthly rate and number of monthly savings to grow each saving with interest.

How it works

Starting earlier matters more than saving a little more later, because interest has longer to compound.

Worked example

Monthly saving $500, annual rate 7 percent, term 35 years. The monthly rate is 7 percent divided by 12, which is about 0.5833 percent a month, and the number of savings is 35 times 12, which is 420 months. Using the page formula, the future value is $500 times one plus the monthly rate to the power of 420, minus one, divided by the monthly rate, which gives $900,527.30. That is the calculator output for these defaults. The couple pays in $210,000 in total, so $690,527.30 of the corpus is interest. That is the same monthly saving and rate as on every page in this group; only the time input changes, and that alone produces the different corpus figures across the years.

Who this term suits for a wedding: nobody as an ordinary wedding plan, because a 35-year horizon spans a working life; its use is as a warning illustration that a small steady habit compounds into a very large figure, but no wedding plan should be trusted for 35 years without regular review.

FAQs

When should I start saving for retirement?
As early as possible. Time in the market allows compounding to do more of the work.

Is this wedding calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

How does $500 a month become $900,527.30 over 35 years?
Because 420 savings compound for up to 35 years at 7 percent. The couple pays in $210,000 and interest adds $690,527.30. The last years add the most, because interest is earned on a very large balance.

Is a 35-year wedding plan worth starting if plans will change?
Yes, as a savings habit, but not as a fixed wedding budget. Plans, prices and incomes will change many times in 35 years, so the monthly saving and the goal should be reviewed regularly.

What if the couple saves only $400 a month?
The formula scales with the monthly saving, so $400 a month over 35 years at 7 percent gives $720,421.84, which is $180,105.46 less than the $900,527.30 from $500 a month.
