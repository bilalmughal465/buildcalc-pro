Shop SIP Future Value Calculator (5 Years)

Estimate the future value of a regular investment plan. This shop version uses a 5-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The Future value is the estimated total your regular monthly investments grow to by the end of the term, so it shows the lump sum you would have built up if the entered rate holds throughout.

Formula

Future value uses the monthly rate and number of monthly investments to grow each investment with interest.

How it works

Regular investing smooths timing and lets compounding build the value over time.

Worked example

Monthly investment $300.00, annual rate 8 percent, term 5 years. The monthly rate is 8 percent divided by 12, which is about 0.6667 percent a month, and the number of monthly investments is 5 times 12, which is 60 months. Using the page formula, the future value is $300.00 times one plus the monthly rate to the power of 60, minus one, divided by the monthly rate, which gives $22,043.06. That is the calculator output for these defaults.

The shop owner pays in $18,000.00 in total, so $4,043.06 of the value is interest. Without any interest at all, reaching the same $22,043.06 would need about $367.38 a month for 60 months, which shows how much of the result comes from compounding rather than from the payments alone. All figures are estimates for planning only, based on the entered 8 percent assumption, before fees and taxes.

Who this term suits for a shop owner: a shop building a near-term fund, such as a till and stock-system upgrade, a shopfront refresh, or a new refrigeration unit due in about 5 years.

FAQs

What is a SIP?
A SIP is a regular investment of a fixed amount, usually monthly, into a fund or plan.

Is this shop calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is $300 a month for 5 years enough for a shop refit?
It gives $22,043.06, of which $18,000 is your own payments and $4,043.06 is interest. That covers a modest refit, a till upgrade, or new refrigeration, but a full shop refit may need a larger monthly amount or a longer term.

Why is the interest only $4,043.06 over 5 years?
Compounding needs time. In the early years most of the value is your own $300 payments, because each payment has only a few years to earn interest. The interest share grows sharply in later years, as the 10-year figure shows.

What if shop takings fall and a payment is missed?
A missed payment means less money compounding, so the final value falls below $22,043.06. The formula counts only the payments actually made, so the plan should be restarted or topped up as soon as takings recover.
