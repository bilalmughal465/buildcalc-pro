Business Retirement Corpus Calculator (35 Years)

Estimate a retirement corpus from monthly savings. This business version uses a 35-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Corpus uses the monthly rate and number of monthly savings to grow each saving with interest.

How it works

Starting earlier matters more than saving a little more later, because interest has longer to compound.

Worked example

A business owner sets aside $500 every month for 35 years at 7% a year. The monthly rate is 7% divided by 12, which is 0.0058333, and there are 420 monthly savings. Using the page formula, the corpus is $500 times ((1.0058333 to the power of 420 minus 1) divided by 0.0058333), which is $900,527.30. The owner paid in $210,000, so interest added $690,527.30, which is 76.7% of the corpus. The final 5 years alone add $290,541.80, which is more than the whole pot after 20 years.

Who this term suits for a business: an owner planning a late, comfortable retirement — for example a professional firm founder who kept the business running into their 60s and wants the pot to fund retirement without selling client relationships at a discount.

FAQs

When should I start saving for retirement?
As early as possible. Time in the market allows compounding to do more of the work.

Is this business calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why do the final 5 years add $290,541.80 on their own?
Because 7% on a $609,985.50 pot adds about $42,700 in a single year, while 7% on an early pot adds only a few hundred dollars. Late compounding is where the largest dollar gains appear, which is why the last years matter most.

Is a 35-year forecast reliable for a business owner?
No single 35-year figure is reliable: tax rules, rates, health, and the business itself will all change. The figure is useful only as a direction and a scale — it shows what steady saving can do, not what will certainly happen.

What costs does this calculator leave out over 35 years?
Fees, taxes, inflation, and any withdrawals. Over 35 years inflation alone can halve the buying power of a fixed sum, so the real value of $900,527.30 in today's money would be far lower. Treat the headline as nominal, not as today's spending power.
