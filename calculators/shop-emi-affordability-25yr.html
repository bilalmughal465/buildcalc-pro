Shop EMI Affordability Calculator (25 Years)

Estimate a safe monthly EMI from income. This shop version uses a 25-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Safe EMI equals monthly income times safe share divided by 100.

How it works

Lenders often use a share of income as a guide, but other debts also matter.

Worked example

Monthly shop income $5,000, safe EMI share 30 percent, term 25 years. Using the page formula, safe EMI equals $5,000 times 30 divided by 100, which is $1,500 per month. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults, and it is the same on every page in this group because the term is not an input.

As an illustration only, paying $1,500 every month for 25 years would total $450,000 in monthly payments. That total is not the loan amount you can borrow, because this page does not include interest, fees, or a changing rate, and shop takings will not stay at $5,000 every month for 25 years.

Who this term suits for a shop owner: a shop owner planning around a premises purchase or a very long lease, where the EMI must be judged against a full generation of trading, including changes in the street, the customer base, and the products sold.

FAQs

How much EMI can I afford?
A common guide is 30 percent of monthly income, but total debts and living costs must also fit.

Is this shop calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why is the 25-year total of $450,000 not the price of a shop I can buy?
Because $450,000 is only the sum of $1,500 monthly payments over 300 months. It is not a loan amount, since this page leaves out interest, fees, and rate changes, and it assumes shop takings stay at $5,000 a month for 25 years, which they will not.

How should a shop review a 25-year EMI?
At least once a year, against real takings, real costs, and the remaining lease. A 25-year plan that is never reviewed will drift away from the shop's actual position, and the owner may only notice when a payment becomes hard.

What shop risks grow over 25 years?
Lease renewals, rent rises, new competitors, changing shopping habits, and the need to refit the shop more than once. Any one of these can change takings enough that a $1,500 EMI stops being comfortable.

Estimates only, not financial advice. Figures are approximate and do not include all fees, taxes, or changing rates unless stated.
