Business Simple Interest Total Calculator (40 Years)

Calculate simple interest and the total amount to repay. This business version uses a 40-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Over 40 years, the Interest figure is the extra cost of borrowing on top of the original principal, and the Total figure is the full amount the business pays back.

Formula

Simple interest equals principal times rate times time, divided by 100.

How it works

Interest is charged only on the original principal, not on interest already added.

Worked example

Principal $10,000, annual rate 5%, term 40 years. Simple interest is $10,000 times 5 times 40, divided by 100, which is $20,000.00. The total to repay is $10,000 plus $20,000.00, which is $30,000.00. After 40 years the interest is twice the amount borrowed: $20,000.00 of interest on a $10,000 loan. The business pays back $30,000.00 in total, which is 200% growth on the original amount, so the business pays back three times the original sum.

Who this term suits for a business: no business as a real borrowing plan. Its only honest use is as a lifetime warning, showing what happens when a small loan is stretched across a whole working life instead of being cleared while the asset still earns.

FAQs

What is simple interest?
Simple interest is interest charged only on the original amount, not on accumulated interest.

Is this business calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is a 40-year business loan a good idea?
No, for ordinary business purposes. After 40 years the business has paid $30,000.00 for $10,000 borrowed, and no ordinary asset, supplier, or market stays unchanged for 40 years. It should be read as a warning, not as a plan.

How can $10,000 turn into $30,000.00 of total payments?
Because simple interest adds $500 every year, and 40 years of $500 is $20,000.00 of interest. Add the original $10,000 back, and the total to repay is $30,000.00, with no compounding involved.

What alternatives should a business compare before a 40-year term?
A shorter loan, saving a larger deposit first, leasing instead of buying, or buying a smaller asset. Any of these cuts the interest bill sharply, because each year removed saves another $500 at this rate.
