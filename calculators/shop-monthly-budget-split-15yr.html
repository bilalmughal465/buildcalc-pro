Shop Monthly Budget Split Calculator (15 Years)

Split monthly income into needs, wants and savings. This shop version uses a 15-year planning term as its starting point.

—

In plain terms, Needs (50%) is the share of monthly income set aside for essentials, Wants (30%) is the share for non-essential spending, and Savings (20%) is the share kept aside, so the three amounts together use up the full monthly income.

Formula

Needs are 50 percent, wants are 30 percent and savings are 20 percent of income.

How it works

This 50-30-20 split is a planning guide, not a rule. Adjust it to your real costs.

Worked example

Monthly income $4,000, term 15 years. Needs are 50 percent of $4,000, which is $2,000 a month. Wants are 30 percent of $4,000, which is $1,200 a month. Savings are 20 percent of $4,000, which is $800 a month. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults, and it is the same on every page in this group because the term is not an input: only the monthly income decides the split.

As a planning illustration only, not an output of this page, keeping the same $4,000 income every month for 15 years means 180 months in total. Total income over the term would be $720,000, of which needs would be $360,000, wants $216,000 and savings $144,000. These totals assume the income never changes, and they leave out fees, taxes, interest and changing rates, so they are a way to see what the split adds up to, not a forecast.

Who this term suits for a shop owner: A shop owner working toward owning the premises rather than renting: fifteen years of steady saving is long enough for the savings pot to rival a deposit, but only if the needs share is reviewed whenever rent rises.

FAQs

What is the 50-30-20 rule?
It is a simple budget guide: 50 percent needs, 30 percent wants and 20 percent savings.

Is this shop calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

How does $800 a month become $144,000 over 15 years?
It is $800 times 180 months at a steady $4,000 monthly income. It ignores interest earned, inflation and any month when takings fall, so treat it as a planning total, not a forecast.

Is a 15-year shop savings plan too long to trust?
The length is fine if the plan is reviewed. Fifteen years of rent rises will push needs above 50 percent unless the shop raises prices or cuts wants, so a yearly review matters more than the starting split.

What happens if the shop needs the savings before 15 years?
Use the pot for its purpose, such as a refit or a deposit, and restart the split afterwards. Dipping into it for everyday wants is what turns a fifteen-year plan into an empty fund.
