Home Loan Total Interest Calculator (5 Years)

Estimate total interest paid over a loan. This home version uses a 5-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Total interest equals monthly EMI times number of payments minus the principal.

How it works

A longer term lowers the monthly payment but raises total interest.

Worked example

Loan amount $200,000, annual rate 6.5%, term 5 years. The monthly rate is 6.5% divided by 12, which is 0.0054167, and there are 60 monthly payments. Using the page formula, the EMI is $3,913.23 a month, so the total paid is $234,793.78. Total interest is the total paid minus the principal, which is $234,793.78 minus $200,000, equal to $34,793.78, or 17.4 of the amount borrowed.

That is the highest monthly payment in this group, but also the lowest total interest, because the debt is cleared before interest has many years to build up. For a home buyer this is a short, sharp loan: the monthly payment is high, so it suits a household with strong, regular income that wants the mortgage gone quickly, rather than a household that needs room for moving costs, furnishing, and repairs.

Who this term suits for a home buyer: a household with strong monthly income, for example two earners buying a first home, who want a smaller mortgage finished quickly, before the next move or a growing family, and who can afford nearly $4,000 a month without cutting maintenance, insurance, or emergency savings.

FAQs

Why does a longer loan cost more interest?
More payments mean interest is charged for longer, so total interest rises even if each payment is smaller.

Is this home calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is $34,793.78 a lot of interest for a 5-year home loan?
No, it is the lowest interest bill in this group, at 17.4% of the amount borrowed, because the loan is cleared in only 60 payments. The price of that low bill is the highest monthly payment, $3,913.23.

Should a home buyer pay $3,913.23 a month, or take a longer term and keep cash for moving and furnishing?
It depends on cash flow. Paying $3,913.23 a month saves tens of thousands in interest, but only if moving, furnishing, repairs, and an emergency fund are still covered. If the payment leaves nothing spare, a longer term with planned overpayments may be safer.

Does a 5-year figure leave room for a job change or a roof repair?
Only if the household keeps a separate reserve. A $3,913.23 payment leaves little slack, so a job change or a major repair during the 5 years could strain the budget unless savings are set aside first.
