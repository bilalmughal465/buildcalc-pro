Business Simple Interest Total Calculator (15 Years)

Calculate simple interest and the total amount to repay. This business version uses a 15-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Over 15 years, the Interest figure is the extra cost of borrowing on top of the original principal, and the Total figure is the full amount the business pays back.

Formula

Simple interest equals principal times rate times time, divided by 100.

How it works

Interest is charged only on the original principal, not on interest already added.

Worked example

Principal $10,000, annual rate 5%, term 15 years. Simple interest is $10,000 times 5 times 15, divided by 100, which is $7,500.00. The total to repay is $10,000 plus $7,500.00, which is $17,500.00. After 15 years the interest reaches $7,500.00 without any compounding at all, simply because $500 a year for 15 years adds up. The business pays back $17,500.00 in total, which is 75% growth on the original amount.

Who this term suits for a business: a business planning leasehold improvements, a larger workshop, or a fleet renewal, where the spending is meant to last well over a decade and the owner wants the total cost in plain sight before signing.

FAQs

What is simple interest?
Simple interest is interest charged only on the original amount, not on accumulated interest.

Is this business calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is 15 years too long for a simple-interest business loan?
It depends on what the money buys. For a short-lived asset it is too long, because the business would still be paying after the asset is gone. For premises work or long-life equipment, 15 years can match the useful life of what was bought.

Why does interest reach $7,500.00 without compounding?
Because simple interest is charged only on the original $10,000, at $500 a year, and 15 times $500 is $7,500.00. No interest is charged on interest already added, which is exactly what makes simple interest predictable.

Can a 15-year simple-interest figure help a business decide between buying and leasing premises?
Yes, as one side of the comparison: the $17,500.00 total shows the full cost of borrowing to buy, which can then be set against 15 years of rent, repairs, and the value left in the premises at the end.
