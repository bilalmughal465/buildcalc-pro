Wedding Credit Card Monthly Interest Calculator (20 Years)

Estimate monthly interest on a card balance. This wedding version uses a 20-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Monthly interest equals balance times annual rate divided by 100 divided by 12.

How it works

Paying only interest leaves the balance unchanged. Paying more than interest reduces the balance.

Worked example

Balance $5,000, annual rate 18%, term 20 years. Using the page formula, the monthly interest is $5,000 times 18 divided by 100 divided by 12, which gives $75.00 a month. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults, and it is the same on every page in this group because the term is not an input: only the balance and the rate are entered. Paying only that $75 of interest leaves the balance unchanged, so over 240 months the interest paid is 240 times $75, which is $18,000, and the $5,000 is still owed at the end. This total is an illustration only, assuming the rate stays at 18% and no payments beyond interest are made; it is not a second output of this page.

Who this term suits for a wedding: a couple reviewing a wedding balance two decades on, for example a celebration that was charged to a card and then rolled forward through house moves and family costs, where the interest alone has long passed the original spending.

FAQs

Why is card interest high?
Card rates are usually higher than loan rates, so balances grow quickly if only minimum payments are made.

Is this wedding calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is $75 a month for 20 years realistic for a wedding debt?
As interest only, yes, but as a plan it is a poor one: after 20 years the couple has paid $18,000 in interest and still owes $5,000. A wedding debt that survives 20 years is a sign the repayment plan failed, not a normal timescale.

What if new celebrations keep being added to the same card?
Each new balance adds its own interest, so anniversaries, renewals, or family weddings charged to the same card push the monthly interest above $75 and the total far above $18,000. Separate cards and separate plans keep the cost visible.

Should a 20-year wedding balance be moved to a cheaper loan?
Usually yes. Even a personal loan at a lower rate, repaid over a fixed term, ends the debt instead of charging interest forever. The sooner the balance moves, the less of the $18,000-plus total is paid.
