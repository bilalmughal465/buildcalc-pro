Business EMI Affordability Calculator (15 Years)

Estimate a safe monthly EMI from income. This business version uses a 15-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Safe EMI equals monthly income times safe share divided by 100.

How it works

Lenders often use a share of income as a guide, but other debts also matter.

Worked example

Monthly business income $10,000, safe EMI share 30%. Using the formula, the safe EMI is $10,000 times 30 divided by 100, which is $3,000 a month. Over a 15-year term that is 180 payments, so the total EMI outflow is $3,000 times 180, which is $540,000. As an illustration only, not the output of this page, a $3,000 monthly payment could support a loan of about $344,389 over 15 years at 6.5% a year, using the standard EMI formula. The extra 5 years beyond 10 years adds about $80,184 to the supportable loan, but adds $180,000 to the total outflow.

Who this term suits for a business: A 15-year term suits a growing business buying machinery or a small fleet it expects to use for well over a decade, for example a delivery business replacing vans. The owner should confirm the asset will still earn money in year 12 or 13, because the $3,000 payment continues whether the asset does or not.

FAQs

How much EMI can I afford?
A common guide is 30 percent of monthly income, but total debts and living costs must also fit.

Is this business calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is a 15-year business loan too long?
Not if the asset lasts that long and income is stable, but it is long enough for rates, rent and competition to change. At $3,000 a month, the business commits $540,000 over 15 years, so the plan should include a review point, for example after 5 years, when refinancing or early repayment can be reconsidered.

Why does the supportable loan grow more slowly after 15 years?
Because later payments are mostly interest at first and the loan is repaid slowly. Moving from 10 to 15 years raises the supportable loan by about $80,184, while the total outflow rises by $180,000. Each extra 5 years buys less additional loan and costs more in total payments.

What if the interest rate rises during a 15-year loan?
This calculator uses only income and safe share, so it does not show rate risk. On a variable-rate loan, a higher rate raises the EMI for the same loan, which could push the payment above the $3,000 safe level. A fixed rate, or a buffer above $3,000 in affordable income, reduces that risk.
