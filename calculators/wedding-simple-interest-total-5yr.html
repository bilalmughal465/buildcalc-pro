Wedding Simple Interest Total Calculator (5 Years)

Calculate simple interest and the total amount to repay. This wedding version uses a 5-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Simple interest equals principal times rate times time, divided by 100.

How it works

Interest is charged only on the original principal, not on interest already added.

Worked example

Principal $10,000, annual rate 5%, term 5 years. Simple interest is $10,000 times 5 times 5, divided by 100, which is $2,500. The total to repay is $10,000 plus $2,500, which is $12,500. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults. The principal and rate are the same on every page in this group; only the time input changes, so the interest grows by $500 for every extra year.

Who this term suits for a wedding: a couple borrowing or saving for a wedding about 5 years away, where the venue, photographer and dress costs are already roughly known and a short, visible loan keeps the total cost in check.

FAQs

What is simple interest?
Simple interest is interest charged only on the original amount, not on accumulated interest.

Is this wedding calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is $2,500 interest on $10,000 over 5 years a fair wedding loan cost?
It is $500 a year for 5 years, which is 25 percent of the amount borrowed in total. Whether it is fair depends on what the loan pays for: a one-day wedding that is over in a day is a high cost to carry for 5 years, while a loan that also covers a home deposit shared with the wedding is easier to justify.

What wedding purchase fits a 5-year simple interest plan?
A single large wedding cost with a clear end date fits best, such as a venue booking, a photographer package, or a dress bought on credit. The plan works when the couple can pay the $500 a year of interest comfortably and clear the $10,000 principal by the wedding date.

Why does simple interest stay $500 a year on this loan?
Simple interest is charged only on the original $10,000, never on interest already added, so each year adds the same $10,000 times 5 divided by 100, which is $500. That is why 5 years gives $2,500 and not more.
