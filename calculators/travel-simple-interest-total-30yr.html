Travel Simple Interest Total Calculator (30 Years)

Calculate simple interest and the total amount to repay. This travel version uses a 30-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The Interest figure is the total cost of borrowing over 30 years, and the Total figure is the principal plus that Interest, which is the full amount to repay by the end of the term.

Formula

Simple interest equals principal times rate times time, divided by 100.

How it works

Interest is charged only on the original principal, not on interest already added.

Worked example

Principal $10,000, annual rate 5 percent, time 30 years. Simple interest is charged only on the original principal, so each year adds 5 percent of $10,000, which is $500. Over 30 years the interest is 30 times $500, which is $15,000. The total amount to repay is the principal plus the interest, $10,000 plus $15,000, which is $25,000. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults, and it follows directly from the page formula: principal times rate times time, divided by 100.

Who this term suits for a traveller: almost nobody as an ordinary travel plan, because 30 years is a mortgage-length commitment and the interest reaches one and a half times the amount borrowed; it belongs beside a holiday-home mortgage comparison, not beside a holiday loan.

FAQs

What is simple interest?
Simple interest is interest charged only on the original amount, not on accumulated interest.

Is this travel calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is a 30-year travel loan the same as a holiday-home mortgage?
It is the same length as many mortgages, but a mortgage usually buys a home that may hold value, while a travel loan buys trips and gear that do not. Over 30 years the interest here is $15,000 and the total repaid is $25,000, so the comparison should be with the home's value, not with the trip's memories.

What happens if travel stops for several years in a 30-year plan?
The interest does not stop. Simple interest keeps adding $500 a year whether the traveller travels or not, so a 5-year break still adds $2,500. A 30-year plan should therefore include a buffer for years when travel is paused, or the debt will outlast the travelling.

Should a traveller borrow the full $10,000 for 30 years on simple interest?
Borrowing less, or saving part of the price first, cuts the interest directly: every $1,000 less borrowed saves $50 a year, which is $1,500 over 30 years. A smaller loan, or a shorter term, is usually the better travel decision than a full 30-year commitment.
