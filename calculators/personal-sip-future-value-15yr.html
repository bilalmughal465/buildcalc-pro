Personal SIP Future Value Calculator (15 Years)

Estimate the future value of a regular investment plan. This personal version uses a 15-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The Future value is the estimated total pot at the end of 15 years from regular monthly investments plus growth, so it gives a planning figure for a medium-term goal rather than a promised return.

Formula

Future value uses the monthly rate and number of monthly investments to grow each investment with interest.

How it works

Regular investing smooths timing and lets compounding build the value over time.

Worked example

Monthly investment $300, annual rate 8%, term 15 years. First, the monthly rate is 8% divided by 12, which is 0.0066667, and the number of monthly investments is 15 times 12, which is 180. Using the future value formula, the future value is $300 times ((1.0066667 to the power of 180) minus 1) divided by 0.0066667, which is $103,811.47. Over 180 investments you pay in $54,000.00, and interest adds $49,811.47, which is 48.0% of the future value. Interest is now almost as large as everything you paid in, which is the point where regular investing starts to do real work on its own.

Who this term suits for a person: a person planning a goal about 15 years away, for example a university fund that must be ready when a child finishes school, a house deposit saved alongside a mortgage-free plan, or a career-break fund built slowly while working.

FAQs

What is a SIP?
A SIP is a regular investment of a fixed amount, usually monthly, into a fund or plan.

Is this personal calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

How does a 15-year term balance the monthly amount and the interest?
At 15 years the two sides are almost equal: you pay in $54,000.00 and interest adds $49,811.47, for $103,811.47 in total. That balance is why 15 years is a useful horizon for a university fund or a house goal, long enough for compounding to matter, short enough to plan around.

What if the rate falls below 8% over 15 years?
The result falls quickly, because 15 years of compounding magnifies any rate change. At 6% the same $300 for 15 years would give about $87,246 instead of $103,811.47, so never plan spending on the 8% figure alone, and review the rate every few years.

Can I use a 15-year SIP for a house deposit at year 15?
Yes, if the $300 can be kept going and the deposit target is reviewed as house prices move. A 15-year SIP is a savings plan, not a house price guarantee, so the deposit you need in 15 years may be larger than the deposit you need today.
