Car SIP Future Value Calculator (20 Years)

Estimate the future value of a regular investment plan. This car version uses a 20-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The Future value is the total amount your regular monthly investments are expected to grow to by the end of 20 years at the rate you entered, so you can compare it with the price of the car you plan to buy.

Formula

Future value uses the monthly rate and number of monthly investments to grow each investment with interest.

How it works

Regular investing smooths timing and lets compounding build the value over time.

Worked example

Monthly investment $300, annual rate 8%, term 20 years. First, the monthly rate is 8% divided by 12, which is 0.0066667, and the number of monthly investments is 20 times 12, which is 240. Using the future value formula, the future value is $300 times ((1.0066667 to the power of 240) minus 1) divided by 0.0066667, which is $176,706.12. Over 240 investments you pay in $72,000.00, and interest adds $104,706.12, which is 59.3% of the future value. At 20 years interest has passed the total deposits, so most of the fund is now growth, not money you paid in.

Who this term suits for a car owner: a driver planning for two or more car replacement cycles, or for a long-held classic car, for example building a fund that can cover a major restoration, a garage, or several replacement cars over a working life, rather than a single purchase.

FAQs

What is a SIP?
A SIP is a regular investment of a fixed amount, usually monthly, into a fund or plan.

Is this car calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does interest pass $100,000 at 20 years?
Because the earliest payments have had 20 years to compound, and each year the interest itself earns more interest. Once that snowball is rolling, the interest added each year is larger than the $3,600 paid in that year.

Is 20 years too long to forecast for a car purchase?
For one specific car, yes: nobody knows what cars will cost, or what type of car they will want, in 20 years. The 20-year figure is more useful as a lifetime driving fund, or a restoration fund, than as the price of one car.

Should car saving come before clearing a car loan?
Usually not: a car loan often charges more than 8%, so paying it off first saves more than this fund earns. Once expensive car debt is cleared, the same $300 a month builds this fund much faster in effect.
