Home Present Value Calculator (5 Years)

Find how much a future amount is worth today. This home version uses a 5-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The Present value result shows what a future home sum is worth in today's money after 5 years, so a smaller present value means the future sum has been discounted over time and should be planned from the lower today's-money figure, not the full future price.

Formula

Present value equals future amount divided by one plus rate to the power of years.

How it works

A future amount is worth less today because money today can earn interest.

Worked example

Future amount $50,000, annual rate 5%, term 5 years. First, one plus the rate is 1.05, and 1.05 to the power of 5 is about 1.27628. Using the page formula, present value is $50,000 divided by 1.27628, which is $39,176.31. That is the calculator's own output for the page defaults. The discount is $50,000 minus $39,176.31, which is $10,823.69, so waiting 5 years removes $10,823.69 of value in today's money at 5% a year. For a home buyer this is a near-term check: a deposit, a roof replacement, or a kitchen refit due in about 5 years should be judged in today's money now, before house prices or builder quotes move.

Who this term suits for a home buyer: a first-time buyer building a deposit over the next few years, or a homeowner planning a roof replacement or kitchen refit in about 5 years, checking what today's money must be set aside before the next few pay rises or price rises, rather than waiting until the quote arrives.

FAQs

What is present value?
Present value is what a future sum is worth today after allowing for interest over time.

Is this home calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

What does $39,176.31 mean for a home cost 5 years away?
It means a $50,000 home cost in 5 years is worth $39,176.31 in today's money at 5% a year, so setting aside $39,176.31 now, if it grew at 5% a year, would reach $50,000 in 5 years.

Why is the discount only $10,823.69 over 5 years?
Because discounting has had only 5 years to work, so the gap between $50,000 in the future and $39,176.31 today is still fairly small; the gap grows much faster over longer terms.

Should a home buyer use the 5-year figure for a deposit?
Yes, as a planning guide: it shows how much a future deposit target is worth today, but the buyer should still check the actual deposit a lender will ask for, since that can change.
