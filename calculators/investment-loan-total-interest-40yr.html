Investment Loan Total Interest Calculator (40 Years)

Estimate total interest paid over a loan. This investment version uses a 40-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Your result means this: Total interest is the extra cost of borrowing over the full 40 years, on top of the loan amount itself, while Total paid is the loan amount plus that interest, so it is the full amount you will have handed over by the end of the loan.

Formula

Total interest equals monthly EMI times number of payments minus the principal.

How it works

A longer term lowers the monthly payment but raises total interest.

Worked example

Loan amount $200,000, annual rate 6.5%, term 40 years. The number of monthly payments is 40 times 12, which is 480 payments. Using the page formula, the monthly EMI is $1,170.91, total paid is $1,170.91 times 480, which is $562,038.54, and total interest is $562,038.54 minus $200,000, which is $362,038.54. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults. Note how the monthly payment falls as the term lengthens, while total interest rises every time: a longer loan is cheaper each month but more expensive overall.

Who this term suits for an investor: nobody as an ordinary investment plan, because a 40-year horizon is a lifetime warning: $1,170.91 a month looks small, yet $362,038.54 of interest is nearly twice the amount borrowed, and the loan can still be running at retirement age.

FAQs

Why does a longer loan cost more interest?
More payments mean interest is charged for longer, so total interest rises even if each payment is smaller.

Is this investment calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is $362,038.54 of interest over 40 years ever worth it for an investor?
Only if decades of rent and growth far exceed that cost, which cannot be known in advance, so it should never be assumed.

Why is the 40-year payment only $1,170.91 but the interest so large?
Because 480 months of interest on a slowly falling balance adds up to $362,038.54, even at the same 6.5 percent rate as the 5-year loan.

What should an investor check before trusting a 40-year loan figure?
That the plan survives rate rises, empty properties, major repairs and a possible sale, and that it does not depend on 40 years of uninterrupted rent.
