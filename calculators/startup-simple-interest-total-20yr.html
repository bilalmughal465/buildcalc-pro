Startup Simple Interest Total Calculator (20 Years)

Calculate simple interest and the total amount to repay. This startup version uses a 20-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The Interest figure is the extra cost of borrowing over 20 years, and the Total is the principal plus that Interest, so the Total is the full amount to repay.

Formula

Simple interest equals principal times rate times time, divided by 100.

How it works

Interest is charged only on the original principal, not on interest already added.

Worked example

Principal $10,000, annual rate 5 percent, term 20 years. Using the page formula, simple interest equals $10,000 times 5 times 20, divided by 100, which is $10,000.00. The total to repay is $10,000 plus $10,000.00, which is $20,000.00. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults.

This is the crossing point: at $500 a year, 20 years of interest is $10,000, exactly the amount borrowed, so the total to repay doubles to $20,000.

Who this term suits for a startup founder: a startup buying its own premises or making a very long investment, where the purchase must keep earning for 20 years to justify a total that is twice the amount borrowed.

FAQs

What is simple interest?
Simple interest is interest charged only on the original amount, not on accumulated interest.

Is this startup calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does interest equal the principal at 20 years?
Because $500 a year times 20 is $10,000, exactly the amount borrowed, so the total to repay is $20,000. This is the point where the loan costs as much as the money itself.

Is a 20-year startup loan the same as a premises mortgage?
It is similar in length, but different in risk. A mortgage is secured on property that may hold its value, while a startup loan for stock or fittings is not, so the risk of a 20-year term is higher.

What if the rate is 4 percent over 20 years?
At 4 percent, the yearly interest is $400, so 20 years of interest is $8,000 and the total is $18,000, which is $2,000 less than at 5 percent.
