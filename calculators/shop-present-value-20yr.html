Shop Present Value Calculator (20 Years)

Find how much a future amount is worth today. This shop version uses a 20-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The Present value figure is what the future amount entered is worth in today's money at the annual rate entered over 20 years, so it is lower than the future amount because money received later is worth less today once the wait and the interest it could have earned are taken into account.

Formula

Present value equals future amount divided by one plus rate to the power of years.

How it works

A future amount is worth less today because money today can earn interest.

Worked example

Future amount $50,000, annual rate 5%, term 20 years. Using the page formula, present value equals $50,000 divided by 1.05 to the power of 20, which is $18,844.47. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults. The discount is $50,000 minus $18,844.47, which is $31,155.53, so time and interest remove $31,155.53 of value between today and year 20.

Who this term suits for a shop owner: a shop owner thinking about the next generation of the business, such as handing the shop to family, buying out a partner, or funding a retirement pot from the shop, where a lump sum will be needed in about twenty years.

FAQs

What is present value?
Present value is what a future sum is worth today after allowing for interest over time.

Is this shop calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why is $50,000 in 20 years worth less than $19,000 today?
Because twenty years of discounting at 5% a year removes $31,155.53 of value, leaving a present value of $18,844.47, which is what must be set aside today.

Should a 20-year shop plan be judged on present value or future value?
On present value, because it shows what must be found today, while the future value only shows the headline cost in twenty years' money.

What happens to this figure if rates rise to 7%?
The present value falls further, because a higher rate discounts the future sum more heavily, so less money needs to be set aside today to reach the same $50,000.
