Personal Retirement Corpus Calculator (10 Years)

Estimate a retirement corpus from monthly savings. This personal version uses a 10-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Corpus uses the monthly rate and number of monthly savings to grow each saving with interest.

How it works

Starting earlier matters more than saving a little more later, because interest has longer to compound.

Worked example

Monthly saving $500, annual rate 7%, term 10 years. First, the monthly rate is 7% divided by 12, which is 0.0058333, and the number of payments is 10 times 12, which is 120. Using the corpus formula, the future value is $500 times (2.009661 minus 1) divided by 0.0058333, which is $86,542.40. Over 120 payments the person deposits $60,000.00 in total, and interest adds $26,542.40 to reach the corpus. That interest is 30.7% of the corpus, almost a third of the final fund, because the balance has had a full decade to compound.

Who this term suits for a person: a person in their early 50s who still has a decade before retirement, or a worker in their 40s who wants a separate retirement pot alongside a workplace pension. For example, someone who paid off a mortgage or finished supporting children could redirect $500 a month for 10 years and build $86,542.40 as extra retirement income. The trade-off is clear: 10 years is long enough for interest to matter, but the corpus still depends on keeping the $500 going every month, including through slow years.

FAQs

When should I start saving for retirement?
As early as possible. Time in the market allows compounding to do more of the work.

Is this personal calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does interest jump to $26,542.40 at 10 years, more than four times the 5-year interest?
Because compounding accelerates. In the second 5 years the balance is already larger, so each month's interest is earned on a bigger sum, and that interest then earns interest itself. The first 5 years add $5,796.45 of interest, while the next 5 years add about $20,745.95 more, which is why starting earlier matters more than saving a little more later.

Is 10 years too late to start retirement saving?
No, it is not too late, but the gap must be faced honestly. $500 a month for 10 years gives $86,542.40 at a fixed 7%, which is a useful pot, yet it may still fall short of a full retirement income. Check pension income, living costs, and whether a higher monthly saving is possible, rather than assuming 10 years alone will be enough.

Should I raise $500 a month as my pay rises over 10 years?
Yes, raising it early helps most. Even a small rise in the first years compounds for the rest of the term, while the same rise in the final year adds almost nothing extra. If pay rises, raise the saving first and let spending rise second, and keep the increase automatic so it is not spent by habit.
