Education Effective Annual Rate Calculator (5 Years)

Convert a nominal annual rate to an effective annual rate. This education version uses a 5-year planning term as its starting point.

—

This effective annual rate is the true yearly rate after allowing for compounding during the year, so it is the figure to compare when two offers show the same nominal rate but compound at different frequencies.

Formula

Effective rate equals one plus nominal rate divided by periods, to the power of periods, minus one.

How it works

More frequent compounding raises the effective rate slightly above the nominal rate.

Worked example

Nominal rate 6%, compounding 12 times a year, term 5 years. The monthly factor is 1 plus 0.06 divided by 12, which is 1.005. Using the page formula, the effective annual rate is 1.005 to the power of 12, minus 1, which is 6.17% a year, not 6.00%. That extra 0.17% is the effect of interest earning interest within the year. Left alone, $10,000 in an education fund grows to $13,488.50 in 5 years at this monthly compounding, compared with $13,382.26 if interest were added only once a year at 6%, a difference of $106.24. Over a short horizon the gap looks small, which is exactly why it is easy to ignore when comparing student loan offers.

Who this term suits for an education saver: a family saving for a degree that starts in about 5 years, for example parents of a teenager in secondary school, who are comparing two education loans with the same 6% headline rate but different compounding, and want to know which one truly costs more before signing.

FAQs

What is an effective rate?
The effective rate is the true yearly rate after allowing for compounding during the year.

Is this education calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why is the effective rate 6.17% when the loan says 6%?
Because the 6% is the nominal rate before compounding. With interest added 12 times a year, each month's interest earns interest in the months that follow, so the true yearly rate is 6.17%, not 6.00%.

Does monthly compounding matter much over only 5 years?
It adds $106.24 on $10,000 over 5 years, $13,488.50 against $13,382.26 at annual compounding. That is small on one loan, but it is real money, and it is the reason two loans with the same 6% headline rate can cost different amounts.

Should a family compare education loans by nominal rate or effective rate?
By effective rate, because it is the true yearly cost after compounding. A loan that compounds monthly at 6% nominal costs 6.17% a year, while a loan that compounds yearly at 6% nominal costs 6.00% a year.
