Car Credit Card Monthly Interest Calculator (40 Years)

Estimate monthly interest on a card balance. This car version uses a 40-year planning term as its starting point.

—

In plain terms, this monthly interest amount is the interest charged for one month on the balance entered at the annual rate entered, before any payment toward the balance itself.

Formula

Monthly interest equals balance times annual rate divided by 100 divided by 12.

How it works

Paying only interest leaves the balance unchanged. Paying more than interest reduces the balance.

Worked example

Balance $5,000, annual rate 18%, term 40 years. Monthly interest is $5,000 times 18, divided by 100, divided by 12, which is $75.00 a month. That is the calculator's own output, and it is the same every month because the balance and rate do not change. If the car owner pays only interest, after 40 years they have paid $36,000 in interest, which is 720% of the balance, and they still owe the original $5,000. Paying more than $75.00 in any month is what finally reduces that balance.

Who this term suits for a car owner: no car owner as a real plan, and that is the point of this page: over a full 40-year working life, interest-only card payments cost $36,000, 720% of the balance, and the car, and probably several cars after it, are long gone while the debt remains.

FAQs

Why is card interest high?
Card rates are usually higher than loan rates, so balances grow quickly if only minimum payments are made.

Is this car calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is $36,000 of interest on $5,000 over 40 years realistic, or just a warning example?
It is the exact maths of $75.00 a month for 480 months, but as a real life it is a warning example, not a plan. No car lasts 40 years of card interest, and no owner should plan to pay interest for a working lifetime without ever reducing the balance.

What would $75 a month add up to over 40 years if it were saved for a car instead?
Saved with no interest at all, $75.00 a month for 480 months is $36,000, the same amount that interest-only payments waste over 40 years. Put towards a car deposit or a repair fund, that $36,000 buys several cars outright, instead of buying nothing and leaving a $5,000 balance still owed.

What is the first step for a car owner who has carried card debt for decades?
Stop adding car spending to the card, then set a monthly payment above $75.00 so the balance actually falls, and get a fixed payoff date. If the balance is large or the rate is high, a qualified adviser or a debt charity can help work out a cheaper, dated plan before any new car is bought.
