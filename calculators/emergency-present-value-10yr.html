Emergency Present Value Calculator (10 Years)

Find how much a future amount is worth today. This emergency version uses a 10-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The "Present value" figure is the amount worth setting aside today so that, at the annual rate and number of years you entered, it grows to the future amount you need for an emergency.

Formula

Present value equals future amount divided by one plus rate to the power of years.

How it works

A future amount is worth less today because money today can earn interest.

Worked example

A household wants to have $50,000 ready for a serious emergency in 10 years, at 5% a year. The divisor is 1.05 to the power of 10, which is 1.628894627. Dividing $50,000 by 1.628894627 gives a present value of $30,695.66. The discount is $50,000 minus $30,695.66, which is $19,304.34, or 38.6% of the future amount. The fall from the 5-year figure is $39,176.31 minus $30,695.66, which is $8,480.65, the largest 5-year dollar drop in this group, because the base is still large. For an emergency fund this is the middle-horizon warning: ten years of discounting already removes well over a third of the future sum.

Who this term suits for an emergency planner: a young family building an emergency fund before taking on a mortgage, for example parents in their late twenties who want a $50,000 safety net in place within ten years, while still young enough that a job loss or a serious illness would hit hardest.

FAQs

What is present value?
Present value is what a future sum is worth today after allowing for interest over time.

Is this emergency calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does 10 years cut the present value by $8,480.65 from the 5-year figure?
Because each extra five years discounts an already discounted sum, and the sum is still large at year 5. From $39,176.31, another five years at 5% divides by a further 1.2762815625, giving $30,695.66. Later 5-year steps remove fewer dollars, because the base they act on is smaller.

Is $30,695.66 enough if emergency costs rise faster than 5% a year?
No, not on its own. If medical bills, repair costs or living costs rise faster than 5% a year, the real emergency in 10 years will cost more than $50,000, so the lump sum needed today is higher than $30,695.66. This page holds the future amount fixed at $50,000; a household facing fast-rising costs should redo the calculation with a higher future amount.

Should a 10-year emergency plan rely on a fixed 5% rate?
No. A fixed 5% rate for ten years is a planning assumption, not a promise. Rates, jobs and health all change over a decade. The honest use of this figure is as a starting target to review every year or two, not as a sum to set aside once and forget.
