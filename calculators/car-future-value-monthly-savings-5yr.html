Car Future Value Monthly Savings Calculator (5 Years)

Estimate the future value of regular monthly savings. This car version uses a 5-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The Future value is the total your regular monthly deposits could grow to after 5 years, including the interest earned on those deposits, based on the numbers you entered.

Formula

Future value uses the monthly rate and the number of monthly deposits to grow each deposit with interest.

How it works

Each monthly deposit earns interest from the month it is made until the end of the term.

Worked example

A car buyer sets aside $500 every month for 5 years at 6% a year. The monthly rate is 6% divided by 12, which is 0.5%, and there are 60 monthly deposits. Using the page formula, the future value is $500 times ((1.005 to the power of 60 minus 1) divided by 0.005), which is $34,885.02. The buyer paid in $30,000.00, so interest added $4,885.02, which is 14.0% of the final value.

Most of the result after only a few years is still the deposits themselves, because compounding has had little time to work, but as the years pass interest becomes a larger and larger share of the pot. That is why the later years add far more than the early years, even though the monthly deposit never changes. These figures assume a fixed 6% rate, with no fees, taxes, gaps, or changing rates, and they do not include car price rises, insurance, fuel, servicing, or registration, so they are planning estimates, not promises.

Who this term suits for a car buyer: a first-time buyer saving for a first car or a solid deposit while still using public transport, a family car-share, or an older car that still runs — for example a young driver putting money aside each month so the deposit is ready when they pass their test, or a commuter saving for a reliable used car to replace bus fares and lifts.

FAQs

How does monthly saving grow?
Regular deposits grow because each deposit earns interest, and that interest then earns more interest.

Is this car calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is $34,885.02 enough to buy a car after 5 years?
It depends on the car. $34,885.02 is a useful deposit for a new car, or it can cover a good used car outright in many cases, but the price of the car itself is only part of the cost. Tax, registration, insurance, and the first service are not included in this calculator, so check those before you treat the whole figure as a car budget.

Why is interest only $4,885.02 after 5 years?
Because compounding has had little time to work. After only 60 deposits, most of the $34,885.02 is your own money, $30,000.00 of deposits, and interest has added just $4,885.02, which is 14.0% of the final value. Early deposits earn interest for the full 5 years, but later deposits earn it for only a few months.

What happens if I stop saving after 3 years instead of 5?
You keep what you have saved plus the interest it has earned, but you lose the last two years of deposits and the compounding on them. Saving $500 a month for 3 years at the same rate gives a much smaller pot than $34,885.02, and you would need a larger deposit from elsewhere, a loan, or a cheaper car. The fund does not disappear, it simply stops growing from new deposits.
