Education SIP Future Value Calculator (25 Years)

Estimate the future value of a regular investment plan. This education version uses a 25-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The Future value figure is the estimated total built up from your regular monthly payments plus the interest earned on them over 25 years.

Formula

Future value uses the monthly rate and number of monthly investments to grow each investment with interest.

How it works

Regular investing smooths timing and lets compounding build the value over time.

Worked example

A family saves $300 every month for 25 years at 8% a year for a child's education. The monthly rate is 8% divided by 12, which is 0.0066667, and the number of monthly investments is 25 times 12, which is 300. Using the future value formula, the future value is $300 times ((1.0066667 to the power of 300) minus 1) divided by 0.0066667, which is $285,307.92. Over 300 investments the family pays in $90,000.00, and interest adds $195,307.92, which is 68.5% of the future value. At 25 years interest is more than double the deposits, so the fund is mostly growth. For education planning this is a fund for a family, not one degree: it could cover degrees for several children, or a degree plus a house deposit, but only if saving never stops for 25 years.

Who this term suits for an education saver: a family or grandparent saving for several children, or for a child and later grandchildren, who wants one long fund rather than a separate short fund for each child.

FAQs

What is a SIP?
A SIP is a regular investment of a fixed amount, usually monthly, into a fund or plan.

Is this education calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does interest pass deposits by so much at 25 years?
Because 25 years of compounding turns the early $300 payments into many times their value, while later payments add little interest, so the total is dominated by growth on the oldest money.

How do I keep saving $300 for 25 years through school changes?
By treating it as a fixed monthly bill and reviewing it only when income or family circumstances change, rather than stopping and restarting it around each school move.

Should the $300 goal rise with education price rises over 25 years?
Yes, because university fees and living costs will rise over 25 years, so a fixed $300 buys less education at the end than at the start.
