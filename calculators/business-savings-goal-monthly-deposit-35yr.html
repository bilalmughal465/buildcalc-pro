Business Savings Goal Monthly Deposit Calculator (35 Years)

Find the monthly deposit needed to reach a savings goal. This business version uses a 35-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Monthly deposit equals goal times monthly rate divided by one plus monthly rate to the power of months minus one.

How it works

Saving a fixed amount each month with interest reaches the goal faster than saving without interest.

Worked example

Goal $50,000, annual rate 5%, term 35 years. The monthly rate is 5% divided by 12, which is 0.0041667, and the number of payments is 35 times 12, which is 420. Using the monthly deposit formula, the monthly deposit is $44.01. Over 420 payments the business deposits $18,484.41 in total, and interest adds $31,515.59 to reach the $50,000 goal. Interest now covers 63.0% of the goal, so growth contributes far more than the deposits themselves.

Who this term suits for a business: A 35-year term suits only a business with a very long horizon, for example a family firm building a handover reserve for the next generation, where the fund is a habit rather than a purchase plan. The monthly amount is almost trivial, but the business must accept that the goal, the owners, and the market will all change several times before the fund matures.

FAQs

How much should I save each month?
Divide the goal by the growth of regular deposits at the assumed rate to get the monthly amount.

Is this business calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does the deposit fall only $16.07 from 30 years to 35 years?
Because the monthly amount is already small at $60.08, and extra years add compounding rather than cutting the payment much. From 30 to 35 years the deposit falls from $60.08 to $44.01, a gain of only $16.07 a month, while the wait grows by 5 years, so the extra time buys very little monthly relief.

Is a 35-year business savings plan realistic with ownership changes?
Only if the plan is owned by the business, not by one person. Ownership changes, partnerships ending, and premises moves all threaten a 35-year plan, so the fund should be written into the business accounts and handed over with the business, not kept as a personal habit.

How often should a business review a 35-year savings plan?
At least every year, and properly every 5 years. Rates, fees, tax rules, and the real cost of the goal will all change over 35 years, and a plan that is never reviewed will drift away from the goal it was meant to reach.
