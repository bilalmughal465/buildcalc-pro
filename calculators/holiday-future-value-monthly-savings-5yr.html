Holiday Future Value Monthly Savings Calculator (5 Years)

Estimate the future value of regular monthly savings. This holiday version uses a 5-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Future value uses the monthly rate and the number of monthly deposits to grow each deposit with interest.

How it works

Each monthly deposit earns interest from the month it is made until the end of the term.

Worked example

Monthly deposit $500, annual rate 6%, term 5 years. The monthly rate is 6% divided by 12, which is 0.5%, and the number of deposits is 5 times 12, which is 60 months. Using the page formula, the future value is $500 times 1.005 to the power of 60, minus one, divided by 0.005. Since 1.005 to the power of 60 is about 1.34885, the multiplier is about 69.7700, so the future value is $500 times 69.7700, which is $34,885.02. That is the calculator's own output for these defaults.

You will have paid in $30,000 over the term, so interest adds $4,885.02. After only 5 years most of the fund is still your own deposits, because compounding has had little time to work. That is normal for a short holiday fund: the saving habit matters more than the interest in the early years.

Who this term suits for a holiday saver: a family saving for a first big holiday in about 5 years, such as a honeymoon, a bucket-list trip, or a first long overseas holiday, where the money must be ready by a fairly fixed date and a poor year must not be allowed to break the plan.

FAQs

How does monthly saving grow?
Regular deposits grow because each deposit earns interest, and that interest then earns more interest.

Is this holiday calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is $34,885.02 enough for a big holiday in 5 years?
It depends on the trip. $34,885.02 is the fund after 60 deposits of $500 plus $4,885.02 of interest, so it suits one large holiday or several smaller ones, but a luxury long trip could cost more, and travel prices will have risen by then. Treat it as a planning target, not a price promise.

Why is the interest only $4,885.02 over 5 years?
Because compounding needs time. Over 60 months the balance is still building, so most of the $34,885.02 is the $30,000 you paid in yourself. The interest share grows sharply in later years, which is why longer terms look so different on this page.

What if a holiday comes up before the 5 years are over?
Taking money out early cuts both the deposits and the interest they would have earned, so the final fund falls by more than the amount withdrawn. For trips sooner than 5 years, keep a separate short-term holiday pot and leave this fund alone.
