Education Break Even Units Calculator (5 Years)

Find how many units must be sold to break even. This education version uses a 5-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Break even units equal fixed costs divided by price minus variable cost per unit.

How it works

At break even, total revenue exactly covers total costs, with no profit and no loss.

Worked example

Fixed costs $10,000, price per unit $50, variable cost per unit $30, term 5 years. First, the contribution per unit is $50 minus $30, which is $20. Using the page formula, break even units are $10,000 divided by $20, which is 500 units. At 500 units, revenue is 500 times $50, which is $25,000, and total cost is $10,000 of fixed costs plus 500 times $30 of variable costs, which is $10,000 plus $15,000, also $25,000, so profit is zero. The term in years does not change this answer, because the formula uses only fixed costs, price and variable cost. As a pacing illustration only, not the output of this page, spreading 500 units evenly over 5 years is 100 units a year, or about 8.33 units a month. That illustration only makes sense if the $10,000 is a one-off cost: if rent, tutor pay or licence fees repeat every year, break even must be reached fresh each year, not once in 5 years.

Who this term suits for an education provider: a new tutoring centre or short-course provider in its first 5 years, for example a centre selling 500 course seats or workbooks to cover a $10,000 setup cost for rooms, materials and advertising, before it can count any intake as profit.

FAQs

What is break even?
Break even is the point where revenue covers all costs, so profit is zero.

Is this education calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is 500 units a lot for a new education provider in 5 years?
It depends on the unit. 500 course seats over 5 years is 100 a year, which a small centre with a few classes a term can reach; 500 expensive textbooks in the same time is a much harder target, so always name the unit before judging the number.

Why does the 5-year page give the same 500 units as the longer-term pages?
Because the term is not an input to this formula. Fixed costs of $10,000, a $50 price and a $30 variable cost give 500 units whatever the planning term is; the term only changes how you pace those 500 units, not the break-even point itself.

What should a 5-year education provider check after break even?
Whether the 500 units were reached before the course or term ended. A centre that breaks even in year 4 of a 5-year plan has only one year of profit-making intakes left, while one that breaks even in year 1 has four, so the timing matters as much as the total.
