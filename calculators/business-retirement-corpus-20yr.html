Business Retirement Corpus Calculator (20 Years)

Estimate a retirement corpus from monthly savings. This business version uses a 20-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Corpus uses the monthly rate and number of monthly savings to grow each saving with interest.

How it works

Starting earlier matters more than saving a little more later, because interest has longer to compound.

Worked example

A business owner sets aside $500 every month for 20 years at 7% a year. The monthly rate is 7% divided by 12, which is 0.0058333, and there are 240 monthly savings. Using the page formula, the corpus is $500 times ((1.0058333 to the power of 240 minus 1) divided by 0.0058333), which is $260,463.33. The owner paid in $120,000, so interest added $140,463.33, which is 53.9% of the corpus. At 20 years interest has passed the total paid in: compounding, not the monthly saving, is now the bigger contributor.

Who this term suits for a business: an owner planning a full handover — for example a family firm owner who wants a personal pot ready when a son, daughter, or junior partner takes over, so the handover price does not have to fund the whole retirement.

FAQs

When should I start saving for retirement?
As early as possible. Time in the market allows compounding to do more of the work.

Is this business calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does interest pass the deposits at 20 years?
Because each year's interest earns interest in later years. After 20 years the pot is $260,463.33, of which $140,463.33 is interest and only $120,000 is deposits. That crossover is the clearest sign that starting early beats saving harder later.

Is 20 years too long to forecast a business owner's retirement pot?
As an exact forecast, yes — rates, tax rules, and the business itself will change. As a planning guide, no: the 20-year figure shows the scale of pot a steady $500 a month can build, and it should be reviewed every few years against real returns.

Should a business owner stop at 20 years, or keep saving?
That depends on the retirement age and other income. Many owners keep a smaller monthly sum going after 20 years, because the pot is now large enough that even a few more years of compounding add a great deal. Stopping early gives up the most powerful years.
