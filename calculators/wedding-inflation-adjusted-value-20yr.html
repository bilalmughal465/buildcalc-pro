Wedding Inflation Adjusted Value Calculator (20 Years)

Estimate what an amount will be worth after inflation. This wedding version uses a 20-year planning term as its starting point.

—

This Future amount is the price of the same wedding after 20 years of inflation, so it is the savings target to aim for rather than the cost seen today.

Formula

Future amount equals amount times one plus inflation rate to the power of years.

How it works

Inflation means the same amount buys less over time, so future prices are higher.

Worked example

Amount $10,000, inflation rate 3 percent, term 20 years. Using the page formula, the future amount is $10,000 times 1.03 to the power of 20, which gives $18,061.11. That is the calculator output for these defaults. The increase is $8,061.11, which is 80.61 percent more than $10,000, so a wedding that costs $10,000 at today's prices would need about $18,061.11 in 20 years if prices rise at 3 percent a year.

Who this term suits for a wedding: Parents planning a child's wedding from an early age, who need a long-range figure for what a wedding will cost when the child is grown, rather than today's prices.

FAQs

What does inflation do to money?
Inflation reduces what money can buy, so a sum today will need to be larger in future to buy the same things.

Is this wedding calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why is $18,061.11 needed after 20 years?
Because $10,000 times 1.03 to the power of 20 is $18,061.11: twenty years of 3 percent rises nearly doubles the price, adding $8,061.11.

Can parents really plan a wedding 20 years ahead on one inflation rate?
Only as a starting point: the rate will not stay at 3 percent for twenty years, so the fund should be recalculated whenever real wedding prices or savings returns change.

What does 20 years of inflation mean for a wedding fund?
It means today's prices are a poor guide on their own: a fund built on $10,000 today would fall about $8,061.11 short of the same wedding in 20 years at 3 percent a year.
