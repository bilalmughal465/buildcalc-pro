Emergency Future Value Monthly Savings Calculator (5 Years)

Estimate the future value of regular monthly savings. This emergency version uses a 5-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The future value is the total amount your regular monthly deposits are worth at the end of the 5-year term, including both the money paid in and the interest those deposits have earned.

Formula

Future value uses the monthly rate and the number of monthly deposits to grow each deposit with interest.

How it works

Each monthly deposit earns interest from the month it is made until the end of the term.

Worked example

A household saves $500 every month for 5 years at 6% a year for emergencies. The monthly rate is 6% divided by 12, which is 0.005, and there are 60 monthly deposits. Using the page formula, the future value is $500 times ((1.005 to the power of 60 minus 1) divided by 0.005), which is $34,885.02. The household paid in $30,000.00, so interest added $4,885.02, which is 14.0% of the future value. After only 5 years most of the result is still the saver's own deposits, because compounding has had little time to work. For emergency planning this is a short, practical cushion: enough to cover several months without pay, a major car repair, or a large medical bill, but not a long period out of work on its own.

Who this term suits for an emergency saver: a household building its first real emergency cushion, for example a family that wants several months of living costs set aside in case of a sudden job loss or a major car repair, without locking money away for decades.

FAQs

How does monthly saving grow?
Regular deposits grow because each deposit earns interest, and that interest then earns more interest.

Is this emergency calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is $34,885.02 enough for a real emergency in 5 years?
It covers several months without pay, a major car repair, or a large medical bill, but not a long period out of work on its own. A household with high rent or a mortgage should compare it with its own monthly bills, not with the headline figure alone.

Why is interest only $4,885.02 after 5 years?
Because compounding has had little time to work: most of the fund is still the saver's own $30,000 of deposits. Interest grows slowly at first and faster later, which is why the 10-year fund is more than double this one.

Should an emergency fund be kept separate from other savings?
Yes. If the same pot is also used for holidays, a car, or a deposit, it will not be there when the emergency arrives. Keeping it in a separate account, and only touching it for a genuine emergency, is what makes the figure real.
