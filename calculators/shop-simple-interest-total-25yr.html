Shop Simple Interest Total Calculator (25 Years)

Calculate simple interest and the total amount to repay. This shop version uses a 25-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Simple interest equals principal times rate times time, divided by 100.

How it works

Interest is charged only on the original principal, not on interest already added.

Worked example

Principal $10,000, annual rate 5 percent, term 25 years. Using the page formula, simple interest is $10,000 times 5 divided by 100 times 25, which is $12,500.00. The total to repay is $10,000 plus $12,500.00, which is $22,500.00. That is the calculator output for these defaults. Because time is an input on this page, each year page gives a genuinely different answer: interest grows by exactly $500 for every extra year, since 5 percent of $10,000 is $500 a year and simple interest never compounds. Interest passed the $10,000 borrowed at 20 years, and by 25 years it has grown to $12,500.00, so the total of $22,500.00 is well above double the original loan.

Who this term suits for a shop owner: a family shop planning across a generation, where the loan outlives any single shopfront or till system, so the owner should check the plan still fits if the shop changes hands.

FAQs

What is simple interest?
Simple interest is interest charged only on the original amount, not on accumulated interest.

Is this shop calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why is interest now more than the amount borrowed?
At $500 a year, 25 years gives $12,500 of interest, which is $2,500 more than the $10,000 borrowed, so the total to repay is $22,500. After the 20-year crossing point, every extra year adds interest on top of an already larger interest bill.

Should a shop plan 25 years ahead on one fixed rate?
No. Rates, rents, suppliers, and customers all change over 25 years, so a fixed 5 percent for the whole term is a planning assumption, not a promise. Review the loan whenever the shop's takings or costs change materially.

What if the shop repays after 12 years instead?
Using the same formula, 12 years of interest is $10,000 times 5 divided by 100 times 12, which is $6,000, so the total would be $16,000. That is a formula calculation for 12 years, not a page output.
