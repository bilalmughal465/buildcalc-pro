Car Loan EMI Calculator (15 Years)

Estimate the monthly EMI for a loan from amount, rate and term. This car version uses a 15-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

EMI equals principal times monthly rate times one plus monthly rate to the power of payments, divided by that power minus one.

How it works

This assumes a fixed rate and equal monthly payments with no extra fees.

Worked example

Loan amount $200,000, annual rate 6.5%, term 15 years. First, the monthly rate is 6.5% divided by 12, which is 0.0054167, and the number of payments is 15 times 12, which is 180. Using the EMI formula, the monthly payment is $1,742.21. Over 180 payments the total paid is $313,598.65, calculated from the unrounded EMI exactly as the calculator does. Total interest is $313,598.65 minus $200,000, which is $113,598.65. That is 56.8% of the amount borrowed, so interest alone is now more than half the car loan.

Who this term suits for a car buyer: A 15-year term suits almost no ordinary car buyer, because a normal car will be replaced long before 15 years pass. Its only honest car use is for a buyer who is certain to keep a very high-value, durable vehicle for the full term, for example a premium car kept as a long-term family vehicle with careful maintenance. For a normal daily car, 15 years means paying for a car that has already been sold or scrapped.

FAQs

What is an EMI?
An EMI is a fixed monthly payment that covers both interest and principal over the loan term.

Is this car calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is a 15-year car loan too long when cars lose value and wear out?
For an ordinary car, yes. A car bought today will usually be replaced, or need major work, well before 15 years, yet the $1,742.21 payment would continue. The buyer would also be paying $113,598.65 in interest for a vehicle that is worth only a fraction of $200,000 by then. A shorter term, a cheaper car or saving first should be compared.

Why is the interest $113,598.65 when the rate is only 6.5%?
Because 6.5% is charged every year on the balance that is still owed, and over 15 years that balance falls slowly at first. The rate looks small, but 180 monthly payments give interest a long time to build up, which is why the total interest passes half the loan amount.

What happens to a car loan if the car is sold or written off before 15 years?
The loan does not end with the car. If the car is sold, the remaining balance must be paid from the sale price or from savings, and if the sale price is lower, the buyer still owes the difference. If the car is written off, insurance may not cover the full remaining balance, so gap cover and the loan terms should be checked before signing.
