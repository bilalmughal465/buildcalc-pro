Education Down Payment Amount Calculator (15 Years)

Calculate a down payment from price and percentage. This education version uses a 15-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The result shows the cash amount to pay upfront as the down payment, and the loan needed shows the remaining amount to borrow after that payment, so a larger down payment means a smaller loan.

Formula

Down payment equals price times down payment percentage divided by 100.

How it works

A larger down payment usually means a smaller loan and lower monthly payments.

Worked example

Price $300,000, down payment 20 percent, term 15 years. Using the page formula, the down payment is $300,000 times 20, divided by 100, which is $60,000.00. The loan needed is $300,000 minus $60,000.00, which is $240,000.00. The term in years does not change this answer, because the formula uses only price and down payment percentage. As a saving illustration only, not the output of this page, building the $60,000.00 deposit in equal monthly savings over 15 years, with no interest, fees or price change counted, needs $333.33 a month, or about $4,000 a year, over 180 payments. That illustration counts no growth on savings and no rise in the price, so it is a scale check only, not a promise that $60,000.00 will still be enough when the home is bought.

Who this term suits for an education borrower: a family planning from the early school years, for example parents who start a deposit fund when their child is young and run it alongside an education fund, so that by the time university choices are made there is a real deposit available for a home near the chosen campus, rather than only rent money.

FAQs

How much down payment do I need?
It depends on the lender and the purchase. A common planning figure is 20 percent, but requirements vary.

Is this education calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is $333.33 a month enough if prices rise over 15 years?
Only if the price stays near $300,000. Over 15 years prices, fees and the family's own circumstances usually change, so the fund should be reviewed every few years and the monthly amount raised if the target home becomes more expensive.

Can the same fund serve as both a deposit fund and an education fund?
No, not honestly. A dollar spent on university fees cannot also be a dollar of house deposit. Families who try to use one pot for both goals usually end up short on one of them, so the two goals need separate targets.

What if the child studies abroad instead of near home?
Then a home bought near a local university may be the wrong purchase entirely. A 15-year deposit plan should stay as a flexible fund until the study destination is clearer, rather than being locked into one town early.
