Business Down Payment Amount Calculator (15 Years)

Calculate a down payment from price and percentage. This business version uses a 15-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Down payment equals price times down payment percentage divided by 100.

How it works

A larger down payment usually means a smaller loan and lower monthly payments.

Worked example

Price $300,000, down payment 20%. Down payment is $300,000 times 20, divided by 100, which is $60,000. The loan needed is $300,000 minus $60,000, which is $240,000. The 15-year label is the planning term only; the formula uses price and percentage, so the result is the same as the 5-year and 10-year pages.

Saving illustration only, not the output of this calculator: setting aside $60,000 over 15 years means about $333.33 a month for 180 months, with no interest counted, so the final month needs a small top-up to reach exactly $60,000.

Who this term suits for a business: Who this term suits for a business: an established wholesaler or distributor buying its own warehouse, where stable, repeat orders make a long, steady saving plan realistic and owning beats paying rent that rises every few years.

FAQs

How much down payment do I need?
It depends on the lender and the purchase. A common planning figure is 20 percent, but requirements vary.

Is this business calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why do lenders treat a 15-year warehouse plan differently from a 5-year shop plan?
A warehouse is a long-life asset with steadier tenants and resale value, so a lender may look at longer trading history, while a new shop is judged more on recent sales and risk.

Should a business buy its warehouse or keep renting for 15 years?
Buying fixes the premises cost and builds an asset, but renting keeps cash free for stock and growth, so the choice depends on how stable the business's orders are over 15 years.

How should a 15-year plan handle repairs and equipment replacement?
Repairs, racking, vehicles, and refrigeration wear out well before 15 years, so the deposit plan needs a separate maintenance budget, or the business will raid the deposit to fix them.
