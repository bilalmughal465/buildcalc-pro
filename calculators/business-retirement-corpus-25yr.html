Business Retirement Corpus Calculator (25 Years)

Estimate a retirement corpus from monthly savings. This business version uses a 25-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Corpus uses the monthly rate and number of monthly savings to grow each saving with interest.

How it works

Starting earlier matters more than saving a little more later, because interest has longer to compound.

Worked example

A business owner sets aside $500 every month for 25 years at 7% a year. The monthly rate is 7% divided by 12, which is 0.0058333, and there are 300 monthly savings. Using the page formula, the corpus is $500 times ((1.0058333 to the power of 300 minus 1) divided by 0.0058333), which is $405,035.85. The owner paid in $150,000, so interest added $255,035.85, which is 63.0% of the corpus. Nearly two-thirds of the final pot is interest, which is why the last 5 years alone add $144,572.52 to the 20-year figure.

Who this term suits for a business: an owner nearing a planned exit in their late 50s — for example a manufacturer who wants to sell the firm and retire on the sale plus this pot, rather than depending on the sale price alone in a weak market.

FAQs

When should I start saving for retirement?
As early as possible. Time in the market allows compounding to do more of the work.

Is this business calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why do the last 5 years add $144,572.52, more than the whole first 10 years?
Because the pot is large by year 20, so 7% a year on $260,463.33 adds far more dollars than 7% on a small early pot. Late years are the most valuable years in any long savings plan, which is why stopping early is so costly.

Does a 25-year business pot need a succession plan alongside it?
Yes. The pot funds the owner, but the business still needs a buyer, a successor, or a wind-down plan. Owners who save well but never plan the handover often find the business value falls just when they need it most.

Should deposits rise with inflation over 25 years?
Ideally, yes. A fixed $500 buys less in year 25 than in year 1, so the real value of the pot is lower than the headline $405,035.85 suggests. Raising the monthly sum with income or inflation keeps the pot's buying power closer to the plan.
