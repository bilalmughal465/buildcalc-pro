Farm Monthly Budget Split Calculator (15 Years)

Split monthly income into needs, wants and savings. This farm version uses a 15-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Needs are 50 percent, wants are 30 percent and savings are 20 percent of income.

How it works

This 50-30-20 split is a planning guide, not a rule. Adjust it to your real costs.

Worked example

Monthly income $4,000, term 15 years. Using the page formula, needs are 50 percent of $4,000, which is $2,000 a month, wants are 30 percent, which is $1,200 a month, and savings are 20 percent, which is $800 a month. Those three shares add back to $4,000, which checks the split. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults, and it is the same on every page in this group because the term is not an input.

As an illustration only, not the output of this page, keeping the same $4,000 every month for 15 years, which is 180 months, would total $720,000 of income, split into $360,000 for needs, $216,000 for wants and $144,000 for savings, before interest, fees, taxes or price rises. The illustration assumes income never varies, which is the weakest part of it for a farm: harvest income arrives in seasons, so a real farm must smooth good months into lean months for the monthly split to hold at all.

Who this term suits on a farm: a farm planning a medium building project, such as new fencing, drainage or storage: fifteen years of steady saving reaches $144,000 before interest, but the building will not cost today's price in fifteen years, so the fund must be re-priced as the project gets closer.

FAQs

What is the 50-30-20 rule?
It is a simple budget guide: 50 percent needs, 30 percent wants and 20 percent savings.

Is this farm calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is $144,000 of savings over 15 years enough for a barn or storage building?
It is a starting fund, not a quote. $800 a month over 180 months totals $144,000 before interest, but building costs in fifteen years will differ from today's, so treat the figure as the amount the habit produces, and get a real price when the project is a few years away.

How should a farm handle a bad year inside a 15-year plan?
Protect needs first, cut wants, and keep saving if possible, even a smaller amount. One bad year does not break a 180-month plan, but borrowing to keep wants at $1,200 a month would, because the debt then takes from future needs as well.

Does the 15-year figure include interest on savings?
No. The page only splits income, and the $144,000 illustration is simply $800 added up over 180 months. Any interest earned would add to it, and any fees or taxes would reduce it, and neither is shown on this page.
