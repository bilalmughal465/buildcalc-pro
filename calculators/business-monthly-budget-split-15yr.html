Business Monthly Budget Split Calculator (15 Years)

Split monthly income into needs, wants and savings. This business version uses a 15-year planning term as its starting point.

—

In plain terms, this result shows how your monthly income is divided: the Needs figure is the amount for essential costs, the Wants figure is the amount for non-essential spending, and the Savings figure is the amount set aside for saving, so the three figures together add up to your monthly income.

Formula

Needs are 50 percent, wants are 30 percent and savings are 20 percent of income.

How it works

This 50-30-20 split is a planning guide, not a rule. Adjust it to your real costs.

Worked example

Monthly income $4,000, term 15 years. Needs are 50 percent of $4,000, which is $2,000 a month. Wants are 30 percent of $4,000, which is $1,200 a month. Savings are 20 percent of $4,000, which is $800 a month. Together they add back to $4,000, so the split covers the whole monthly income with nothing left over. As a cumulative illustration only, not an output of this calculator, and with no interest, pay rises, or price rises counted, over 180 months the needs slice totals $360,000, the wants slice totals $216,000, and the savings slice totals $144,000, from total income of $720,000. The monthly split is the same on every page in this group, because the term in years is a planning horizon, not an input in the formula.

Who this term suits for a business: a workshop or trade business facing a lease renewal and a machinery replacement cycle, where the savings slice over 15 years is earmarked for plant, not for daily spending.

FAQs

What is the 50-30-20 rule?
It is a simple budget guide: 50 percent needs, 30 percent wants and 20 percent savings.

Is this business calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does a 15-year business plan need a separate savings slice?
Because machinery, vehicles, and premises wear out on roughly that timescale. Without a protected savings slice, a business must borrow for every replacement.

What happens if a business spends its savings slice on wants for 15 years?
It arrives at the replacement point with nothing set aside, so it faces a loan, a cheaper machine, or a shutdown while it raises funds.

Should rent rises be treated as needs or wants over 15 years?
As needs, because the business cannot trade without premises. If rent pushes needs above 50 percent, the honest response is to cut wants or raise prices, not to stop saving.
