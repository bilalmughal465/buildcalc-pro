Investment Present Value Calculator (5 Years)

Find how much a future amount is worth today. This investment version uses a 5-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The present value result is the amount you would need to invest today, at the rate you entered, to reach your future amount after 5 years, so a lower result means the future amount is worth less in today's money.

Formula

Present value equals future amount divided by one plus rate to the power of years.

How it works

A future amount is worth less today because money today can earn interest.

Worked example

Future amount $50,000, annual rate 5%, term 5 years. Using the page formula, present value equals $50,000 divided by 1.05 to the power of 5, which is $39,176.31. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults. The discount is $50,000 minus $39,176.31, which is $10,823.69, so time and interest remove $10,823.69 of value between today and year 5.

In other words, $39,176.31 invested today at a steady 5% a year would grow to $50,000 in 5 years, before fees and taxes. The present value is different on every page in this group: only the time input changes, and that alone produces the different figure on each page, because the future amount ($50,000) and the rate (5%) are identical on all 8 pages.

Who this term suits for an investor: an investor comparing a lump sum promised in about 5 years, such as a maturing bond, a fixed deposit payout, or a business exit payment, with money that could be invested today instead.

FAQs

What is present value?
Present value is what a future sum is worth today after allowing for interest over time.

Is this investment calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is $39,176.31 what I should invest today for $50,000 in 5 years?
Yes, if a steady 5% return is assumed: $39,176.31 invested today at 5% a year grows to $50,000 in 5 years, before fees and taxes.

Why is the discount only $10,823.69 over 5 years?
Because 5 years is short for compounding: the $50,000 is reduced by 5% a year only five times, so most of its value is still present today.

What if the real return is lower than 5% over 5 years?
Then the present value would be higher than $39,176.31, because a lower rate discounts the future sum less. Re-enter your own expected rate to check.
