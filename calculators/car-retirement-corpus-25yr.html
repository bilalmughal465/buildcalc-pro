Car Retirement Corpus Calculator (25 Years)

Estimate a retirement corpus from monthly savings. This car version uses a 25-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The Future value figure shows the total amount your regular monthly savings could grow to by retirement over 25 years at the rate you entered, combining what you paid in with the growth earned along the way.

Formula

Corpus uses the monthly rate and number of monthly savings to grow each saving with interest.

How it works

Starting earlier matters more than saving a little more later, because interest has longer to compound.

Worked example

A car owner sets aside $500 every month for 25 years at 7% a year. The monthly rate is 7% divided by 12, which is 0.0058333, and there are 300 monthly savings. Using the page formula, the corpus is $500 times ((1.0058333 to the power of 300 minus 1) divided by 0.0058333), which is $405,035.85. The owner paid in $150,000, so interest added $255,035.85, which is 63.0% of the corpus. The last 5 years alone add $144,572.52, more than the whole first 10 years built, which shows how much of a long pot comes at the end. For a car owner this is a generous driving fund, but it should not be read as spare money, because 25 years of car price rises will also have made cars and running costs much dearer.

Who this term suits for a car owner: A car owner planning a full working life of driving into retirement, who wants the pot to cover not just one car but the cars, insurance and servicing of a long retirement, including help for a partner who also drives.

FAQs

When should I start saving for retirement?
As early as possible. Time in the market allows compounding to do more of the work.

Is this car calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why do the last 5 years add $144,572.52, more than the whole first 10 years?
Because the pot is much larger by year 20, so 7% growth on $260,463.33 adds far more each year than 7% on a small early pot. Late growth dominates long plans, which is why the final years matter most.

Does a 25-year car pot need a plan for handing cars to family?
Yes, in practical terms. Over 25 years a driver may stop driving, downsize, or pass a car to family, so the pot should be planned alongside licences, insurance and who will actually use and maintain the car.

Should deposits rise with inflation over 25 years?
Yes, if possible. A fixed $500 buys far less car after 25 years of price rises, so raising the monthly saving with income helps the pot keep pace with the cars and running costs it is meant to pay for.
