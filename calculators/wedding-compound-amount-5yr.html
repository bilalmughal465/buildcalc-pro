Wedding Compound Amount Calculator (5 Years)

Calculate the amount after compound interest over time. This wedding version uses a 5-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Amount equals principal times one plus rate to the power of years.

How it works

Interest is added to the balance each year, so later interest is charged on a larger amount.

Worked example

Principal $10,000, annual rate 6%, term 5 years. Using the page formula, the amount is $10,000 times one plus 0.06 to the power of 5, which gives $13,382.26. That is the calculator output for these defaults. The interest is $13,382.26 minus $10,000, which is $3,382.26. The principal ($10,000) and rate (6%) are the same on every page in this group; only the term changes, so the amount genuinely differs by year.

Who this term suits for a wedding: a couple saving for a wedding about five years away, for example a venue deposit fund, a honeymoon pot, or a ring fund that must be ready before bookings are made.

FAQs

What is compound interest?
Compound interest is interest earned on both the principal and on interest already added.

Is this wedding calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is $13,382.26 a realistic wedding fund after 5 years?
Yes, for these defaults: $10,000 at 6% a year for 5 years grows to $13,382.26, so $3,382.26 of it is interest. It assumes the rate stays at 6% and nothing is withdrawn early.

What if the couple saves for the wedding at 8% instead of 6%?
At 8% a year the same $10,000 reaches $14,693.28 after 5 years, which is $1,311.02 more than at 6%. A higher rate helps, but it is not guaranteed.

Should wedding prices be counted at today's prices for a 5-year plan?
No. Wedding prices usually rise, so a fund that reaches $13,382.26 in 5 years may buy less than $13,382.26 buys today. Recheck venue and catering prices each year.
