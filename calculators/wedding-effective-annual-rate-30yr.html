Wedding Effective Annual Rate Calculator (30 Years)

Convert a nominal annual rate to an effective annual rate. This wedding version uses a 30-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The effective annual rate is the true yearly cost of borrowing after compounding is taken into account, so a higher effective rate means more interest is charged over a year than the nominal rate alone suggests.

Formula

Effective rate equals one plus nominal rate divided by periods, to the power of periods, minus one.

How it works

More frequent compounding raises the effective rate slightly above the nominal rate.

Worked example

Nominal rate 6%, compounded 12 times a year, term 30 years. The effective rate is one plus 0.06 divided by 12, to the power of 12, minus one, which gives 6.17%. That is the calculator's stated-formula result for these defaults, and it is the same on every page in this group because the term is not an input: only the nominal rate and the number of compounding periods are entered. On $10,000 for one year, the interest at the effective rate is $616.78, while at the nominal 6% it would be $600.00, so monthly compounding adds $16.78 in the first year. As an illustration only, $10,000 grown at the exact effective rate of 6.1678% for 30 years reaches $60,225.75, which is $50,225.75 of growth.

Who this term suits for a wedding: a long wedding savings plan of about thirty years, where comparing the effective rate, not the nominal rate, decides which loan or account is really cheaper.

FAQs

What is an effective rate?
The effective rate is the true yearly rate after allowing for compounding during the year.

Is this wedding calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is a 30-year wedding loan the same as a house mortgage?
No, even though the term can look similar. A house mortgage is secured on a home that usually keeps value, while a wedding loan is unsecured spending on one day. At the exact effective rate of 6.1678%, $10,000 grows to $60,225.75 over thirty years, which is $50,225.75 of growth, so a thirty-year wedding debt costs far more than the wedding itself.

What happens if the couple stops earning for a time in a 30-year plan?
Interest keeps compounding while payments stop, so the balance grows. At 6.17% effective, a $10,000 balance left untouched for one year becomes $10,616.78, and after five untouched years it becomes $13,488.50. A payment break therefore makes a long wedding loan much dearer, not just longer.

Should a couple borrow the full amount for 30 years on this rate?
Only after checking the total, not just the monthly payment. A small monthly payment over thirty years can hide a huge total: $10,000 at 6.1678% becomes $60,225.75, so the interest alone is five times the amount borrowed. Saving first, or borrowing over a shorter term, usually costs far less.
