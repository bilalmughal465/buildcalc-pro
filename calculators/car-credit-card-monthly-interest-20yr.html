Car Credit Card Monthly Interest Calculator (20 Years)

Estimate monthly interest on a card balance. This car version uses a 20-year planning term as its starting point.

—

In plain terms, this monthly interest amount is the interest charged for one month on the balance entered at the annual rate entered, before any payment toward the balance itself.

Formula

Monthly interest equals balance times annual rate divided by 100 divided by 12.

How it works

Paying only interest leaves the balance unchanged. Paying more than interest reduces the balance.

Worked example

Balance $5,000, annual rate 18%, term 20 years. Monthly interest is $5,000 times 18, divided by 100, divided by 12, which is $75.00 a month. That is the calculator's own output, and it is the same every month because the balance and rate do not change. If the car owner pays only interest, after 20 years they have paid $18,000 in interest, which is 360% of the balance, and they still owe the original $5,000. Paying more than $75.00 in any month is what finally reduces that balance.

Who this term suits for a car owner: a long-term car keeper, for example someone restoring or holding a car for 20 years, who needs a blunt check on what two decades of interest-only card payments cost compared with the car's value.

FAQs

Why is card interest high?
Card rates are usually higher than loan rates, so balances grow quickly if only minimum payments are made.

Is this car calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is it ever sensible to pay card interest on a car for 20 years?
Almost never. Over 240 months at $75.00 a month you pay $18,000 in interest, which is 360% of the $5,000 balance, and you still owe $5,000. No ordinary car holds its value or its usefulness for 20 years of card interest, so a 20-year card balance is a warning, not a plan.

How much must a car owner earn just to cover $75 a month?
The $75.00 is only the interest, so it buys nothing and reduces nothing. A car owner must earn that $75.00 after tax and after fuel, insurance and servicing, every month, for 240 months, just to stand still. Any month the payment is missed, charges or a higher balance can make the position worse.

What should a car owner do first if the card balance outlives the car?
Face the balance on its own, separate from the car. The car is gone, but the $5,000 balance remains, so the first step is a payoff plan that pays more than $75.00 a month, or a cheaper loan to replace the card debt, agreed before any new car is bought on credit.
