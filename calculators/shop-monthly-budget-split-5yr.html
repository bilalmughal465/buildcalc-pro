Shop Monthly Budget Split Calculator (5 Years)

Split monthly income into needs, wants and savings. This shop version uses a 5-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The result divides the monthly income entered into three spending limits: the Needs amount is for essential costs, the Wants amount is for optional spending, and the Savings amount is the portion set aside each month, and the three amounts together equal the monthly income entered.

Formula

Needs are 50 percent, wants are 30 percent and savings are 20 percent of income.

How it works

This 50-30-20 split is a planning guide, not a rule. Adjust it to your real costs.

Worked example

Monthly income $4,000, term 5 years. Needs are 50 percent of $4,000, which is $2,000 a month. Wants are 30 percent of $4,000, which is $1,200 a month. Savings are 20 percent of $4,000, which is $800 a month. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults, and it is the same on every page in this group because the term is not an input: only the monthly income decides the split.

As a planning illustration only, not an output of this page, keeping the same $4,000 income every month for 5 years means 60 months in total. Total income over the term would be $240,000, of which needs would be $120,000, wants $72,000 and savings $48,000. These totals assume the income never changes, and they leave out fees, taxes, interest and changing rates, so they are a way to see what the split adds up to, not a forecast.

Who this term suits for a shop owner: A shop owner in the first years of trading, setting aside a refit fund or a deposit for better premises: $800 a month of savings builds a visible pot quickly enough to plan around, while the $2,000 needs budget covers rent, stock and wages.

FAQs

What is the 50-30-20 rule?
It is a simple budget guide: 50 percent needs, 30 percent wants and 20 percent savings.

Is this shop calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is $800 a month of savings realistic for a new shop?
It depends on takings. On a $4,000 monthly income the split gives $800 for savings, but a new shop whose rent and stock push needs above $2,000 will have less left, and the savings figure should be cut first rather than borrowing to keep it at $800.

What shop costs count as needs in the $2,000 figure?
Rent, utilities, stock, wages, insurance and loan payments are needs. Shopfront displays, advertising treats and extra fittings are wants, and mixing the two is the most common reason a shop budget fails.

What if shop takings fall below $4,000 in a slow month?
Redo the split on the lower income. On $3,000, for example, needs become $1,500, wants $900 and savings $600, so the savings pot grows more slowly instead of the shop running short on rent.
