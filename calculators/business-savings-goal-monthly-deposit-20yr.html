Business Savings Goal Monthly Deposit Calculator (20 Years)

Find the monthly deposit needed to reach a savings goal. This business version uses a 20-year planning term as its starting point.

—

In plain terms, the Monthly deposit figure is the fixed amount to set aside every month so that, together with interest at the entered rate, the payments reach the goal amount by the end of the 20 years.

Formula

Monthly deposit equals goal times monthly rate divided by one plus monthly rate to the power of months minus one.

How it works

Saving a fixed amount each month with interest reaches the goal faster than saving without interest.

Worked example

Goal $50,000, annual rate 5%, term 20 years. The monthly rate is 5% divided by 12, which is 0.0041667, and the number of payments is 20 times 12, which is 240. Using the monthly deposit formula, the monthly deposit is $121.64. Over 240 payments the business deposits $29,194.69 in total, and interest adds $20,805.31 to reach the $50,000 goal. Interest now covers 41.6% of the goal, so growth does almost as much work as the deposits themselves.

Who this term suits for a business: A 20-year term suits a business building a long-term reserve, for example a family firm saving toward buying its premises, or a trades business building a fund for the owner's eventual exit. The monthly amount is small, but the plan must survive staff changes, recessions, and shifts in the business itself.

FAQs

How much should I save each month?
Divide the goal by the growth of regular deposits at the assumed rate to get the monthly amount.

Is this business calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does interest pass $20,000 at 20 years?
Because 240 monthly deposits have 20 years to compound at 5%. Each early deposit earns interest for many years, so interest reaches $20,805.31 even though the business deposits only $29,194.69. Time, not the monthly amount, does the heavy lifting here.

Is 20 years too long to lock a business savings goal?
It can be, if the goal is a specific purchase that may change. A 20-year plan works best for a broad reserve, such as a premises fund or an exit fund, rather than a single machine or vehicle that will be outdated long before the fund matures.

Should a business save for a goal before clearing other debts?
Usually not, if the debts cost more than 5%. Paying off expensive debt first saves more than a 5% savings plan earns. This calculator assumes a clean 5% return with no debts, so the business should compare its real borrowing cost before choosing to save.
