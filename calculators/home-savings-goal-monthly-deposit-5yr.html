Home Savings Goal Monthly Deposit Calculator (5 Years)

Find the monthly deposit needed to reach a savings goal. This home version uses a 5-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Monthly deposit equals goal times monthly rate divided by one plus monthly rate to the power of months minus one.

How it works

Saving a fixed amount each month with interest reaches the goal faster than saving without interest.

Worked example

Goal amount $50,000, annual rate 5%, term 5 years. The monthly rate is 5% divided by 12, which is about 0.4167% a month, and the number of deposits is 5 times 12, which is 60 months. Using the page formula, the monthly deposit is $50,000 times the monthly rate, divided by one plus the monthly rate to the power of 60, minus one, which gives $735.23 a month. That is the calculator output for these defaults.

Over 60 months the household itself deposits $44,113.70, and interest adds $5,886.30 to reach the $50,000 goal. Without interest the same goal would need $833.33 a month, so interest saves about $98.10 a month. After only 5 years the interest help is still small, because compounding has had little time to work, so most of the goal comes from the household's own deposits. All figures are nominal, before fees and taxes, and are planning estimates, not promises of any rate or final amount. The 5% rate is only the entered assumption; a lower real average rate would leave the same deposit short of the goal.

Who this term suits for a home household: a home household saving for a near-term, known home cost, such as a roof repair, a replacement boiler, or a kitchen appliance set due in about 5 years — the deposit is high because interest has little time to help, so the amount must be protected even when an unexpected home bill arrives.

FAQs

How much should I save each month?
Divide the goal by the growth of regular deposits at the assumed rate to get the monthly amount.

Is this home calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Can a home household really afford $735.23 a month for 5 years?
Only if the household budget can carry it after rent or mortgage, bills, and everyday costs. At $735.23 a month for 60 months the household itself deposits $44,113.70, and interest adds $5,886.30 to reach $50,000. If $735.23 squeezes home essentials, a longer term or a smaller goal is safer than starting a plan that stops after a few months.

Why does interest only add $5,886.30 over 5 years?
Because compounding needs time. With a 5% annual rate and 60 monthly deposits, most deposits are made near the end of the term and earn interest for only a short while, so interest adds $5,886.30 on $44,113.70 deposited. Over longer terms the same rate adds far more, which is why the monthly deposit falls so fast as the term lengthens.

What if a home repair comes up before the 5 years are done?
Then the plan should be paused or reduced rather than raided without a plan, because stopping deposits early leaves the goal short and the repair still has to be paid for. A household saving for a home cost should keep a separate small emergency buffer for urgent repairs, so one burst pipe or broken boiler does not wipe out the whole savings goal.
