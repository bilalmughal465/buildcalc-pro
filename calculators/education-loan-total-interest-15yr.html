Education Loan Total Interest Calculator (15 Years)

Estimate total interest paid over a loan. This education version uses a 15-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The total interest figure is the extra amount paid on top of the loan amount over the full term, and the total paid figure is the loan amount plus that interest, so the two figures together show the full cost of the loan.

Formula

Total interest equals monthly EMI times number of payments minus the principal.

How it works

A longer term lowers the monthly payment but raises total interest.

Worked example

Loan amount $200,000, annual rate 6.5%, term 15 years. The monthly rate is 6.5% divided by 12, which is 0.0054167, and there are 180 monthly payments. Using the page formula, the EMI is $1,742.21 a month, so the total paid is $313,598.65. Total interest is $313,598.65 minus $200,000, equal to $113,598.65, or 56.8% of the amount borrowed. That is about $7,573.24 of interest a year. The extra 5 years beyond 10 years adds $41,083.50 of interest while cutting the EMI by only $528.75 a month. Totals use the unrounded EMI, as the calculator does, so rounded figures multiplied out can differ by a dollar or two.

Who this term suits for an education borrower: a borrower funding a postgraduate or second qualification while working, who needs a lower monthly payment during study and early career years, and who plans to overpay once earnings rise.

FAQs

Why does a longer loan cost more interest?
More payments mean interest is charged for longer, so total interest rises even if each payment is smaller.

Is this education calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

When does a 15-year term make sense for an education loan?
When the 10-year EMI of $2,270.96 would squeeze essentials, but the borrower still wants the debt cleared well before retirement. The cost of that breathing room is $41,083.50 more interest than the 10-year term.

How can a 15-year borrower cut the interest bill?
By overpaying whenever pay rises, because extra payments cut the balance that interest is charged on. Even small, regular overpayments early in the term save more than the same overpayments made near the end.

Can a 15-year figure be used for a second degree?
Only as a rough guide. A second degree may mean a new loan, a different rate, or a gap in earnings, and none of that is in this calculation. The $113,598.65 applies to one $200,000 loan at a fixed 6.5% over 15 years.
