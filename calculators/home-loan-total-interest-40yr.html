Home Loan Total Interest Calculator (40 Years)

Estimate total interest paid over a loan. This home version uses a 40-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Over 40 years, total interest is the extra amount paid to the lender on top of the borrowed amount, and total paid is the borrowed amount plus that interest, so together they show the full cost of the home loan rather than just the price of the home.

Formula

Total interest equals monthly EMI times number of payments minus the principal.

How it works

A longer term lowers the monthly payment but raises total interest.

Worked example

Loan amount $200,000, annual rate 6.5%, term 40 years. The monthly rate is 6.5% divided by 12, which is 0.0054167, and there are 480 monthly payments. Using the page formula, the EMI is $1,170.91 a month, so the total paid is $562,038.54. Total interest is the total paid minus the principal, which is $562,038.54 minus $200,000, equal to $362,038.54, or 181.0 of the amount borrowed.

From 35 to 40 years the EMI falls by only $37.40 a month, while interest rises by $54,548.91, so the extension buys almost nothing. For a home buyer a 40-year term is a lifetime warning, showing a home debt that could still be repaid at retirement age, with total payments nearly three times the amount borrowed.

FAQs

Why does a longer loan cost more interest?
More payments mean interest is charged for longer, so total interest rises even if each payment is smaller.

Is this home calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is a 40-year home loan a good idea?
Almost never as a first choice. The payment is $1,170.91 a month, only $37.40 below the 35-year figure, while total interest reaches $362,038.54, which is 181.0% of the amount borrowed. It should only be compared against clearly better alternatives, not accepted by default.

How can $200,000 turn into $562,038.54 of total payments?
Through 480 monthly payments of $1,170.91. The total paid is $562,038.54, so $362,038.54 of it is interest charged over 40 years, which is why very long loans cost so much even when the monthly figure looks low.

What alternatives should a borrower compare before a 40-year term?
A smaller home, a larger deposit, a shorter term with overpayments, and waiting to buy until income is higher. Each of these cuts the $362,038.54 interest bill far more than shaving a few dollars off the monthly payment.
