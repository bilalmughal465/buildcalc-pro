Farm Loan Total Interest Calculator (20 Years)

Estimate total interest paid over a loan. This farm version uses a 20-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Total interest equals monthly EMI times number of payments minus the principal.

How it works

A longer term lowers the monthly payment but raises total interest.

Worked example

Loan amount $200,000, annual rate 6.5%, term 20 years. The monthly rate is 6.5% divided by 12, which is 0.0054167, and there are 240 monthly payments. Using the page formula, the EMI is $1,491.15 a month, so the total paid is $357,875.11. Total interest is $357,875.11 minus $200,000, equal to $157,875.11, or 78.9% of the amount borrowed. That is about $7,893.76 of interest a year. From 15 to 20 years the EMI falls by only $251.06 a month, while interest rises by $44,276.46.

Who this term suits for a farm borrower: a farmer buying extra acreage or a farmhouse, for example adding neighbouring land that will be farmed for a generation, where a 20-year horizon matches the working life of the purchase better than a short loan.

FAQs

Why does a longer loan cost more interest?
More payments mean interest is charged for longer, so total interest rises even if each payment is smaller.

Is this farm calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is 20 years too long for a farm loan?
It depends on what the loan buys. For land, which can be farmed for generations, 20 years can fit. For machinery, which wears out sooner, 20 years is too long, because the farmer would pay interest long after the machine is gone.

Why does the EMI fall by only $251.06 from 15 to 20 years?
Because most of the monthly payment at longer terms goes to interest rather than to cutting the balance. Stretching the term mainly stretches the interest, so the monthly saving gets smaller with each extension.

What happens if the farmer refinances at year 10 of a 20-year loan?
Refinancing restarts the clock on the remaining balance at the new rate and term. It can lower the payment or the total, but fees and a longer new term can also raise the total, so the new total should be compared with the $357,875.11 shown here.
