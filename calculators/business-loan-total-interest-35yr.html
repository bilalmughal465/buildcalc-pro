Business Loan Total Interest Calculator (35 Years)

Estimate total interest paid over a loan. This business version uses a 35-year planning term as its starting point.

—

In plain terms, the total interest is the extra cost of borrowing beyond the amount borrowed, and the total paid is the amount borrowed plus that interest over the full 35-year term.

Formula

Total interest equals monthly EMI times number of payments minus the principal.

How it works

A longer term lowers the monthly payment but raises total interest.

Worked example

Loan amount $200,000, annual rate 6.5%, term 35 years. The monthly rate is 6.5% divided by 12, which is 0.0054167, and the number of payments is 35 times 12, which is 420. Using the EMI formula, the monthly payment is $1,208.31. Over 420 payments the total paid is $507,489.63. Total interest is $507,489.63 minus $200,000, which is $307,489.63. That is 153.7% of the amount borrowed, so the business pays back more than two and a half times the original loan.

Who this term suits for a business: A 35-year term suits almost no ordinary business borrowing. It can only be discussed for a freehold property the business expects to occupy for decades, and even then the $307,489.63 interest bill must be weighed against buying a smaller property, leasing, or delaying the purchase. The monthly saving over 30 years is only $55.83, from $1,264.14 to $1,208.31, while total interest rises by $52,400.65.

FAQs

Why does a longer loan cost more interest?
More payments mean interest is charged for longer, so total interest rises even if each payment is smaller.

Is this business calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why is the payment only $55.83 lower than the 30-year payment?
Because after 30 years most of each payment is already going to interest, and adding 60 more payments mainly adds more interest. The payment falls from $1,264.14 to $1,208.31, but total interest rises from $255,088.98 to $307,489.63. Longer terms stop buying meaningful monthly relief and mostly buy extra interest.

Who should even consider a 35-year business term?
Almost nobody except a business buying freehold premises it will occupy for the full 35 years, with a clear succession plan for who runs the business in year 35. For any shorter-lived purpose, a 35-year term means paying interest long after the reason for borrowing has gone.

What must a business check before signing a very long term?
The total interest, not just the payment; the prepayment and exit terms; whether the loan is secured and what is at risk; and whether the business, its owners and its premises will plausibly still be in place for the full term. This calculator assumes a fixed 6.5% rate and no fees, so real terms must be checked separately.
