Emergency Savings Goal Monthly Deposit Calculator (15 Years)

Find the monthly deposit needed to reach a savings goal. This emergency version uses a 15-year planning term as its starting point.

—

This Monthly deposit figure is the regular amount to save each month so that, together with interest at the entered annual rate, the monthly deposits reach the goal amount by the end of the 15 years.

Formula

Monthly deposit equals goal times monthly rate divided by one plus monthly rate to the power of months minus one.

How it works

Saving a fixed amount each month with interest reaches the goal faster than saving without interest.

Worked example

A household wants a $50,000 emergency fund in 15 years, at 5% a year. The monthly rate is 5% divided by 12, which is 0.0041667, and there are 180 monthly deposits. Using the page formula, the monthly deposit is $50,000 times 0.0041667, divided by 1.0041667 to the power of 180 minus 1, which is $187.06 a month. Over 180 deposits the household pays in $33,671.43, and interest adds $16,328.57, which is 32.7% of the goal.

Fifteen years is where the monthly deposit falls below $200, and interest passes $16,000, nearly a third of the goal. For an emergency fund this horizon suits a household that is already stable and is building a deeper reserve, for example to cover a long period of self-employment or a second spell without work, but it is too slow as a first fund: fifteen years without a safety net is not a safety net at all, so a smaller, faster first fund should come first.

Who this term suits for an emergency saver: a self-employed worker building a deeper reserve over fifteen years, so that a dry spell in work or a serious illness does not mean borrowing to pay rent and bills.

FAQs

How much should I save each month?
Divide the goal by the growth of regular deposits at the assumed rate to get the monthly amount.

Is this emergency calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is 15 years the sweet spot for an emergency fund?
It is a good pace for a second, deeper reserve, but a poor pace for a first fund. Build three to six months of essentials as fast as possible first, then use a longer horizon like this one to deepen the fund toward $50,000.

What happens if the rate falls below 5% over 15 years?
The monthly deposit must rise to reach the same $50,000. At a lower rate the $187.06 figure no longer reaches the goal, so the plan should be re-checked whenever the account rate changes, not left on autopilot for fifteen years.

Can a 15-year fund cover a second emergency?
Only if it is refilled after the first one. A $50,000 fund spent on a home repair is a $0 fund until deposits rebuild it, and the fifteen-year clock for the replacement fund starts again from the amount left.
