Travel Effective Annual Rate Calculator (15 Years)

Convert a nominal annual rate to an effective annual rate. This travel version uses a 15-year planning term as its starting point.

—

In plain terms, the Effective annual rate is the true yearly cost after compounding, so a 6% nominal rate compounded monthly really costs 6.17% a year, slightly more than the headline rate suggests. The 15-year planning term on this page does not change this rate, because only the nominal rate and the number of compounding periods decide it.

Formula

Effective rate equals one plus nominal rate divided by periods, to the power of periods, minus one.

How it works

More frequent compounding raises the effective rate slightly above the nominal rate.

Worked example

Nominal rate 6 percent, compounding 12 times a year, term 15 years. Using the page formula, the effective rate is one plus 6 percent divided by 12, to the power of 12, minus one, which is 6.17 percent a year. That is the calculator's stated-formula result for these defaults, and it is the same on every page in this group because the term is not an input: only the nominal rate and the number of compounding periods decide the rate. One year of interest on 10,000 dollars is 616.78 dollars at the effective rate, against 600.00 dollars at the nominal 6 percent, a difference of 16.78 dollars from monthly compounding.

As an illustration only, not the output of this page, 10,000 dollars grown at a steady 6.17 percent a year for 15 years would reach 24,540.94 dollars, a gain of 14,540.94 dollars. The page itself only gives the yearly rate; the illustration shows what that rate means over the term.

Who this term suits for a traveller: a traveller taking a longer loan for a holiday home or a travel base abroad, where 15 years of monthly compounding makes the effective rate the figure that should be compared, not the nominal rate alone.

FAQs

What is an effective rate?
The effective rate is the true yearly rate after allowing for compounding during the year.

Is this travel calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does the effective rate matter more over 15 years for a traveller?
Because the compounding gap repeats every year, so a 0.17% yearly difference grows into a much larger total over 15 years. As an illustration only, 10,000 dollars at a steady 6.17% for 15 years would reach 24,540.94 dollars.

Does the 6.17% figure stay the same for the whole 15 years?
Only if the nominal rate and the monthly compounding stay fixed for all 15 years. If either changes, the true rate changes too, so the figure is a planning estimate, not a promise.

How should a traveller use this figure when comparing 15-year loans?
Compare effective rates first, then fees, then the total interest over the full term. A loan with a lower nominal rate but monthly compounding can still beat a higher nominal rate with yearly compounding.
