Business SIP Future Value Calculator (15 Years)

Estimate the future value of a regular investment plan. This business version uses a 15-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Over 15 years, the Future value figure is the estimated total your regular monthly investments would grow to by the end of the term, including interest earned along the way.

Formula

Future value uses the monthly rate and number of monthly investments to grow each investment with interest.

How it works

Regular investing smooths timing and lets compounding build the value over time.

Worked example

Monthly investment $300, annual rate 8%, term 15 years. First, the monthly rate is 8% divided by 12, which is 0.0066667, and the number of monthly investments is 15 times 12, which is 180. Using the future value formula, the future value is $300 times ((1.0066667 to the power of 180) minus 1) divided by 0.0066667, which is $103,811.47. Over 180 investments the business pays in $54,000.00, and interest adds $49,811.47, which is 48.0% of the future value. By year 15 interest is almost as large as all the deposits put together.

Who this term suits for a business: a business working towards a premises or major equipment goal over a medium lease cycle, for example a deposit on its own shop unit, a full kitchen or workshop refit, or a fleet renewal, where the monthly sum must survive several trading cycles, not just one good year.

FAQs

What is a SIP?
A SIP is a regular investment of a fixed amount, usually monthly, into a fund or plan.

Is this business calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

How does a 15-year term balance the monthly amount and the interest?
At 15 years the two sides are almost equal: $54,000.00 paid in and $49,811.47 of interest, giving $103,811.47. The monthly burden stays at $300, but interest has had enough time to nearly match the deposits, which is the middle point between a short reserve and a long-term fund.

What if the rate falls below 8% over 15 years?
The result falls sharply. At a fixed 6% instead of 8%, the same $300 a month for 15 years gives about $87,246, instead of $103,811.47. That is why the 8% figure must be read as an assumption, not a promise, and reviewed if returns change.

Can a 15-year SIP fund a premises deposit at year 15?
It can fund a deposit for a modest unit, because $103,811.47 is a substantial sum. Whether it is enough depends on prices in year 15, the lender deposit percentage and fit-out costs, so the business should re-check prices near the time, not 15 years ahead.
