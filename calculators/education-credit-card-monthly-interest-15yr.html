Education Credit Card Monthly Interest Calculator (15 Years)

Estimate monthly interest on a card balance. This education version uses a 15-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Monthly interest equals balance times annual rate divided by 100 divided by 12.

How it works

Paying only interest leaves the balance unchanged. Paying more than interest reduces the balance.

Worked example

Balance $5,000, annual rate 18%, term 15 years. Using the page formula, monthly interest is $5,000 times 18, divided by 100, divided by 12, which is $75.00 a month, or $900 a year. The term in years does not change this monthly answer, because the formula uses only balance and annual rate. As a multi-year extension only, not a forecast, assuming the same $5,000 balance and the same 18% rate stay unchanged, with no new spending, no repayments of principal, no fees and no late charges counted, the interest over 15 years is 180 months times $75.00, which is $13,500. That is 270% of the original balance: the interest alone is nearly three times the amount first borrowed, while the $5,000 itself is still owed at the end. Over a school career of 15 years, that is the price of never paying down the principal.

Who this term suits for an education borrower: a parent who used a card for school fees, tutoring or exam costs and is weighing a long, slow repayment, because the 15-year figure shows that stretching the same $5,000 balance does not make it cheaper: it multiplies the interest while the debt stays put.

FAQs

Why is card interest high?
Card rates are usually higher than loan rates, so balances grow quickly if only minimum payments are made.

Is this education calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why is 15-year interest ($13,500) so much larger than the balance?
Because interest is charged on the full $5,000 every month it remains unpaid: $900 a year for 15 years is $13,500, and none of that $13,500 reduces the $5,000 owed.

Can a 15-year card figure help choose between a card and a loan?
Yes, as a warning: a lower-rate education loan on the same $5,000 would charge far less than $900 a year, so the 15-year card total is a strong reason to compare rates before carrying the balance.

What breaks a 15-year card estimate most often?
New spending on the same card, a rate rise, or a missed payment adding fees. Any of these raises the balance or the rate, so the real total would be higher than $13,500, not lower.
