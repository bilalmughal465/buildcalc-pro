Emergency Present Value Calculator (20 Years)

Find how much a future amount is worth today. This emergency version uses a 20-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Present value equals future amount divided by one plus rate to the power of years.

How it works

A future amount is worth less today because money today can earn interest.

Worked example

A household wants to have $50,000 ready for a serious emergency in 20 years, at 5% a year. The divisor is 1.05 to the power of 20, which is 2.653297706. Dividing $50,000 by 2.653297706 gives a present value of $18,844.47. The discount is $50,000 minus $18,844.47, which is $31,155.53, or 62.3% of the future amount. The fall from the 15-year figure is $24,050.85 minus $18,844.47, which is $5,206.38, smaller in dollars than the $8,480.65 fall from 5 to 10 years, because the base being discounted is now smaller. For an emergency fund this is the point where the headline figure starts to mislead if it is read without its assumptions.

Who this term suits for an emergency planner: a parent planning a safety net that will still be there when the children are adults, for example a household twenty years from retirement that wants a $50,000 reserve for a health crisis or a long spell without work in later working life, while the mortgage is still being paid.

FAQs

What is present value?
Present value is what a future sum is worth today after allowing for interest over time.

Is this emergency calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does the fall from 15 to 20 years ($5,206.38) look smaller than from 5 to 10 years?
Because discounting is multiplicative, not a fixed dollar amount per five years. Each five-year step divides by the same 1.2762815625, but it acts on a smaller and smaller base, so the dollar drop shrinks: $8,480.65 from 5 to 10 years, $6,644.81 from 10 to 15 years, and $5,206.38 from 15 to 20 years, even though the percentage discount keeps rising.

Is $18,844.47 a safe lump sum to set aside for an emergency 20 years away?
Only as a starting estimate. Twenty years is long enough for jobs, health, homes and prices to change completely, and for the fund to be raided for other needs. A household should treat $18,844.47 as a floor to build on and review, not as a sum that guarantees $50,000 in 20 years.

Should a 20-year emergency fund be checked more often than a 5-year one?
Yes. A 5-year fund has little time to drift off course; a 20-year fund has twenty years of rate changes, cost rises and temptations to spend it. Checking it every year or two, and topping it up when costs rise, matters far more than getting the first figure exactly right.
