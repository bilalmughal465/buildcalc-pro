Retirement Savings Goal Monthly Deposit Calculator (35 Years)

Find the monthly deposit needed to reach a savings goal. This retirement version uses a 35-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The Monthly deposit figure is the fixed amount to set aside every month over 35 years, with interest assumed at the entered rate, so that the deposits plus growth reach the goal amount by the end of the term.

Formula

Monthly deposit equals goal times monthly rate divided by one plus monthly rate to the power of months minus one.

How it works

Saving a fixed amount each month with interest reaches the goal faster than saving without interest.

Worked example

Goal amount $50,000, annual rate 5%, term 35 years. The monthly rate is 5% divided by 12, which is about 0.4167% a month, and the number of deposits is 35 times 12, which is 420 months. Using the page formula, the monthly deposit is $50,000 times the monthly rate, divided by one plus the monthly rate to the power of 420, minus one, which gives $44.01 a month. That is the calculator output for these defaults. On an unrounded basis, total deposits over 420 months are $18,484.41, so growth adds $50,000 minus $18,484.41, which is $31,515.59, toward the goal. Without interest, the same goal would need $50,000 divided by 420, which is $119.05 a month, so interest saves about $75.04 a month on an unrounded basis.

Who this term suits for a retirement saver: A very early starter planning for a retirement that is still decades away, for example someone who begins in their twenties and wants time, not a large monthly amount, to carry most of the goal.

FAQs

How much should I save each month?
Divide the goal by the growth of regular deposits at the assumed rate to get the monthly amount.

Is this retirement calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

How does $44.01 a month become $50,000 over 35 years?
Through thirty-five years of compounding: 420 deposits total $18,484.41 and growth adds $31,515.59, nearly twice what you pay in. Without interest you would need $119.05 a month instead of $44.01.

Is a 35-year retirement plan worth starting if the goal will change?
Yes, because the habit matters more than the first target: starting at $44.01 a month builds the discipline, and the deposit and goal can both be raised later as pay and retirement plans become clearer.

What happens to this plan if I retire earlier than planned?
Retiring early shortens the term, so the pot will be smaller than $50,000 unless deposits rise. An early retirement date needs a new calculation, not the same $44.01 deposit.
