Wedding Break Even Units Calculator (20 Years)

Find how many units must be sold to break even. This wedding version uses a 20-year planning term as its starting point.

—

In plain terms, the Break even units figure is the number of units that must be sold for revenue to exactly cover all costs: sell fewer and the business makes a loss, sell more and it starts to make a profit. The term shown on the page is a planning label only, not an input to this calculation.

Formula

Break even units equal fixed costs divided by price minus variable cost per unit.

How it works

At break even, total revenue exactly covers total costs, with no profit and no loss.

Worked example

Fixed costs $10,000, price per unit $50, variable cost per unit $30, term 20 years. The contribution per unit is $50 minus $30, which is $20. Dividing $10,000 by $20 gives 500 break even units. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults, and it is the same on every page in this group because the term is not an input: only fixed costs, price and variable cost are entered. At 500 units, revenue is 500 times $50, which is $25,000, and total cost is $10,000 plus 500 times $30, which is also $25,000, so profit is zero. As an illustration only, spreading 500 units over 20 years means about 25 units a year, or about 2.08 a month, on average.

Who this term suits for a wedding: a family-run wedding business planning across a generation, such as a venue, a photography studio, or a catering company handed from one generation to the next.

FAQs

What is break even?
Break even is the point where revenue covers all costs, so profit is zero.

Is this wedding calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why is 25 units a year enough over 20 years?
Because 25 times 20 is 500. The same 500-unit total spread over 20 years needs only 25 units a year, or about 2.08 a month on average, as an illustration only.

Does a 20-year wedding plan survive changing wedding trends?
Only if it is reviewed. Styles, venues and guest numbers change over 20 years, so a wedding business should update its price and costs regularly instead of trusting one 20-year figure.

What if the variable cost falls to $25 per unit?
The contribution becomes $50 minus $25, which is $25, so break even falls to $10,000 divided by $25, which is 400 units. Lower variable costs mean fewer units are needed to break even.
