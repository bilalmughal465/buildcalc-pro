Car Loan EMI Calculator (5 Years)

Estimate the monthly EMI for a loan from amount, rate and term. This car version uses a 5-year planning term as its starting point.

—

This means the Monthly payment is the fixed amount due every month, and the Total paid is the sum of all monthly payments over the full 5-year term.

Formula

EMI equals principal times monthly rate times one plus monthly rate to the power of payments, divided by that power minus one.

How it works

This assumes a fixed rate and equal monthly payments with no extra fees.

Worked example

Loan amount $200,000, annual rate 6.5%, term 5 years. First, the monthly rate is 6.5% divided by 12, which is 0.0054167, and the number of payments is 5 times 12, which is 60. Using the EMI formula, the monthly payment is $3,913.23. Over 60 payments the total paid is $234,793.78, calculated from the unrounded EMI exactly as the calculator does. Total interest is $234,793.78 minus $200,000, which is $34,793.78. That is 17.4% of the amount borrowed, the lowest total interest in this car year group, because the loan is cleared quickly.

Who this term suits for a car buyer: A 5-year term suits a car buyer who wants a new, reliable car for daily driving and wants the loan finished before major repair bills arrive, for example a family buying a new car for commuting and school runs, or a driver who needs a dependable car for work and can carry $3,913.23 a month. The trade-off is clear: the monthly payment is high, but the interest bill stays small, and the car is still relatively new when the loan ends.

FAQs

What is an EMI?
An EMI is a fixed monthly payment that covers both interest and principal over the loan term.

Is this car calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is a 5-year car loan a good match for a new car?
Yes, for most new cars it is the best match in this group. The car is still fairly new when the $34,793.78 interest bill is finished, so the buyer is not still paying for a car that needs expensive repairs. Test the $3,913.23 payment against fuel, insurance and servicing costs as well, not against the loan payment alone.

How much does a car buyer save by choosing 5 years instead of 10 years?
On $200,000 at 6.5%, a 5-year loan costs $34,793.78 in interest and a 10-year loan costs $72,515.15, so the 5-year term saves $37,721.37. That saving is the reward for the higher monthly payment, and it leaves the buyer free to save for the next car sooner.

Can a car buyer pay a 5-year car loan off early and sell the car?
That depends on the lender, because some car loans charge a prepayment fee. This calculator assumes no fees and a fixed 6.5% rate, so ask the lender about early-repayment terms before signing. If the car is sold, the remaining loan balance must still be settled, even if the sale price is lower than the balance.
