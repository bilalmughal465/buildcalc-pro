Home Savings Goal Monthly Deposit Calculator (25 Years)

Find the monthly deposit needed to reach a savings goal. This home version uses a 25-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Monthly deposit equals goal times monthly rate divided by one plus monthly rate to the power of months minus one.

How it works

Saving a fixed amount each month with interest reaches the goal faster than saving without interest.

Worked example

Goal amount $50,000, annual rate 5%, term 25 years. The monthly rate is 5% divided by 12, which is about 0.4167% a month, and the number of deposits is 25 times 12, which is 300 months. Using the page formula, the monthly deposit is $50,000 times the monthly rate, divided by one plus the monthly rate to the power of 300, minus one, which gives $83.96 a month. That is the calculator output for these defaults.

Over 300 months the household itself deposits $25,188.51, and interest adds $24,811.49 to reach the $50,000 goal. Without interest the same goal would need $166.67 a month, so interest saves about $82.71 a month. Over 25 years interest covers almost half of the goal, which is why $83.96 a month can still reach $50,000. All figures are nominal, before fees and taxes, and are planning estimates, not promises of any rate or final amount. The 5% rate is only the entered assumption; a lower real average rate would leave the same deposit short of the goal.

Who this term suits for a home household: a home household planning a generation ahead, such as saving for a retirement home, helping children with a first home deposit while keeping the family home in good repair, or funding a full home refurbishment over about 25 years.

FAQs

How much should I save each month?
Divide the goal by the growth of regular deposits at the assumed rate to get the monthly amount.

Is this home calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is $83.96 a month really enough for a $50,000 home goal?
On the page's assumptions, yes: 300 monthly deposits of $83.96 total $25,188.51 from the household, and interest at 5% adds $24,811.49 to reach $50,000. It is enough only if the deposits are actually made for all 25 years and the rate averages 5% — both are big assumptions over a quarter of a century.

What is the biggest risk in a 25-year home savings plan?
That the goal itself changes. Over 25 years a household may move, renovate, retire, or pass the home on, and a $50,000 target set today may no longer match the home project it was meant to pay for. Regular reviews matter more than the starting figure.

Should the deposit rise as household income grows over 25 years?
Yes, where possible. Raising the deposit when income rises protects the plan against years when deposits are missed and against home costs rising faster than expected. A deposit that never rises over 25 years is unlikely to keep pace with real home prices.
