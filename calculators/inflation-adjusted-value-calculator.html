Inflation-Adjusted Value Calculator

Find what a past or future amount is worth after inflation.

—

This result shows the future nominal amount needed to match the starting amount after the stated years of inflation, not an increase in what that money can buy.

Formula

Future nominal amount equals amount times one plus inflation to the power of years; today's equivalent of a future amount divides by the same factor.

How it works

Inflation quietly shrinks purchasing power. A 3% rate roughly halves purchasing power in about 24 years, which is why long-term savings need returns above inflation.

Worked example

For example, $10,000 at 3% inflation for 10 years gives $10,000 × 1.03^10 = $13,439.16.

FAQs

What rate should I use?
A long-run average for your country, often around 2–3%, or your own expected rate.

Is this the same as a price index?
It uses the same idea — a steady rate instead of a published index series.

Why does direction matter?
Future value inflates a sum forward; today's equivalent discounts a future sum back.

Are these figures financial advice?
No — they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.
