Personal Present Value Calculator (20 Years)

Find how much a future amount is worth today. This personal version uses a 20-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Present value equals future amount divided by one plus rate to the power of years.

How it works

A future amount is worth less today because money today can earn interest.

Worked example

Future amount $50,000, annual rate 5%, term 20 years. First, the discount factor is 1.05 to the power of 20, which is 2.653298. Present value is $50,000 divided by 2.653298, which is $18,844.47. The discount is $50,000 minus $18,844.47, which is $31,155.53, or 62.3% of the future amount. After 20 years only 37.7% of the value remains today.

Who this term suits for a person: A 20-year term suits a person planning retirement or a long family goal, for example someone in their 40s looking at a pension payout at 65, or a parent planning for a child over two decades. The headline $50,000 should never be compared directly with cash today.

FAQs

What is present value?
Present value is what a future sum is worth today after allowing for interest over time.

Is this personal calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why is only $18,844.47 left after 20 years?
Because money today can more than double in 20 years at 5%, with a factor of 2.653298. A future sum must therefore be divided by that factor, which leaves less than two fifths of $50,000 in today's money.

Is a 20-year forecast reliable at a fixed 5% rate?
No, it is an estimate for planning only. Rates, inflation, work, and health will change over 20 years, so review the plan every few years instead of locking a decision on $18,844.47 alone.

Should I borrow against a $50,000 sum due in 20 years?
A lender will discount even more for risk and fees, so do not expect to borrow $18,844.47 against it. This figure is a planning comparison, not an amount a lender will offer you today.

Estimates only, not financial advice. Figures are approximate and do not include all fees, taxes, or changing rates unless stated.
