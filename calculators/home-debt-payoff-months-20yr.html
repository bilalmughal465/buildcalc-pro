Home Debt Payoff Months Calculator (20 Years)

Estimate months to pay off a debt with fixed payments. This home version uses a 20-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The result, labelled Months to pay off, is the number of months needed to clear the balance at the entered monthly payment and annual rate, so a larger payment shortens this time and a smaller payment lengthens it; if the payment does not cover the monthly interest, the calculator instead shows Payment is too low to pay off this debt.

Formula

Months are found from the balance, monthly rate and monthly payment using the loan payoff formula.

How it works

If the payment is less than monthly interest, the balance will not fall.

Worked example

Balance $10,000, annual rate 12 percent, monthly payment $300, term 20 years. The monthly rate is 12 percent divided by 12, which is 1 percent a month, so the first month of interest on $10,000 is $100. A $300 payment covers that $100 of interest and puts $200 toward the balance in the first month. Using the loan payoff formula, the months to pay off are 40.7. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults, and it is the same on every page in this group because the term is not an input: only the balance, the rate and the monthly payment are entered. Over 40.7 months at $300 a month, the total paid is about $12,225, so the interest paid is about $12,225 minus $10,000, which is $2,225. Against a 20-year planning window of 240 months, the debt is cleared about 199.3 months before the window ends, as a comparison only, not as a second output of this page. All figures are estimates for planning only, as the page disclaimer states, and they assume the rate stays at 12 percent and every $300 payment is made on time.

Who this term suits for a home household: a household carrying a home debt alongside a longer mortgage, who need to know that a $300 payment clears this balance in about 41 months rather than letting it drift for 20 years, so they can plan the mortgage itself separately instead of mixing the two debts together.

FAQs

What if my payment is too low?
If the payment does not cover monthly interest, the debt will grow instead of shrinking.

Is this home calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is $300 a month for 20 years realistic for a home debt?
No household needs to pay this debt for 20 years: on these defaults it is cleared in about 41 months. A 20-year view is useful only for comparing this short debt with a long mortgage, not for planning 240 payments of $300.

What if other home debts are counted before this one?
This page handles one balance only, so a household with a mortgage, a car loan and a card balance should list each debt separately and decide the order, for example clearing the highest-rate balance first, rather than trusting one 40.7-month figure for everything.

Should the $300 payment rise with income over 20 years?
Raising the payment whenever income rises would clear the balance even faster than 40.7 months and cut interest below about $2,225, which is worth doing, since keeping the payment flat for 20 years wastes the chance to finish early.
