Medical Future Value Monthly Savings Calculator (15 Years)

Estimate the future value of regular monthly savings. This medical version uses a 15-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Future value uses the monthly rate and the number of monthly deposits to grow each deposit with interest.

How it works

Each monthly deposit earns interest from the month it is made until the end of the term.

Worked example

Monthly deposit $500, annual rate 6 percent, term 15 years. The monthly rate is 6 percent divided by 12, which is 0.5 percent a month, and the number of monthly deposits is 15 times 12, which is 180 deposits. Using the page formula, future value equals $500 times one plus 0.005 to the power of 180, minus one, all divided by 0.005, which is $145,409.36. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults. Total deposits are $500 times 180, which is $90,000, so interest adds $145,409.36 minus $90,000, which is $55,409.36.

Who this term suits for a medical saver: a practice saving toward a larger step, such as expanding to a second treatment room, buying a practice share, or funding a major refurbishment in about 15 years, where the goal needs time for compounding to do real work.

FAQs

How does monthly saving grow?
Regular deposits grow because each deposit earns interest, and that interest then earns more interest.

Is this medical calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does interest reach $55,409.36 over 15 years?
Compounding speeds up with time. By year 15 the interest earned each year is itself earning interest, so the $90,000 paid in has grown by $55,409.36, far more than the $4,885.02 earned over only 5 years.

Can a 15-year medical fund survive changing equipment prices?
Only if the target is reviewed. The $145,409.36 is a nominal figure, and equipment bought in 15 years will cost more than today, so the goal amount should be raised over time to match real prices.

What happens if the rate falls below 6 percent for 15 years?
The final value falls, and the fall grows with the length of the shortfall. A lower average return over 15 years leaves the same $500 deposits well short of $145,409.36, so the plan should be rechecked if returns drop.
