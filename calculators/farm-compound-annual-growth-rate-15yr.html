Farm Compound Annual Growth Rate Calculator (15 Years)

Calculate compound annual growth over a period. This farm version uses a 15-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

CAGR equals ending value divided by starting value, to the power of one over years, minus one.

How it works

CAGR smooths growth into a single yearly rate, even if actual yearly growth varied.

Worked example

Starting value $10,000, ending value $20,000, term 15 years. First, the growth factor is $20,000 divided by $10,000, which is 2.00, so the value doubled. Using the page formula, CAGR is 2.00 to the power of one over 15, minus one, times 100. The 15th root of 2.00 is about 1.0473, so CAGR is about 4.73% a year. That is the calculator's own output for the page defaults. It is a smoothed average, not a claim that the farm grew by 4.73% in every single year: on a real farm some years grow fast and some stand still or fall back, and CAGR averages them into one steady rate.

Who this term suits on a farm: a farm reviewing a medium-term investment, such as an orchard planted 15 years ago now in full bearing, a barn or storage building paid for over time, or a herd bloodline developed across several generations.

FAQs

What does CAGR mean?
CAGR is the steady yearly growth rate that would take a starting value to an ending value.

Is this farm calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

What does 4.73% a year mean over 15 years?
It is the steady yearly rate that doubles $10,000 to $20,000 in 15 years. It does not mean the farm grew by 4.73% in every single year; some years will have been higher and some lower, and the average smooths them into one figure.

Why does a 15-year doubling look modest beside a 5-year doubling?
Because the same $10,000 gain is spread over 15 years instead of 5. The 5-year figure is about 14.87% a year, while the 15-year figure is about 4.73% a year, even though the start and end values are identical.

Is a 15-year farm CAGR useful for a new planting decision?
Only as background. A new orchard or crop faces its own establishment years, prices, and risks, so use the 15-year average to sense-check the plan, not to replace a year-by-year budget for the new planting.
