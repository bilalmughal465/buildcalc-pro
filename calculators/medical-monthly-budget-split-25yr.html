Medical Monthly Budget Split Calculator (25 Years)

Split monthly income into needs, wants and savings. This medical version uses a 25-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Needs are 50 percent, wants are 30 percent and savings are 20 percent of income.

How it works

This 50-30-20 split is a planning guide, not a rule. Adjust it to your real costs.

Worked example

Monthly income $4,000, term 25 years. Using the page formula, needs are 50 percent of $4,000, which is $2,000 a month, wants are 30 percent, which is $1,200 a month, and savings are 20 percent, which is $800 a month. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults, and it is the same on every page in this group because the term is not an input: only the monthly income decides the split.

As an illustration only, not the output of this page, holding that same monthly split steady for 25 years (300 months) would total $600,000 for needs, $360,000 for wants and $240,000 for savings. That illustration counts no interest earned, no inflation, no fees and no change in income, so it shows the scale of a steady habit, not a forecast.

Who this term suits for a medical household: a practitioner planning a full career arc, such as building a practice that can later be handed over or sold — where 25 years of a disciplined split turns a modest monthly saving into a fund large enough to matter at handover.

FAQs

What is the 50-30-20 rule?
It is a simple budget guide: 50 percent needs, 30 percent wants and 20 percent savings.

Is this medical calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why is the 25-year savings total $240,000, not a clinic I can buy?
Because $240,000 is only the sum of $800 monthly savings over 300 months, before interest, inflation, fees and price rises — a clinic purchase price in 25 years will look very different from today's prices.

How should a medical practice review a 25-year budget split?
At least once a year, and after every big change: a new partner, a premises move, a major equipment purchase, or a lasting change in patient numbers. A 25-year plan that is never reviewed is just a guess.

What medical risks grow over 25 years on one fixed split?
Career breaks, illness, regulation changes, new technology and rising staff costs — any of these can break a fixed $4,000 income assumption, so the split needs a buffer, not just three neat columns.
