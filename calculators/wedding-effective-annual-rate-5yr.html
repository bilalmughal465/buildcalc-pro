Wedding Effective Annual Rate Calculator (5 Years)

Convert a nominal annual rate to an effective annual rate. This wedding version uses a 5-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The effective annual rate is the true yearly cost of borrowing after compounding is taken into account, so a higher effective rate means more interest is charged over a year than the nominal rate alone suggests.

Formula

Effective rate equals one plus nominal rate divided by periods, to the power of periods, minus one.

How it works

More frequent compounding raises the effective rate slightly above the nominal rate.

Worked example

Nominal rate 6%, compounded 12 times a year, term 5 years. The effective rate is one plus 0.06 divided by 12, to the power of 12, minus one, which gives 6.17%. That is the calculator's stated-formula result for these defaults, and it is the same on every page in this group because the term is not an input: only the nominal rate and the number of compounding periods are entered. On $10,000 for one year, the interest at the effective rate is $616.78, while at the nominal 6% it would be $600.00, so monthly compounding adds $16.78 in the first year. As an illustration only, $10,000 grown at the exact effective rate of 6.1678% for 5 years reaches $13,488.50, which is $3,488.50 of growth.

Who this term suits for a wedding: a couple saving for a wedding about five years away, who want to know the true yearly cost of a loan or the true return on a wedding fund before booking a venue.

FAQs

What is an effective rate?
The effective rate is the true yearly rate after allowing for compounding during the year.

Is this wedding calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is 6.17% the real yearly cost of a 5-year wedding loan at 6% nominal?
Yes, with monthly compounding. A 6% nominal rate compounded 12 times a year gives an effective rate of (1 + 0.06 divided by 12) to the power of 12, minus one, which is 6.17%. On $10,000 for one year that is $616.78 of interest, not $600.00, so the extra $16.78 is the cost of monthly compounding.

Should a couple compare nominal or effective rates when borrowing for a wedding?
Effective rates, because two loans can share the same 6% nominal rate and still cost different amounts if one compounds monthly and the other yearly. A loan compounded yearly at 6% costs exactly 6.00% effective, while the same rate compounded monthly costs 6.17% effective, so the monthly-compounded loan is dearer even though the headline rate looks the same.

Why does monthly compounding matter for a short wedding loan?
Because interest is charged on interest. Each month the lender adds that month's interest to the balance, and the next month's interest is charged on the larger balance. Over one year that lifts $600.00 of simple interest to $616.78, and over five years $10,000 grown at the exact effective rate of 6.1678% reaches $13,488.50.
