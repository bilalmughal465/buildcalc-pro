Car EMI Affordability Calculator (10 Years)

Estimate a safe monthly EMI from income. This car version uses a 10-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Safe EMI equals monthly income times safe share divided by 100.

How it works

Lenders often use a share of income as a guide, but other debts also matter.

Worked example

Monthly income $5,000, safe EMI share 30 percent, term 10 years. First, the safe EMI is $5,000 times 30 divided by 100, which is $1,500 a month. The number of payments is 10 times 12, which is 120, so the total EMI outflow is $1,500 times 120, which is $180,000. That safe EMI of $1,500 does not change with the term, because the page formula uses only income and safe share. As an illustration only, not the output of this page, a $1,500 payment could support about $132,102.75 over 10 years at 6.5 percent, with about $47,897.25 of interest, so the total paid would be $180,000. The monthly payment is unchanged, but the total outflow has doubled compared with 5 years, while the supportable loan has grown by only $55,439.73, because a car loan stretched over 10 years pays far more interest.

Who this term suits for a car buyer: A 10-year term suits a car buyer who needs the same $1,500 payment spread over a longer period, but only if the buyer accepts that the car will be old before the loan ends, for example a buyer keeping a well-maintained car for a decade and budgeting for repairs, tyres, and insurance in later years. The buyer must check that the car will still be worth running at 10 years, because the loan does not end when the car wears out.

FAQs

How much EMI can I afford?
A common guide is 30 percent of monthly income, but total debts and living costs must also fit.

Is this car calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why is the safe EMI still $1,500 after 10 years?
The safe EMI formula uses only income and safe share, so it does not change with the term. The term changes what that $1,500 adds up to: 120 payments make $180,000 in total outflow, and the illustration loan at 6.5 percent is $132,102.75, with $47,897.25 of interest.

Why is a 10-year car loan already long for a car?
A car loses value, wears out, and needs more repairs over 10 years, yet the $1,500 payment continues through all of it. A buyer must budget for later-year repairs and check whether the car will still be worth running at 10 years, or whether a replacement will be needed while the loan is still running.

How should a car buyer test whether $1,500 a month is affordable?
Add the car payment to fuel, insurance, servicing, and any other debts, then compare that total with the lowest recent monthly income, not the best month, and keep a cash buffer for repairs. If the total strains the budget in a slow month, the car price, not the loan term, should change.
