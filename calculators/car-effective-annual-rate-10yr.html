Car Effective Annual Rate Calculator (10 Years)

Convert a nominal annual rate to an effective annual rate. This car version uses a 10-year planning term as its starting point.

—

This result shows the true yearly rate after compounding, so it is the rate to compare with other car finance offers rather than the headline nominal rate alone.

Formula

Effective rate equals one plus nominal rate divided by periods, to the power of periods, minus one.

How it works

More frequent compounding raises the effective rate slightly above the nominal rate.

Worked example

Nominal rate 6%, compounding 12 times a year, term 10 years. Using the formula shown on this page, the effective annual rate is one plus 6 divided by 100 and then by 12, which is 1.005, raised to the power of 12, which is about 1.061678, minus one, which is 6.17%. That 6.17% is the same on every page in this group, because the term in years is a planning horizon, not an input in the formula. As a term illustration only, not a separate output of this page, every $10,000 borrowed for a car grows to about $18,193.97 over 10 years at the 6.17% effective rate, compared with about $17,908.48 at a flat 6% with no compounding. The difference, $285.49, is the cost of monthly compounding alone over 10 years, and the totals are rounded to the nearest cent.

Who this term suits for a car: a family comparing a 10-year view on a car purchase, for example weighing a dealer loan against a bank loan for a family SUV, where the effective rate decides which quote is really cheaper over the years the car is kept.

FAQs

What is an effective rate?
The effective rate is the true yearly rate after allowing for compounding during the year.

Is this car calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does the compounding bonus grow to $285.49 over 10 years?
Because each year's extra interest itself earns interest in later years. The bonus is only $106.25 after 5 years, but compounding on that bonus, and on every later year's bonus, pushes it to $285.49 by year 10 on the same $10,000.

Is the effective rate more important for a 10-year car plan than for a 1-year car loan?
Yes. Over one year the gap between a nominal and an effective rate is small, but over 10 years of car ownership, with loans, refinancing, or savings for the next car, the gap compounds and changes which deal is cheaper.

How should a car buyer compare two 10-year quotes with different compounding?
Convert both quotes to their effective annual rates first, then compare those figures, not the headline rates. A quote that compounds monthly at 6% costs more than a quote that compounds yearly at 6%, even though both adverts say 6%.
