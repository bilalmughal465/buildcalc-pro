Home Down Payment Amount Calculator (25 Years)

Calculate a down payment from price and percentage. This home version uses a 25-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Down payment equals price times down payment percentage divided by 100.

How it works

A larger down payment usually means a smaller loan and lower monthly payments.

Worked example

Price $300,000, down payment 20 percent, term 25 years. Using the page formula, down payment equals $300,000 times 20 divided by 100, which is $60,000. Loan needed is $300,000 minus $60,000, which is $240,000. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults, and it is the same on every page in this group because the term is not an input.

As a planning illustration only, not the output of this page: saving $60,000 evenly over 25 years means $200.00 a month for 300 months, before any interest. That illustration shows the saving pace, not a different deposit, and it ignores interest earned on savings, fees, taxes and changing home prices.

Who this term suits for a home buyer: a home household matching a typical mortgage-length horizon, for example buyers who want the deposit saved by the time they are ready to settle for the long term, where the monthly pace is modest but must be kept up for twenty-five years.

FAQs

How much down payment do I need?
It depends on the lender and the purchase. A common planning figure is 20 percent, but requirements vary.

Is this home calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why is the 25-year total of $60,000 not the price of a house I can buy?
Because $60,000 is only the deposit in this example, not the home price. On a $300,000 home the loan needed is still $240,000, and the household must be able to afford the monthly payments on that loan as well as the deposit.

How should a home family review a 25-year deposit plan?
At least every few years, by checking the current price of the kind of home they want, their income, and how much they have actually saved. A plan that is never reviewed can look on track while quietly falling behind rising prices.

What home risks grow over 25 years?
Job loss, illness, interest-rate rises, and major repairs to a current home can all interrupt saving. Keeping an emergency fund separate from the deposit protects the plan when one of those risks appears.
