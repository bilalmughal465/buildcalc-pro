Retirement Savings Goal Monthly Deposit Calculator (5 Years)

Find the monthly deposit needed to reach a savings goal. This retirement version uses a 5-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The Monthly deposit figure is the fixed amount to set aside every month over 5 years, with interest assumed at the entered rate, so that the deposits plus growth reach the goal amount by the end of the term.

Formula

Monthly deposit equals goal times monthly rate divided by one plus monthly rate to the power of months minus one.

How it works

Saving a fixed amount each month with interest reaches the goal faster than saving without interest.

Worked example

Goal amount $50,000, annual rate 5%, term 5 years. The monthly rate is 5% divided by 12, which is about 0.4167% a month, and the number of deposits is 5 times 12, which is 60 months. Using the page formula, the monthly deposit is $50,000 times the monthly rate, divided by one plus the monthly rate to the power of 60, minus one, which gives $735.23 a month. That is the calculator output for these defaults. On an unrounded basis, total deposits over 60 months are $44,113.70, so growth adds $50,000 minus $44,113.70, which is $5,886.30, toward the goal. Without interest, the same goal would need $50,000 divided by 60, which is $833.33 a month, so interest saves about $98.10 a month on an unrounded basis.

Who this term suits for a retirement saver: A worker a few years from retirement topping up a pension pot, for example closing a shortfall before retiring, funding the first years of retirement spending, or building a small reserve for home help and health costs soon after stopping work.

FAQs

How much should I save each month?
Divide the goal by the growth of regular deposits at the assumed rate to get the monthly amount.

Is this retirement calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Can I still build a $50,000 retirement pot in only 5 years?
Yes, at $735.23 a month for 60 months, but it is a demanding habit so close to retirement: total deposits are $44,113.70 and growth adds only $5,886.30, so almost the whole pot must come from your own payments.

Why does interest add only $5,886.30 over 5 years?
Because there is little time for compounding: the first deposits earn interest for only a few years, so the pot is mostly your own money. Over longer terms the same rate adds far more, which is why starting earlier matters.

What if I have to stop deposits before retirement?
Stopping early leaves the pot short of $50,000, since the plan needs all 60 deposits. A missed year near retirement is hard to make up, so keep an emergency reserve separate from this retirement pot.
