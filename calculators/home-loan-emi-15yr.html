Home Loan EMI Calculator (15 Years)

Estimate the monthly EMI for a loan from amount, rate and term. This home version uses a 15-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

EMI equals principal times monthly rate times one plus monthly rate to the power of payments, divided by that power minus one.

How it works

This assumes a fixed rate and equal monthly payments with no extra fees.

Worked example

Loan amount $200,000, annual rate 6.5%, term 15 years. The monthly rate is 6.5% divided by 12, which is 0.0054167, and there are 180 monthly payments. Using the page formula, the EMI is $1,742.21 a month. Over 180 payments the total paid is $313,598.65, so interest is $113,598.65, which is 56.8% of the amount borrowed. Stretching from 10 to 15 years cuts the EMI by $528.75 a month, while interest rises by $41,083.50. The payment becomes easier, but the house now costs more than half its price again in interest. For a home buyer this is the point where the trade-off between a lower payment and a higher total cost becomes hard to ignore. All figures assume a fixed 6.5% rate with no fees, taxes or changing rates, and are planning estimates, not promises.

Who this term suits for a home buyer: a family upsizing to a larger house, for example for growing children or a home office, who need a payment that fits around childcare and school costs, and who expect their income to rise over the next few years.

FAQs

What is an EMI?
An EMI is a fixed monthly payment that covers both interest and principal over the loan term.

Is this home calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

When does a 15-year term make sense for a home loan?
When the monthly payment on a shorter term would squeeze everyday living, but the buyer still wants the mortgage finished in a reasonable time. It balances a liveable payment against a total interest bill of $113,598.65.

How can a 15-year borrower cut the interest bill?
By paying a little extra whenever income allows, for example from a bonus or a tax refund. Extra payments cut the balance directly, so later interest falls, provided the lender allows overpayments without a penalty.

Can a 15-year figure be used for a second home purchase?
Only as a rough guide. A second purchase has its own price, rate and deposit, so its EMI and interest will differ. The method is the same, but the numbers must be recalculated for that property.
