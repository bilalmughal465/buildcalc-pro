Personal Down Payment Amount Calculator (25 Years)

Calculate a down payment from price and percentage. This personal version uses a 25-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The Down payment figure is the cash amount paid upfront from the price and percentage entered, and the Loan needed figure is the remaining amount to borrow, so a larger down payment directly reduces the loan needed.

Formula

Down payment equals price times down payment percentage divided by 100.

How it works

A larger down payment usually means a smaller loan and lower monthly payments.

Worked example

Price $300,000, down payment 20 percent, term 25 years. First, the down payment is $300,000 times 20 divided by 100, which is $60,000. The loan needed is $300,000 minus $60,000, which is $240,000. That $60,000 does not depend on the term, because the page formula uses only price and percentage. Saving illustration only, not the output of this calculator: to save $60,000 in 25 years, with no interest counted, takes exactly $200.00 a month for 300 months. At $200.00 a month the habit is easy to keep, but the purchase it is saving for will have changed, because 25 years of price rises will move the target well beyond $300,000.

Who this term suits for a person: A 25-year term suits a family saver who can keep $200.00 a month going through school years and job changes, for example parents saving slowly for a larger home while their children grow up. The habit is the strength here, but the plan must be re-priced regularly, because a 25-year-old $300,000 target is a starting point, not a promise.

FAQs

How much down payment do I need?
It depends on the lender and the purchase. A common planning figure is 20 percent, but requirements vary.

Is this personal calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does saving $200.00 a month for 25 years still need a price review?
Because $200.00 a month for 300 months reaches $60,000 with no interest counted, but that $60,000 was 20 percent of a $300,000 price at the start. After 25 years the same kind of home will cost far more, so 20 percent of the new price will be far more than $60,000. The monthly habit is the easy part; keeping the target honest across 25 years of price rises is the hard part.

How do I save through job changes and family costs over 25 years?
Keep the fund separate from everyday money, so a job change does not quietly spend it, and set a floor the saver keeps even in tight months, then raise the amount again when income recovers. Saving $200.00 a month is a floor, not a ceiling: months when pay is higher should add more, because 25 years contains both good and bad stretches, and the fund only survives if the good months carry the bad ones.

Is a 25-year plan better than borrowing sooner with a smaller deposit?
It depends on rent and price rises. Saving for 25 years avoids a bigger loan, but it also means 25 years of rent and 25 years of house-price rises, which can cost more than the interest saved by a larger deposit. Borrowing sooner with a smaller deposit means a bigger loan, $240,000 at 20 percent becomes larger if the deposit is smaller, and higher monthly payments. This calculator shows only the down payment amount, so the saver should compare rent, loan payments, and price growth together before choosing.
