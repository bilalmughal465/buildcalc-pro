Education SIP Future Value Calculator (20 Years)

Estimate the future value of a regular investment plan. This education version uses a 20-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The Future value figure is the estimated total built up from your regular monthly payments plus the interest earned on them over 20 years.

Formula

Future value uses the monthly rate and number of monthly investments to grow each investment with interest.

How it works

Regular investing smooths timing and lets compounding build the value over time.

Worked example

A family saves $300 every month for 20 years at 8% a year for a child's education. The monthly rate is 8% divided by 12, which is 0.0066667, and the number of monthly investments is 20 times 12, which is 240. Using the future value formula, the future value is $300 times ((1.0066667 to the power of 240) minus 1) divided by 0.0066667, which is $176,706.12. Over 240 investments the family pays in $72,000.00, and interest adds $104,706.12, which is 59.3% of the future value. At 20 years interest passes $100,000 and is well ahead of deposits, so most of the fund is growth, not saving. For education planning this is a fund for more than one child, or for a degree plus living costs and postgraduate study, but it rests on 20 years of steady saving at a fixed rate.

Who this term suits for an education saver: a family planning for two children, or for one child plus a postgraduate degree, who starts saving while the children are young and keeps the plan going through school changes and fee rises.

FAQs

What is a SIP?
A SIP is a regular investment of a fixed amount, usually monthly, into a fund or plan.

Is this education calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does interest pass $100,000 at 20 years?
Because the earliest payments have compounded for 20 years, and each year's interest is earned on a larger and larger balance, so growth accelerates in the later years.

Is 20 years too long to forecast for education costs?
Yes, in the sense that courses, universities and fees will change, so a 20-year figure should be reviewed every few years, not trusted once and left alone.

Should education saving come before clearing a family loan?
Usually the loan comes first if its rate is higher than 8%, because paying it off is a guaranteed saving, while investment returns are not guaranteed.
