Home Inflation Adjusted Value Calculator (25 Years)

Estimate what an amount will be worth after inflation. This home version uses a 25-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The Future amount is the larger sum needed after 25 years to buy the same things as the starting amount buys today, so it shows how much rising prices reduce the buying power of your money over time.

Formula

Future amount equals amount times one plus inflation rate to the power of years.

How it works

Inflation means the same amount buys less over time, so future prices are higher.

Worked example

Amount $10,000, inflation rate 3 percent, term 25 years. Using the page formula, future amount equals $10,000 times 1.03 to the power of 25, which is $20,937.78. That is the calculator's own output for these defaults. The increase is $20,937.78 minus $10,000, which is $10,937.78, so a home cost priced at $10,000 today would need $20,937.78 in 25 years to buy the same thing if inflation ran at 3 percent every year. After 25 years the inflation alone is larger than the original sum.

Looked at the other way, $10,000 kept aside without any return would buy only about $4,776.06 worth of today's goods after 25 years at 3 percent inflation, because $10,000 divided by 1.03 to the power of 25 is $4,776.06. Less than half the buying power remains.

Who this term suits for a home household: a home household planning a retirement home or a major downsizing move in about 25 years, where today's $10,000 of moving, furnishing, or adaptation costs will need about $20,937.78 by then, and a fund that does not grow will cover less than half of it.

FAQs

What does inflation do to money?
Inflation reduces what money can buy, so a sum today will need to be larger in future to buy the same things.

Is this home calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why is the 25-year figure more than double the starting sum?
Because 3 percent compounding for 25 years multiplies the sum by about 2.09. The increase alone, $10,937.78, is larger than the original $10,000, which shows how quietly inflation builds over a quarter of a century.

Should a 25-year home fund just match inflation?
Matching inflation only holds buying power steady. A retirement home fund that merely keeps $10,000 in cash will still buy only about $4,776.06 of today's goods after 25 years, so the fund itself needs to grow to stay useful.

How often should a 25-year home plan be revisited?
Every few years at least. A 25-year figure assumes the same 3 percent every year, which will not happen, so the target should be recalculated as real prices, pay, and home plans change.
