Car Loan Total Interest Calculator (5 Years)

Estimate total interest paid over a loan. This car version uses a 5-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Total interest is the extra amount paid on top of the amount borrowed, and Total paid is the amount borrowed plus that interest, so together they show the full cost of the loan over its term.

Formula

Total interest equals monthly EMI times number of payments minus the principal.

How it works

A longer term lowers the monthly payment but raises total interest.

Worked example

Loan amount $200,000, annual rate 6.5%, term 5 years. First, the monthly rate is 6.5% divided by 12, which is 0.0054167, and the number of payments is 5 times 12, which is 60. Using the EMI formula, the monthly payment is $3,913.23. Over 60 payments the total paid is $234,793.78, calculated from the unrounded EMI exactly as the calculator does. Total interest is $234,793.78 minus $200,000, which is $34,793.78. That is 17.4% of the amount borrowed.

Who this term suits for a car buyer: A 5-year term suits a car buyer who wants a new, reliable car for daily driving and wants the loan finished before major repair bills arrive, for example a family buying a new car for commuting and school runs, or a driver who needs a dependable car for work and can carry $3,913.23 a month. The trade-off is clear: the monthly payment is high, but the total interest bill stays the smallest in this group, and the car is still worth a useful amount when the loan ends.

FAQs

Why does a longer loan cost more interest?
More payments mean interest is charged for longer, so total interest rises even if each payment is smaller.

Is this car calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is a 5-year car loan a good match for a new car?
For a new car, yes, it is the best match in this group. The loan ends in 60 payments while the car is still reliable and still worth a useful amount, and total interest is $34,793.78, the lowest in this group. The test is whether $3,913.23 a month fits in a slow month, not just a good month, after insurance, fuel and maintenance are paid.

How much does a car buyer save by choosing 5 years instead of 10 years?
On $200,000 at 6.5%, a 5-year loan costs $34,793.78 in interest and a 10-year loan costs $72,515.15, so the 5-year term saves $37,721.37. That saving is the reward for the higher monthly payment, and it frees cash sooner for the next car or for other costs.

Can a car buyer pay a 5-year car loan off early and sell the car?
That depends on the lender, because some car loans charge a prepayment fee. This calculator assumes no fees and a fixed 6.5% rate, so ask the lender about early-repayment terms before signing. If the car is sold before the loan ends, the loan balance must still be settled from the sale price, and the balance could be larger than the car's value in the early years.
