Education Loan EMI Calculator (15 Years)

Estimate the monthly EMI for a loan from amount, rate and term. This education version uses a 15-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The Monthly payment is the fixed amount to pay each month over 15 years, and the Total paid is the sum of all those monthly payments across the full term.

Formula

EMI equals principal times monthly rate times one plus monthly rate to the power of payments, divided by that power minus one.

How it works

This assumes a fixed rate and equal monthly payments with no extra fees.

Worked example

Loan amount $200,000, annual rate 6.5%, term 15 years. The monthly rate is 6.5% divided by 12, which is 0.0054167, and there are 180 monthly payments. Using the page formula, the EMI is $1,742.21 a month. Over 180 payments the total paid is $313,598.65, so interest is $113,598.65. Compared with 10 years, the EMI falls by $528.75 a month, but interest rises by $41,083.50, which is the price of five extra years of borrowing.

Who this term suits for an education borrower: a borrower balancing loan repayment with other mid-career costs, for example a graduate who also plans further study, a wedding, or a first home, and who needs a lower monthly payment even though the total cost rises.

FAQs

What is an EMI?
An EMI is a fixed monthly payment that covers both interest and principal over the loan term.

Is this education calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

When does a 15-year term make sense for an education loan?
When the 10-year EMI of $2,270.96 would strain monthly living costs, but the borrower still plans to clear the debt before retirement. The trade is explicit: about $529 a month less now, for $41,083.50 more interest overall.

How can a 15-year borrower cut the interest bill?
By paying extra in good years, such as from a bonus or a pay rise, because extra payments cut principal directly and shorten the interest tail. Even small regular overpayments matter more early, when the balance is highest.

Can a 15-year figure be used for a second degree?
Only as a fresh calculation. A second loan has its own amount, rate and start date, and running two education loans together means two EMIs, so the combined monthly burden must be checked, not just one page result.
