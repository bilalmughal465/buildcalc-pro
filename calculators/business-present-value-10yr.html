Business Present Value Calculator (10 Years)

Find how much a future amount is worth today. This business version uses a 10-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The Present value figure is what the future amount is worth in today's money at the rate and term entered, so it is the amount to compare with cash available today rather than with the future amount itself.

Formula

Present value equals future amount divided by one plus rate to the power of years.

How it works

A future amount is worth less today because money today can earn interest.

Worked example

Future amount $50,000, annual rate 5%, term 10 years. First, the discount factor is 1.05 to the power of 10, which is 1.628895. Present value is $50,000 divided by 1.628895, which is $30,695.66. The discount is $50,000 minus $30,695.66, which is $19,304.34, or 38.6% of the future amount. After 10 years only 61.4% of the value remains in today's money, so the business gives up more than one third of the sum by waiting a decade.

Who this term suits for a business: A 10-year term suits a business weighing a 10-year service contract or a machine it plans to sell or replace in 10 years. If a buyer promises $50,000 in 10 years, the business should compare it with $30,695.66 today, not with $50,000, because the decade of waiting removes $19,304.34 of present value at a 5% rate.

FAQs

What is present value?
Present value is what a future sum is worth today after allowing for interest over time.

Is this business calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does 10 years remove $19,304.34, almost double the 5-year discount?
Because the discount factor compounds: it is 1.276282 after 5 years but 1.628895 after 10 years. Present value therefore falls from $39,176.31 to $30,695.66, and the discount rises from $10,823.69 to $19,304.34. Each extra year removes a growing amount of present value, not a fixed amount.

Is a $50,000 receipt in 10 years worth $30,695.66 to every business?
No. That figure assumes a fixed 5% rate, no fees, no tax, and certain payment. A business with a higher cost of money would value it lower, and a business that doubts the payer will pay should value it lower still. Use $30,695.66 as a starting comparison, not as a guaranteed price.

How should a business compare a 10-year sum with a nearer sum?
Discount both sums to today at the same rate. A $50,000 sum in 10 years is $30,695.66 today, while a $40,000 sum in 5 years is $31,341.05 today at 5%, so the nearer, smaller sum is actually worth more today. Comparing face values alone would give the wrong answer.
