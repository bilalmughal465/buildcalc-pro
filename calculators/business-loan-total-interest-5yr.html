Business Loan Total Interest Calculator (5 Years)

Estimate total interest paid over a loan. This business version uses a 5-year planning term as its starting point.

—

In plain terms, the total interest is the extra cost of borrowing beyond the amount borrowed, and the total paid is the amount borrowed plus that interest over the full 5-year term.

Formula

Total interest equals monthly EMI times number of payments minus the principal.

How it works

A longer term lowers the monthly payment but raises total interest.

Worked example

Loan amount $200,000, annual rate 6.5%, term 5 years. First, the monthly rate is 6.5% divided by 12, which is 0.0054167, and the number of payments is 5 times 12, which is 60. Using the EMI formula, the monthly payment is $3,913.23. Over 60 payments the total paid is $234,793.78. Total interest is $234,793.78 minus $200,000, which is $34,793.78. That is 17.4% of the amount borrowed, the lowest total interest in this business year group, because the loan is cleared quickly and interest has little time to build up.

Who this term suits for a business: A 5-year term suits a business with steady monthly revenue that can carry $3,913.23 a month and wants the debt gone fast, for example a workshop buying machinery it expects to pay for itself within a few years, or a shop fitting out a new branch before the next lease renewal. The trade-off is clear: the monthly payment is high, but the total interest bill stays small, and cash flow must still cover slow months and stock purchases.

FAQs

Why does a longer loan cost more interest?
More payments mean interest is charged for longer, so total interest rises even if each payment is smaller.

Is this business calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is a 5-year business loan term too short for a $200,000 loan?
It can be, if $3,913.23 a month strains the business in slow months. Test the payment against the lowest recent monthly revenue, not the best month, and keep a cash buffer for stock and wages. If it does not fit, a longer term lowers the payment but raises total interest.

How much total interest does a business save by choosing 5 years instead of 10 years?
On the same $200,000 at 6.5%, a 5-year loan costs $34,793.78 in total interest and a 10-year loan costs $72,515.15, so the 5-year term saves $37,721.37 in interest. That saving is the reward for the higher monthly payment, and it frees cash flow sooner for reinvestment in the business.

Should a business treat total interest as a cost to minimise, or just look at the monthly payment?
A business should look at both, but total interest is the real cost of the loan. A low monthly payment can hide a very large total interest bill over a long term, so compare the total interest figures across terms before signing, not the monthly payment alone.
