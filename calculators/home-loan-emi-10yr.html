Home Loan EMI Calculator (10 Years)

Estimate the monthly EMI for a loan from amount, rate and term. This home version uses a 10-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

EMI equals principal times monthly rate times one plus monthly rate to the power of payments, divided by that power minus one.

How it works

This assumes a fixed rate and equal monthly payments with no extra fees.

Worked example

Loan amount $200,000, annual rate 6.5%, term 10 years. The monthly rate is 6.5% divided by 12, which is 0.0054167, and there are 120 monthly payments. Using the page formula, the EMI is $2,270.96 a month. Over 120 payments the total paid is $272,515.15, so interest is $72,515.15, which is 36.3% of the amount borrowed. Doubling the term from 5 to 10 years cuts the monthly payment by $1,642.27, but more than doubles the interest, because the balance stays higher for longer. For a home buyer this is a middle-length mortgage: the payment is much easier to live with, yet the house still costs $72,515.15 in interest. All figures assume a fixed 6.5% rate with no fees, taxes or changing rates, and are planning estimates, not promises.

Who this term suits for a home buyer: a young family buying a starter house or flat, who need a payment they can live with while raising children, and who plan to stay long enough to build equity, rather than a buyer who expects to move again within a year or two.

FAQs

What is an EMI?
An EMI is a fixed monthly payment that covers both interest and principal over the loan term.

Is this home calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does doubling the term from 5 to 10 years more than double the interest?
Because the balance falls more slowly. With lower payments, more of each early payment goes to interest and less to the balance, so interest keeps being charged on a larger amount for more years.

Is 10 years a usual horizon for a home loan?
Yes, it is a common middle choice for buyers who want the house paid off well before retirement without the very high payment of a 5-year term. It suits buyers with steady jobs and a plan to stay put.

What if a job is lost in year 3 of a 10-year home loan?
The EMI of $2,270.96 is still due every month, so an emergency fund covering several months of payments matters more than the term itself. A buyer should agree a safety buffer before signing, not after trouble starts.
