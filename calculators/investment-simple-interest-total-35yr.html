Investment Simple Interest Total Calculator (35 Years)

Calculate simple interest and the total amount to repay. This investment version uses a 35-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The Interest figure is the extra cost of borrowing over 35 years, and the Total figure is the original amount plus that interest, so the full amount to repay is the Total, not just the amount borrowed.

Formula

Simple interest equals principal times rate times time, divided by 100.

How it works

Interest is charged only on the original principal, not on interest already added.

Worked example

Principal $10,000, annual rate 5%, term 35 years. Using the page formula, simple interest equals $10,000 times 5 times 35, divided by 100, which is $17,500.00. Total to repay is $10,000 plus $17,500.00, which is $27,500.00. That is the calculator's own output for these defaults.

Because simple interest never compounds, the interest grows by the same $500 every year: after 35 years it is 35 times $500, which is $17,500.00. At 35 years the interest is $17,500, nearly twice the amount borrowed, so the plan only works if the investment itself grows far beyond the loan over the same period.

Who this term suits for an investor: an investor planning across generations, such as a property meant to pass to family, where the loan may still be running when the original investor retires and someone else must be ready to carry it.

FAQs

What is simple interest?
Simple interest is interest charged only on the original amount, not on accumulated interest.

Is this investment calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does the 35-year total reach $27,500?
Because 35 years at $500 a year is $17,500 of interest, plus the $10,000 principal, which is $27,500 in total, before any fees or taxes.

Who repays a 35-year investment loan if the original investor retires?
Whoever owns the investment then, which is why a 35-year plan needs a succession plan, not just a loan calculation.

Is 35 years too long to judge from one investment figure?
Yes, on its own: markets, rates, rents and tax rules will all change many times in 35 years, so this figure is a scale for planning, not a forecast.
