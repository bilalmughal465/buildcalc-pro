Shop Savings Goal Monthly Deposit Calculator (5 Years)

Find the monthly deposit needed to reach a savings goal. This shop version uses a 5-year planning term as its starting point.

—

This Monthly deposit is the fixed amount to save every month for 60 monthly deposits over 5 years, so your regular savings plus the interest earned reach your goal amount by the end of the term.

Formula

Monthly deposit equals goal times monthly rate divided by one plus monthly rate to the power of months minus one.

How it works

Saving a fixed amount each month with interest reaches the goal faster than saving without interest.

Worked example

Goal amount 50,000 dollars, annual rate 5 percent, term 5 years. The monthly rate is 5 percent divided by 12, which is about 0.4167 percent a month, and the number of deposits is 5 times 12, which is 60 months. Using the page formula, the monthly deposit is 50,000 dollars times the monthly rate, divided by one plus the monthly rate to the power of 60, minus one, which gives 735.23 dollars a month. That is the calculator output for these defaults. Total deposits are 735.23 dollars times 60 months, which is 44,113.70 dollars on an unrounded basis, so interest adds 5,886.30 dollars to reach the 50,000 dollar goal. Without interest the monthly amount would be 50,000 dollars divided by 60, which is 833.33 dollars a month, so interest saves about 98.10 dollars a month. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults, and it is the same goal and rate on every page in this group, with only the term changing.

Who this term suits for a shop owner: a shop saving for a near-term, known cost, such as a shop refit, a new till and stock system, or a shopfront refresh due in about 5 years, where the money must be ready before the old fit-out starts losing customers.

FAQs

How much should I save each month?
Divide the goal by the growth of regular deposits at the assumed rate to get the monthly amount.

Is this shop calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Can a shop really afford 735.23 dollars a month for 5 years?
Yes, if the shop sets the deposit as a fixed monthly cost, like rent, and reviews it against takings every quarter. If takings fall, the deposit should be cut early rather than missed, because missed months are hard to catch up at 5 percent.

Why does interest only add 5,886.30 dollars over 5 years?
Because compounding needs time. Over 60 months the deposits themselves do most of the work, and interest adds only the 5,886.30 dollars on top of the 44,113.70 dollars deposited.

What if shop takings drop before the 5 years are done?
Pause the goal, not the shop. Keep a smaller deposit going if possible, and extend the deadline rather than borrowing at a high rate to cover a refit that can wait.
