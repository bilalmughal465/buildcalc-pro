Wedding EMI Affordability Calculator (30 Years)

Estimate a safe monthly EMI from income. This wedding version uses a 30-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Safe EMI equals monthly income times safe share divided by 100.

How it works

Lenders often use a share of income as a guide, but other debts also matter.

Worked example

Monthly income $5,000, safe EMI share 30 percent, term 30 years. Using the page formula, the safe EMI is $5,000 times 30 divided by 100, which gives $1,500 a month. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults, and it is the same on every page in this group because the term is not an input: only the income and the safe share are entered. Against a 30-year planning window of 360 months, $1,500 a month adds up to $540,000 in total EMI capacity, as a labelled illustration only, not a second output of this page.

Who this term suits for a wedding: a couple comparing a wedding loan with a house mortgage over the same span, where the cap shows the wedding debt should never have rivalled the mortgage payment.

FAQs

How much EMI can I afford?
A common guide is 30 percent of monthly income, but total debts and living costs must also fit.

Is this wedding calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is a 30-year wedding loan the same as a house mortgage?
Only in length, never in sense. A mortgage buys an asset that usually holds value, while a wedding loan pays for a day that is gone. Over 360 months, $1,500 a month is $540,000 of total EMI capacity — a mortgage-scale sum for something with no resale value.

What happens if the couple stops earning for a time in a 30-year plan?
The $1,500 payment does not stop, and missed payments add charges and damage credit. That is why the safe share is only 30 percent: the other 70 percent of income must cover living costs and an emergency buffer, not another loan.

Should a couple borrow the full amount $1,500 a month can carry for 30 years?
No. The cap is a ceiling, not a target. Borrowing up to the ceiling for the longest possible term maximises interest and leaves no room for a mortgage, children, or emergencies. A wedding loan should be a small fraction of the cap, cleared in a few years.
