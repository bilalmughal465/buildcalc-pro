Business Loan EMI Calculator (5 Years)

Estimate the monthly EMI for a loan from amount, rate and term. This business version uses a 5-year planning term as its starting point.

—

In plain terms, the monthly payment is the fixed amount the business must pay every month for 5 years, and the total paid is the full amount paid by the end of the loan, including interest on top of the amount borrowed.

Formula

EMI equals principal times monthly rate times one plus monthly rate to the power of payments, divided by that power minus one.

How it works

This assumes a fixed rate and equal monthly payments with no extra fees.

Worked example

Loan amount $200,000, annual rate 6.5%, term 5 years. First, the monthly rate is 6.5% divided by 12, which is 0.0054167, and the number of payments is 5 times 12, which is 60. Using the EMI formula, the monthly payment is $3,913.23. Over 60 payments the total paid is $3,913.23 times 60, which is $234,793.78. Total interest is $234,793.78 minus $200,000, which is $34,793.78. That is 17.4% of the amount borrowed, the lowest total interest in this year group, because the loan is cleared quickly.

Who this term suits for a business: A 5-year term suits a business with steady monthly revenue that can carry $3,913.23 a month and wants the debt cleared fast, for example a workshop buying machinery it expects to pay for itself within a few years, or a shop fitting out a new branch before the next lease renewal. The trade-off is clear: the monthly payment is high, but the interest bill stays small, and cash flow must still cover slow months.

FAQs

What is an EMI?
An EMI is a fixed monthly payment that covers both interest and principal over the loan term.

Is this business calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is a 5-year business loan term too short for a $200,000 loan?
It can be, if $3,913.23 a month strains the business in slow months. Test the payment against the lowest recent monthly revenue, not the best month, and keep a cash buffer. If it does not fit, a longer term lowers the payment but raises total interest.

How much does a business save by choosing 5 years instead of 10 years?
On $200,000 at 6.5%, a 5-year loan costs $34,793.78 in interest and a 10-year loan costs $72,515.15, so the 5-year term saves $37,721.37. That saving is the reward for the higher monthly payment, and it frees cash flow sooner for reinvestment in the business.

Can a business repay a 5-year loan early?
That depends on the lender, because some business loans charge a prepayment fee. This calculator assumes no fees and a fixed 6.5% rate, so ask the lender about early-repayment terms and any fees before signing.
