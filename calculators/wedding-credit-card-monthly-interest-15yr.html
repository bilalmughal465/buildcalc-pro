Wedding Credit Card Monthly Interest Calculator (15 Years)

Estimate monthly interest on a card balance. This wedding version uses a 15-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Monthly interest equals balance times annual rate divided by 100 divided by 12.

How it works

Paying only interest leaves the balance unchanged. Paying more than interest reduces the balance.

Worked example

Balance $5,000, annual rate 18%, term 15 years. Using the page formula, the monthly interest is $5,000 times 18 divided by 100 divided by 12, which gives $75.00 a month. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults, and it is the same on every page in this group because the term is not an input: only the balance and the rate are entered. Paying only that $75 of interest leaves the balance unchanged, so over 180 months the interest paid is 180 times $75, which is $13,500, and the $5,000 is still owed at the end. This total is an illustration only, assuming the rate stays at 18% and no payments beyond interest are made; it is not a second output of this page.

Who this term suits for a wedding: a couple who married young and carried part of the wedding cost on a card, such as a venue upgrade or a guest list that grew, and who now need a plan that clears it before the children start school.

FAQs

Why is card interest high?
Card rates are usually higher than loan rates, so balances grow quickly if only minimum payments are made.

Is this wedding calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

What does $13,500 over 15 years mean for a wedding budget?
It means the wedding cost $18,500 in total for $5,000 of card spending, with the $5,000 still owed at the end. A couple planning a wedding should treat that $13,500 as money that could have paid for a honeymoon, a deposit, or a home upgrade.

Should wedding extras ever stay on a card for 15 years?
No. Extras such as flowers, a cake, or a photographer are one-day costs. Paying card interest on them for 15 years means paying for the wedding long after the day is forgotten, at nearly three times the original price.

How does a growing family change a 15-year card plan?
Children, a mortgage, and school costs all compete for the same monthly money, so a card balance carried for 15 years usually gets pushed aside and keeps growing. Clearing it before family costs rise is almost always cheaper.
