Medical Return on Investment Calculator (5 Years)

Calculate return on investment as a percentage. This medical version uses a 5-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The Return is the total percentage gain or loss on the amount invested over the whole period, found by comparing the final value with the amount invested: a positive Return means the final value is higher than the amount invested, a negative Return means it is lower, and it is not a yearly rate.

Formula

Return equals final value minus invested amount, divided by invested amount, times 100.

How it works

A positive return means the final value is higher than the amount invested.

Worked example

Amount invested $10,000, final value $15,000, term 5 years. Using the page formula, return equals $15,000 minus $10,000, divided by $10,000, times 100, which is $5,000 divided by $10,000, times 100, which is 50.00 percent. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults.

That 50.00 percent is the same on every page in this group, because the term is not an input on this page: only the amount invested and the final value decide the answer. The yearly pace is different, though. As an illustration only, not the output of this page, spreading the 50.00 percent evenly over 5 years gives a simple average of 10.00 percent a year. That average is not compounded and it is not what the calculator outputs; it only shows why the same total return looks weaker the longer it takes to earn.

Who this term suits for a medical investor: a newly opened dental clinic buying its first chair, a GP practice fitting out one treatment room, or a small physiotherapy clinic buying basic equipment, where the return must be judged over a short, defined period before the equipment itself needs replacing.

FAQs

What is a good ROI?
There is no single good figure. Compare returns over the same period and with the same risk before deciding.

Is this medical calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is 50% a good return for a medical investment over only 5 years?
It depends on the risk and the period. A 50% total return over 5 years averages 10.00% a year on a simple basis, which is a strong result for a clinic investment, but only if the final value of $15,000 is realistic after fees, taxes, and equipment wear.

Why does the same 50% look stronger over 5 years than over longer terms?
Because the gain of $5,000 is earned in only 5 years. Spread over 5 years it averages 10.00% a year, while the same $5,000 spread over 40 years averages only 1.25% a year, so the period always matters when comparing returns.

Should a clinic judge a 5-year medical investment on ROI alone?
No. ROI ignores when the money comes back, what happens to patient numbers, and whether the equipment still works at year 5. Use it beside cash flow, running costs, and how long the equipment will last.
