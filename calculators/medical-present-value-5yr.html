Medical Present Value Calculator (5 Years)

Find how much a future amount is worth today. This medical version uses a 5-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Present value equals future amount divided by one plus rate to the power of years.

How it works

A future amount is worth less today because money today can earn interest.

Worked example

Future amount $50,000, annual rate 5%, term 5 years. Using the page formula, present value equals $50,000 divided by 1.05 to the power of 5, which is $39,176.31. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults. The discount is $50,000 minus $39,176.31, which is $10,823.69, so time and interest remove $10,823.69 of value between today and year 5.

In plain terms, a medical bill of $50,000 due in 5 years is worth $39,176.31 today at 5%. Setting aside $39,176.31 now, growing at 5% a year, would reach $50,000 when the bill falls due, before fees and taxes.

Who this term suits for a medical household: a family planning a known treatment in about 5 years, such as a planned surgery, a course of dental work, or a fertility treatment, where the cost is quoted today but will be paid later and the family wants to know what to set aside now.

FAQs

What is present value?
Present value is what a future sum is worth today after allowing for interest over time.

Is this medical calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is $39,176.31 what I should set aside today for a $50,000 medical bill in 5 years?
Yes, at the page defaults: $50,000 divided by 1.05 to the power of 5 is $39,176.31. If the treatment costs more than $50,000, or the return is lower than 5%, you would need to set aside more.

Why is the discount only $10,823.69 over 5 years?
Because 5 years is short for discounting: 1.05 to the power of 5 is only 1.2763, so most of the $50,000 value is still there today. The discount grows much faster over longer terms.

What if medical prices rise faster than 5% over the 5 years?
Then $50,000 will not be enough when the bill arrives, because medical inflation often runs above general inflation. Re-check the quoted treatment price before relying on this figure.
