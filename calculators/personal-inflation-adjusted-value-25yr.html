Personal Inflation Adjusted Value Calculator (25 Years)

Estimate what an amount will be worth after inflation. This personal version uses a 25-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Future amount equals amount times one plus inflation rate to the power of years.

How it works

Inflation means the same amount buys less over time, so future prices are higher.

Worked example

Amount $10,000, inflation rate 3%, term 25 years. First, the factor is 1.03 to the power of 25, which is 2.093778. Future amount is $10,000 times 2.093778, which is $20,937.78. That is $10,937.78 more than today, or 109.4% higher, so the same basket would cost more than double, $20,937.78, in 25 years at 3% inflation.

Who this term suits for a person: A 25-year term suits a person planning across a generation, for example a young parent funding a child's university and early adult costs, or a worker starting a long retirement fund. The crossing point matters: after 25 years the price rise alone is larger than the original $10,000, so the plan must be sized for $20,937.78, not $10,000.

FAQs

What does inflation do to money?
Inflation reduces what money can buy, so a sum today will need to be larger in future to buy the same things.

Is this personal calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does the rise pass the original amount at 25 years?
Because the factor passes 2: 1.03 to the power of 25 is 2.093778, so $10,000 becomes $20,937.78 and the rise is $10,937.78. That doubling is the point where inflation, even at a modest 3%, has done more damage than the starting sum itself.

How do I keep saving for 25 years through job changes?
By treating the future-price target as the goal and reviewing it, not by fixing a monthly amount once. A job change, a move, or a family change does not change the $20,937.78 price, but it changes what a person can save, so the plan must be re-run after each change.

Should the $10,000 goal rise with inflation over 25 years?
Yes, if the goal is a real basket of goods. Keeping the target at $10,000 in nominal terms means accepting about half the basket in 25 years, since $10,000 will buy only what about $4,776 buys today at a factor of 2.093778.
