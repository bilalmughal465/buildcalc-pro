Wedding Break Even Units Calculator (40 Years)

Find how many units must be sold to break even. This wedding version uses a 40-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Break even units equal fixed costs divided by price minus variable cost per unit.

How it works

At break even, total revenue exactly covers total costs, with no profit and no loss.

Worked example

Fixed costs $10,000, price per unit $50, variable cost per unit $30, term 40 years. The contribution per unit is $50 minus $30, which is $20. Dividing $10,000 by $20 gives 500 break even units. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults, and it is the same on every page in this group because the term is not an input: only fixed costs, price and variable cost are entered. At 500 units, revenue is 500 times $50, which is $25,000, and total cost is $10,000 plus 500 times $30, which is also $25,000, so profit is zero. As an illustration only, spreading 500 units over 40 years means about 12.5 units a year, or about 1.04 a month, on average.

Who this term suits for a wedding: nobody as an ordinary wedding plan, because a 40-year horizon is a whole working life; its use is as a warning illustration that even a slow pace of about 12.5 units a year reaches 500 units over 40 years, but no wedding business should be trusted for 40 years without regular review.

FAQs

What is break even?
Break even is the point where revenue covers all costs, so profit is zero.

Is this wedding calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is 12.5 units a year over 40 years actually a wedding business plan?
Not as a working plan. 12.5 units a year for 40 years reaches 500 units, but a 40-year horizon is a whole working life, so it should be read as an illustration of how slowly a total can build, not as a plan to follow.

What does 40 years mean for wedding prices and costs?
That both will change many times. A $50 price and a $30 variable cost will not hold for 40 years, so the 500-unit result must be recalculated whenever prices or costs move.

What should a wedding business check before trusting a 40-year break-even figure?
Current fixed costs, current price, current variable cost, and realistic bookings for the next season. If those do not support the $20 contribution, the 500-unit total does not hold either.
