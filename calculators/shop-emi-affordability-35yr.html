Shop EMI Affordability Calculator (35 Years)

Estimate a safe monthly EMI from income. This shop version uses a 35-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Safe EMI equals monthly income times safe share divided by 100.

How it works

Lenders often use a share of income as a guide, but other debts also matter.

Worked example

Monthly shop income $5,000, safe EMI share 30 percent, term 35 years. Using the page formula, safe EMI equals $5,000 times 30 divided by 100, which is $1,500 per month. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults, and it is the same on every page in this group because the term is not an input.

As an illustration only, paying $1,500 every month for 35 years would total $630,000 in monthly payments. That total is not the loan amount you can borrow, because this page does not include interest, fees, or a changing rate, and shop takings will not stay at $5,000 every month for 35 years.

Who this term suits for a shop owner: a shop owner thinking about succession, such as a family shop that may pass to children or be sold, where the EMI must be judged against who will actually be trading and paying in the later years.

FAQs

How much EMI can I afford?
A common guide is 30 percent of monthly income, but total debts and living costs must also fit.

Is this shop calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does the total matter more than the $1,500 headline at 35 years?
Because 35 years of $1,500 payments total $630,000, and that scale is what the shop really commits to. A monthly figure that looks small can still add up to far more than the original shop investment once the term is this long.

Who repays a 35-year shop loan if the original owner retires?
Whoever owns or runs the shop then, whether family, a buyer, or a new tenant. A loan that outlives its borrower needs a succession plan agreed early, not discovered when the owner steps back.

Is 35 years too long to judge from one shop income figure?
Yes, on its own. No shop keeps $5,000 monthly takings for 35 years, so the figure should be used only to compare loans today, alongside a realistic view of leases, refits, and market change.
