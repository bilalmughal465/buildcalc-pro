Wedding Retirement Corpus Calculator (5 Years)

Estimate a retirement corpus from monthly savings. This wedding version uses a 5-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The Future value shown is the total amount built up after 5 years of monthly saving, including growth from the annual rate, so it is the sum you would have available at the end of that period, not just the monthly payments added together.

Formula

Corpus uses the monthly rate and number of monthly savings to grow each saving with interest.

How it works

Starting earlier matters more than saving a little more later, because interest has longer to compound.

Worked example

Monthly saving $500, annual rate 7 percent, term 5 years. The monthly rate is 7 percent divided by 12, which is about 0.5833 percent a month, and the number of savings is 5 times 12, which is 60 months. Using the page formula, the future value is $500 times one plus the monthly rate to the power of 60, minus one, divided by the monthly rate, which gives $35,796.45. That is the calculator output for these defaults. The couple pays in $30,000 in total, so $5,796.45 of the corpus is interest. That is the same monthly saving and rate as on every page in this group; only the time input changes, and that alone produces the different corpus figures across the years.

Who this term suits for a wedding: a couple only a few years from the wedding who need a short, visible fund for the wedding itself and the first years of married life, where 5 years of saving is a bridge, not a whole retirement plan.

FAQs

When should I start saving for retirement?
As early as possible. Time in the market allows compounding to do more of the work.

Is this wedding calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is $500 a month for 5 years enough for a wedding fund?
For these defaults it builds a corpus of $35,796.45, which covers a modest wedding and leaves a start for married life, but it is not a full retirement fund. A larger wedding needs a larger monthly saving or a longer term.

Why is the interest only $5,796.45 over 5 years?
Because compounding needs time. Over 60 months the $30,000 paid in earns $5,796.45 at 7 percent. The same $500 a month over 10 years earns $26,542.40, more than four times as much, because interest starts earning interest of its own.

What if the couple saves $600 a month instead of $500?
The formula scales with the monthly saving, so $600 a month over 5 years at 7 percent gives $42,955.74. That is $7,159.29 more than the $35,796.45 from $500 a month.
