Home Debt Payoff Months Calculator (35 Years)

Estimate months to pay off a debt with fixed payments. This home version uses a 35-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The result, labelled Months to pay off, is the number of months needed to clear the balance at the entered monthly payment and annual rate, so a larger payment shortens this time and a smaller payment lengthens it; if the payment does not cover the monthly interest, the calculator instead shows Payment is too low to pay off this debt.

Formula

Months are found from the balance, monthly rate and monthly payment using the loan payoff formula.

How it works

If the payment is less than monthly interest, the balance will not fall.

Worked example

Balance $10,000, annual rate 12 percent, monthly payment $300, term 35 years. The monthly rate is 12 percent divided by 12, which is 1 percent a month, so the first month of interest on $10,000 is $100. A $300 payment covers that $100 of interest and puts $200 toward the balance in the first month. Using the loan payoff formula, the months to pay off are 40.7. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults, and it is the same on every page in this group because the term is not an input: only the balance, the rate and the monthly payment are entered. Over 40.7 months at $300 a month, the total paid is about $12,225, so the interest paid is about $12,225 minus $10,000, which is $2,225. Against a 35-year planning window of 420 months, the debt is cleared about 379.3 months before the window ends, as a comparison only, not as a second output of this page. All figures are estimates for planning only, as the page disclaimer states, and they assume the rate stays at 12 percent and every $300 payment is made on time.

Who this term suits for a home household: nobody as an ordinary borrowing plan, because a 35-year window is far longer than this debt needs: the useful warning here is that paying only the minimum for decades on a home debt would cost far more than $2,225 of interest, so a household should check that its real payment is at least $300 and not just the interest.

FAQs

What if my payment is too low?
If the payment does not cover monthly interest, the debt will grow instead of shrinking.

Is this home calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does the payoff still take only 40.7 months at 35 years?
Because the term is not an input on this page: only the $10,000 balance, the 12 percent rate and the $300 payment decide the answer. The 35-year window only frames the comparison, leaving about 379.3 months after the debt is cleared.

Who repays a 35-year home debt if the original borrower retires?
This balance would be repaid long before retirement on these defaults, in about 41 months, so the real question for a 35-year horizon is whether larger home debts, like a mortgage, are cleared before retirement, not this $10,000 balance.

Is 35 years too long to judge from one household payment figure?
Yes, income, rates and home costs all change over 35 years, so a single $300 figure is only a starting point. The page is useful for this one balance today, and the figures should be re-entered whenever the real numbers change.
