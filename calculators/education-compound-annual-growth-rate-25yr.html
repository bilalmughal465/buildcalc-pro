Education Compound Annual Growth Rate Calculator (25 Years)

Calculate compound annual growth over a period. This education version uses a 25-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The CAGR result is the steady yearly rate that would take your starting value to your ending value over 25 years, if growth were perfectly smooth every year. It smooths out uneven years into a single yearly figure, so it describes the past window you entered rather than promising the same rate in the future.

Formula

CAGR equals ending value divided by starting value, to the power of one over years, minus one.

How it works

CAGR smooths growth into a single yearly rate, even if actual yearly growth varied.

Worked example

Starting value $10,000, ending value $20,000, term 25 years. First, the growth ratio is $20,000 divided by $10,000, which is 2.00. Using the page formula, CAGR is 2.00 to the power of one over 25, minus one, which is 2.81% a year. That is the steady yearly rate that would take $10,000 to $20,000 in 25 years, if growth were perfectly smooth every year.

Over 25 years the same doubling needs only 2.81% a year. A generation-long window flatters the total: the fund doubled, but the yearly pace is below what many fee bills rise by in a single year. Remember that CAGR smooths the path: the fund may have grown unevenly, with flat or negative years in between, and still show 2.81% a year. It describes the past window, it does not promise the next 25 years.

Who this term suits for an education saver: a university scholarship fund reviewed over 25 years, for example an alumni fund that doubled since it started, where the committee wants to know whether the yearly pace was strong or merely the result of a very long window.

FAQs

What does CAGR mean?
CAGR is the steady yearly growth rate that would take a starting value to an ending value.

Is this education calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is 2.81% a year over 25 years a strong result for an education endowment?
As a total it is a doubling, but as a yearly pace it is modest. Judge it against 25 years of fee rises and inflation: if costs rose faster, the fund lost real value despite doubling.

How should a 25-year education fund handle a bad decade?
By checking decade-by-decade rates, not just the 25-year figure. A bad decade early on matters less than a bad decade near the payout date, when there is little time to recover.

Should deposits rise if CAGR falls below 2.81%?
That depends on the goal. If the fund must meet a fixed fee bill, falling growth means top-ups or a lower target; the CAGR figure alone does not decide that, the remaining gap does.
