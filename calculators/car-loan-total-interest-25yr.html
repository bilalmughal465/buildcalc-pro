Car Loan Total Interest Calculator (25 Years)

Estimate total interest paid over a loan. This car version uses a 25-year planning term as its starting point.

—

This means the Total interest is the extra cost of borrowing beyond the loan amount, and the Total paid is the loan amount plus that interest over the full 25-year term.

Formula

Total interest equals monthly EMI times number of payments minus the principal.

How it works

A longer term lowers the monthly payment but raises total interest.

Worked example

Loan amount $200,000, annual rate 6.5%, term 25 years. First, the monthly rate is 6.5% divided by 12, which is 0.0054167, and the number of payments is 25 times 12, which is 300. Using the EMI formula, the monthly payment is $1,350.41. Over 300 payments the total paid is $405,124.30, calculated from the unrounded EMI exactly as the calculator does. Total interest is $405,124.30 minus $200,000, which is $205,124.30. That is 102.6% of the amount borrowed.

Who this term suits for a car buyer: A 25-year term does not suit a car buyer looking for value. In 25 years a buyer will pass through several cars, yet the payment of $1,350.41 a month continues through all of them, and total interest of $205,124.30 is already more than the $200,000 borrowed. Its only honest use is as a planning warning: if even this payment is hard, the loan amount or the car itself needs to change, because stretching the term cannot fix affordability without a very large interest bill.

FAQs

Why does a longer loan cost more interest?
More payments mean interest is charged for longer, so total interest rises even if each payment is smaller.

Is this car calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does interest pass the loan amount at 25 years?
Because 300 payments at $1,350.41 a month total $405,124.30, so total interest is $405,124.30 minus $200,000, which is $205,124.30, or 102.6% of the amount borrowed. The buyer pays back the whole loan again in interest, plus the loan itself. That crossing point is a useful warning: beyond about 25 years, interest alone is larger than the car loan it started from.

Is a 25-year car loan realistic when a car will be replaced several times?
No. In 25 years a buyer will own several cars, yet the first loan payment of $1,350.41 a month continues through all of them. The buyer would be paying for the first car long after it has been scrapped, while also funding its replacements. A 25-year term shows why a car loan should end well before the car's useful life ends, not decades after it.

What exit plan should a 25-year car borrower have?
A realistic exit plan is a shorter term, a smaller loan, or a larger deposit, agreed before signing, not after. Ask the lender about early repayment and any fees, because this calculator assumes no fees and a fixed 6.5% rate. Without an exit plan, a 25-year borrower is locked into $1,350.41 a month for 300 months, through job changes, moves and several car replacements.
