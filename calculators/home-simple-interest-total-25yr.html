Home Simple Interest Total Calculator (25 Years)

Calculate simple interest and the total amount to repay. This home version uses a 25-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The Interest figure is the total extra cost over the full term, and the Total figure is the original principal plus that interest, which is the full amount to repay by the end of the 25-year term.

Formula

Simple interest equals principal times rate times time, divided by 100.

How it works

Interest is charged only on the original principal, not on interest already added.

Worked example

Principal $10,000, annual rate 5%, term 25 years. Using the page formula, simple interest equals $10,000 times 5 times 25, divided by 100, which is $12,500.00. The total amount to repay is $10,000 plus $12,500.00, which is $22,500.00. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults. Simple interest adds the same $500 every year, so the interest grows in a straight line: 25 years times $500 is $12,500.00.

Who this term suits for a home household: a home household looking at a mortgage-length horizon of about 25 years, where the interest, at $12,500, is now larger than the amount borrowed, and where the loan will likely outlast the roof, boiler, and kitchen it helped pay for.

FAQs

What is simple interest?
Simple interest is interest charged only on the original amount, not on accumulated interest.

Is this home calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why is the interest now more than the amount borrowed?
Because 25 years at 5 percent simple interest is 125 percent of the principal: 25 times $500 is $12,500. The total to repay is $22,500, which is two and a quarter times the original $10,000.

Does a 25-year home loan really cost $22,500?
Only under this page's exact terms: $10,000, a fixed 5 percent, simple interest, no fees, and no rate changes. A real 25-year home loan almost always costs more, because most mortgages compound and add fees.

How should a home family review a 25-year loan?
At least every few years: check the remaining balance, the current rate, and whether refinancing or extra payments would cut the total. A figure set today will not stay right for 25 years on its own.
