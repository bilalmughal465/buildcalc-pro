Education Savings Goal Monthly Deposit Calculator (10 Years)

Find the monthly deposit needed to reach a savings goal. This education version uses a 10-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Monthly deposit equals goal times monthly rate divided by one plus monthly rate to the power of months minus one.

How it works

Saving a fixed amount each month with interest reaches the goal faster than saving without interest.

Worked example

A family wants $50,000 for university fees in 10 years, at 5% a year. The monthly rate is 5% divided by 12, which is 0.0041667, and there are 120 monthly deposits. Using the page formula, the monthly deposit is $50,000 times 0.0041667, divided by 1.0041667 to the power of 120 minus 1, which is $321.99 a month. Over 120 deposits the family pays in $38,639.31, and interest adds $11,360.69, which is 22.7% of the goal. Doubling the term from 5 to 10 years cuts the monthly deposit by $413.24, because the family has twice as long to save and interest has twice as long to compound. For an education saver this is a practical middle plan: a monthly amount many households can manage, for a child still in primary or early secondary school.

Who this term suits for an education saver: a parent of a younger child, for example a family whose child is in primary school, who wants a steady, affordable monthly habit for university fees rather than a last-minute scramble in the final school years.

FAQs

How much should I save each month?
Divide the goal by the growth of regular deposits at the assumed rate to get the monthly amount.

Is this education calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does doubling the term more than halve the monthly deposit?
Two effects combine: the same $50,000 is spread over 120 deposits instead of 60, and interest has 10 years to compound instead of 5, adding $11,360.69 rather than $5,886.30.

Is $321.99 a month enough if university fees rise faster than 5% a year?
No, not on its own. This page fixes the goal at $50,000. If fees rise faster than 5% a year, the real goal in 10 years will be higher, so the family should review the goal every year or two.

Should the deposit rise as family income grows over 10 years?
Yes, if pay rises. Raising the deposit with income protects the plan against fee rises and can finish the goal early, which leaves room for living costs or a second qualification.
