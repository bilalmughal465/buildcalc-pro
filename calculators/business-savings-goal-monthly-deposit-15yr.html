Business Savings Goal Monthly Deposit Calculator (15 Years)

Find the monthly deposit needed to reach a savings goal. This business version uses a 15-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Monthly deposit equals goal times monthly rate divided by one plus monthly rate to the power of months minus one.

How it works

Saving a fixed amount each month with interest reaches the goal faster than saving without interest.

Worked example

Goal $50,000, annual rate 5%, term 15 years. The monthly rate is 5% divided by 12, which is 0.0041667, and the number of payments is 15 times 12, which is 180. Using the monthly deposit formula, the monthly deposit is $187.06. Over 180 payments the business deposits $33,671.43 in total, and interest adds $16,328.57 to reach the $50,000 goal. Interest now covers 32.7% of the goal, so the business funds about two thirds of the fund itself.

Who this term suits for a business: A 15-year term suits a business tied to a long premises cycle, for example a restaurant saving for a major refit at the next lease renewal, or a retailer saving for a deposit on its own shop. The monthly amount is low enough to run alongside normal costs, but the business must be sure it will still want the same goal in 15 years.

FAQs

How much should I save each month?
Divide the goal by the growth of regular deposits at the assumed rate to get the monthly amount.

Is this business calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

How does a 15-year term balance the monthly amount and the interest?
It cuts the monthly deposit to $187.06 while letting interest reach $16,328.57, so the business pays less each month but waits longer. The trade-off is time: the fund is not ready for 15 years, and the goal may change before then.

What if the rate falls below 5% over 15 years?
A lower rate means the same $187.06 a month will fall short of $50,000, because interest would add less than $16,328.57. The calculator assumes a fixed 5% rate, so the business should recheck the plan whenever rates change and raise the deposit if needed.

Can a business use a 15-year fund for a lease renewal?
Yes, if the renewal date and the likely refit cost are known in advance. The business should set the goal from the expected renewal cost, not from a round number, and keep the fund separate from working cash so it is not spent on day-to-day costs.
