Business Inflation Adjusted Value Calculator (5 Years)

Estimate what an amount will be worth after inflation. This business version uses a 5-year planning term as its starting point.

—

In real terms, the Future amount is the sum you would need after 5 years at the entered inflation rate to buy the same things that the original amount buys today.

Formula

Future amount equals amount times one plus inflation rate to the power of years.

How it works

Inflation means the same amount buys less over time, so future prices are higher.

Worked example

Amount $10,000, inflation rate 3%, term 5 years. The growth factor is 1.03 to the power of 5, which is 1.159274. The future amount is $10,000 times 1.159274, which is $11,592.74. That is $1,592.74 more than today, a rise of 15.9%. In plain terms, a basket of business costs that is $10,000 today would need $11,592.74 after 5 years at a steady 3% inflation to buy the same things. This is the future nominal amount needed, not a present value, and it assumes inflation stays at 3% every year, with no change in what the business buys.

Who this term suits for a business: a shop, cafe, or small wholesaler repricing its menu or price list and renegotiating a short supplier contract, where a 5-year inflation figure keeps prices realistic without guessing decades ahead.

FAQs

What does inflation do to money?
Inflation reduces what money can buy, so a sum today will need to be larger in future to buy the same things.

Is this business calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

How should a small business use a 5-year inflation figure when setting prices?
Use it as a planning guide for the next few price reviews, not as a fixed price rise. If costs of $10,000 today need $11,592.74 in 5 years at 3%, the business should plan small, regular price increases and check supplier prices each year, rather than one large jump at the end.

Does $11,592.74 mean my costs will rise exactly that much in 5 years?
No. It is the result of a steady 3% every year. Real inflation varies year to year, and some costs, like rent or wages, may rise faster or slower than 3%. Treat $11,592.74 as a central planning figure and keep a margin for years when inflation is higher.

Should a 5-year supplier contract include an inflation clause?
It is worth asking for one. A fixed price for 5 years looks safe, but if the supplier faces the same $1,592.74 rise on every $10,000 of costs, they may cut quality or add charges later. A clear annual adjustment linked to an agreed index protects both sides.
