Business SIP Future Value Calculator (20 Years)

Estimate the future value of a regular investment plan. This business version uses a 20-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Future value uses the monthly rate and number of monthly investments to grow each investment with interest.

How it works

Regular investing smooths timing and lets compounding build the value over time.

Worked example

Monthly investment $300, annual rate 8%, term 20 years. First, the monthly rate is 8% divided by 12, which is 0.0066667, and the number of monthly investments is 20 times 12, which is 240. Using the future value formula, the future value is $300 times ((1.0066667 to the power of 240) minus 1) divided by 0.0066667, which is $176,706.12. Over 240 investments the business pays in $72,000.00, and interest adds $104,706.12, which is 59.3% of the future value. By year 20 interest has passed the total paid in, which is why a steady plan looks very different over two decades than over five years.

Who this term suits for a business: an established business building a long-term fund, for example a partner buyout reserve, a succession fund for a family firm, or a multi-site expansion pot, where the owner is planning beyond the next lease and wants the fund to grow steadily in the background.

FAQs

What is a SIP?
A SIP is a regular investment of a fixed amount, usually monthly, into a fund or plan.

Is this business calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does interest pass $100,000 at 20 years?
Because the balance is now large enough that 8% a year adds more each year than the $3,600 a year of deposits. Over 240 months the business pays in $72,000.00, but interest adds $104,706.12, giving $176,706.12 in total.

Is 20 years too long to forecast for a business?
As a fixed forecast, yes. No business can assume the same rate, fund, ownership and trading conditions for 20 years. As a planning exercise it is still useful, provided the owner reviews the fund at each lease renewal and adjusts the monthly sum as profits change.

Should business saving come before clearing other debts?
Not automatically. Expensive debts, such as high-rate card balances, usually cost more than 8% a year, so clearing them first can save more than a SIP earns. The owner should compare the debt rate with the assumed 8% before choosing, and this page does not make that choice for them.
