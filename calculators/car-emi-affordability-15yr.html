Car EMI Affordability Calculator (15 Years)

Estimate a safe monthly EMI from income. This car version uses a 15-year planning term as its starting point.

—

This result is the monthly EMI that stays within the safe share of your income you entered, so keeping a car loan payment at or below this amount leaves the rest of your income for living costs and other debts.

Formula

Safe EMI equals monthly income times safe share divided by 100.

How it works

Lenders often use a share of income as a guide, but other debts also matter.

Worked example

Monthly income $5,000, safe EMI share 30 percent, term 15 years. First, the safe EMI is $5,000 times 30 divided by 100, which is $1,500 a month. The number of payments is 15 times 12, which is 180, so the total EMI outflow is $1,500 times 180, which is $270,000. That safe EMI of $1,500 does not change with the term, because the page formula uses only income and safe share. As an illustration only, not the output of this page, a $1,500 payment could support about $172,194.62 over 15 years at 6.5 percent, with about $97,805.38 of interest, so the total paid would be $270,000. The monthly payment is still $1,500, but the total outflow is now $270,000, three times the 5-year total, while the supportable loan has grown by only $40,091.87 since 10 years.

Who this term suits for a car buyer: A 15-year term suits almost no ordinary car buyer, and only a buyer with a very long ownership plan, for example someone buying a durable vehicle and planning to maintain it for 15 years, or a collector-type buyer who keeps a car far longer than average. The buyer must budget for several rounds of repairs, replacement parts, and rising insurance and fuel costs, because the $1,500 payment continues long after the car has lost most of its value.

FAQs

How much EMI can I afford?
A common guide is 30 percent of monthly income, but total debts and living costs must also fit.

Is this car calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is a 15-year car loan too long when cars lose value and wear out?
For an ordinary car, yes. After 15 years most cars have lost most of their value and need major work, yet the $1,500 payment would still be running, with a total outflow of $270,000. A 15-year term only makes sense for a very long ownership plan with a full maintenance budget.

Why does the supportable loan grow more slowly after 15 years?
Each extra 5 years adds a flat $90,000 to total outflow, but buys less and less extra loan, because more of each later payment goes to interest. From 10 years to 15 years the illustration loan grows by $40,091.87, and the growth keeps shrinking after that.

What happens to a car loan if the car is sold or written off before 15 years?
The loan does not end with the car. The buyer may still owe payments, or may owe more than the car is worth, and insurance may not cover the full balance. That risk is why a long car term needs an exit plan, including insurance cover and a plan for the remaining balance.
