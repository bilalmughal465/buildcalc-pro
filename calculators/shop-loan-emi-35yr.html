Shop Loan EMI Calculator (35 Years)

Estimate the monthly EMI for a loan from amount, rate and term. This shop version uses a 35-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The monthly payment is the fixed amount due every month over 35 years, and the total paid is everything repaid across the full term, including both the borrowed amount and the interest charged on it.

Formula

EMI equals principal times monthly rate times one plus monthly rate to the power of payments, divided by that power minus one.

How it works

This assumes a fixed rate and equal monthly payments with no extra fees.

Worked example

Loan amount $200,000, annual rate 6.5%, term 35 years. The monthly rate is 6.5% divided by 12, which is about 0.5417% a month, and the number of monthly payments is 35 times 12, which is 420 payments. Using the page formula, the monthly EMI is $1,208.31, total paid is $1,208.31 times 420, which is $507,489.63, and total interest is $507,489.63 minus $200,000, which is $307,489.63. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults. By 35 years the shop pays back more than two and a half times what it borrowed.

Who this term suits for a shop owner: almost no ordinary shop plan, except a freehold premises purchase meant to pass to the next generation, where the monthly payment matters more than the total because the property, not the loan, is the long-term asset.

FAQs

What is an EMI?
An EMI is a fixed monthly payment that covers both interest and principal over the loan term.

Is this shop calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does the monthly payment matter more than the 35-year total?
It does not, but it feels that way. A $1,208.31 payment looks easy, while $307,489.63 of interest is the real price, and it is easy to ignore until the loan is nearly done.

Who repays a 35-year shop loan if the owner retires?
Whoever takes over the shop, or the sale of the premises. A 35-year loan signed at age 30 still runs at age 65, so succession must be part of the plan from the start.

Is 35 years too long to judge from one loan figure?
Yes. No shop, high street, or rate stays still for 35 years, so this figure is a warning about long debt, not a forecast any shop should trust unchanged.
