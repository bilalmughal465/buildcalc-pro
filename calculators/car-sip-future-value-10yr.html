Car SIP Future Value Calculator (10 Years)

Estimate the future value of a regular investment plan. This car version uses a 10-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The Future value is the total amount your regular monthly investments are expected to grow to by the end of 10 years at the rate you entered, so you can compare it with the price of the car you plan to buy.

Formula

Future value uses the monthly rate and number of monthly investments to grow each investment with interest.

How it works

Regular investing smooths timing and lets compounding build the value over time.

Worked example

Monthly investment $300, annual rate 8%, term 10 years. First, the monthly rate is 8% divided by 12, which is 0.0066667, and the number of monthly investments is 10 times 12, which is 120. Using the future value formula, the future value is $300 times ((1.0066667 to the power of 120) minus 1) divided by 0.0066667, which is $54,883.81. Over 120 investments you pay in $36,000.00, and interest adds $18,883.81, which is 34.4% of the future value. The 10-year result more than doubles the 5-year result, even though you only paid in twice as much, because the earlier payments have had a full decade to compound.

Who this term suits for a car owner: a driver planning one full car replacement cycle, for example saving through the life of the current car so that the next car can be bought with a much smaller loan, or bought outright if it is a used car, instead of starting the next loan from zero.

FAQs

What is a SIP?
A SIP is a regular investment of a fixed amount, usually monthly, into a fund or plan.

Is this car calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does 10 years more than double the 5-year result?
Because the payments from the first 5 years keep earning interest during the second 5 years, on top of the new payments. That extra compounding is why $54,883.81 is more than twice $22,043.06, even though the deposits only doubled.

Should I raise $300 a month as my pay rises over 10 years?
Yes, if you can, because car prices also rise over 10 years. A fixed $300 buys less car at year 10 than it would have at year 1, so raising the monthly amount when pay rises keeps the fund matched to real car prices.

Is $54,883.81 enough for a new car after 10 years?
It may be enough for a good used car or a large deposit on a new one, but probably not for a new car outright after 10 years of price rises. Treat it as a replacement fund, and check future prices rather than today's prices.
