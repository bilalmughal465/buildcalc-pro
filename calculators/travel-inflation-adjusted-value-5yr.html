Travel Inflation Adjusted Value Calculator (5 Years)

Estimate what an amount will be worth after inflation. This travel version uses a 5-year planning term as its starting point.

—

In plain terms, the Future amount is the sum you would need after 5 years to buy the same trip that costs the original amount today, so a higher figure means you should save or budget more if the trip is delayed.

Formula

Future amount equals amount times one plus inflation rate to the power of years.

How it works

Inflation means the same amount buys less over time, so future prices are higher.

Worked example

Amount 10,000 dollars, inflation rate 3 percent, term 5 years. Using the page formula, the future amount is 10,000 dollars times one plus 3 percent, to the power of 5, which is 11,592.74 dollars. That is the calculator output for these defaults. The increase over today's amount is 11,592.74 dollars minus 10,000 dollars, which is 1,592.74 dollars. That is a rise of about 15.93 percent in total over 5 years, which is why even a short travel delay costs real money.

Who this term suits for a traveller: a traveller saving for a trip that is only a few years away, such as a round-the-world trip, a honeymoon, or a campervan deposit, where flight and hotel prices in 5 years are close enough to today's prices to plan around.

FAQs

What does inflation do to money?
Inflation reduces what money can buy, so a sum today will need to be larger in future to buy the same things.

Is this travel calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does 10,000 dollars need 11,592.74 dollars in 5 years at 3 percent inflation?
Because 10,000 dollars times 1.03 to the power of 5 is 11,592.74 dollars. Each year prices rise 3 percent, and each rise applies to the already higher price, so the increase compounds to 1,592.74 dollars over 5 years.

Should a traveller raise a trip budget by 3 percent every year for 5 years?
As a planning rule, yes, because a fixed budget loses about 15.93 percent of its buying power over 5 years at 3 percent a year. A trip fund that is not topped up will buy a shorter trip, cheaper hotels, or fewer stops than planned.

What if travel prices rise faster than 3 percent over 5 years?
Then 11,592.74 dollars will not be enough. At 5 percent a year, the same trip would cost 12,762.82 dollars in 5 years, which is 1,170.08 dollars more, so a traveller should recheck flight and hotel prices before booking, not trust a single fixed rate.

Estimates only, not financial advice. Figures are approximate and do not include all fees, taxes, or changing rates unless stated.
