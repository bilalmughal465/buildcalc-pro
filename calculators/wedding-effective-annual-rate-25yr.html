Wedding Effective Annual Rate Calculator (25 Years)

Convert a nominal annual rate to an effective annual rate. This wedding version uses a 25-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The effective annual rate is the true yearly cost of borrowing after compounding is taken into account, so a higher effective rate means more interest is charged over a year than the nominal rate alone suggests.

Formula

Effective rate equals one plus nominal rate divided by periods, to the power of periods, minus one.

How it works

More frequent compounding raises the effective rate slightly above the nominal rate.

Worked example

Nominal rate 6%, compounded 12 times a year, term 25 years. The effective rate is one plus 0.06 divided by 12, to the power of 12, minus one, which gives 6.17%. That is the calculator's stated-formula result for these defaults, and it is the same on every page in this group because the term is not an input: only the nominal rate and the number of compounding periods are entered. On $10,000 for one year, the interest at the effective rate is $616.78, while at the nominal 6% it would be $600.00, so monthly compounding adds $16.78 in the first year. As an illustration only, $10,000 grown at the exact effective rate of 6.1678% for 25 years reaches $44,649.70, which is $34,649.70 of growth.

Who this term suits for a wedding: a family planning a wedding about twenty-five years ahead, where a small gap between the nominal and effective rate compounds into a large difference over time.

FAQs

What is an effective rate?
The effective rate is the true yearly rate after allowing for compounding during the year.

Is this wedding calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why is the 25-year illustration so much larger than the 5-year one?
Because compounding accelerates. At the exact effective rate of 6.1678%, $10,000 reaches $13,488.50 in five years but $44,649.70 in twenty-five years, which is $34,649.70 of growth. Each year's interest earns interest in later years, so the later years add far more than the early years.

Does a 25-year wedding loan really cost 6.17% every year?
Only while the nominal rate stays at 6% and compounding stays monthly. If the lender raises the nominal rate, the effective rate rises with it, and if compounding switches to yearly, the effective rate falls to 6.00%. A twenty-five-year plan should therefore be reviewed whenever the loan terms change.

What wedding risk grows over 25 years on a loan like this?
The risk that the loan outlives the wedding it paid for. A wedding is one day, but a twenty-five-year loan is a generation of payments, and the total interest on $10,000 at 6.1678% over twenty-five years is $34,649.70, more than three times the amount borrowed. Borrowing less, or repaying early, cuts that total sharply.
