Holiday Return on Investment Calculator (15 Years)

Calculate return on investment as a percentage. This holiday version uses a 15-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The "Return" percentage shows the total gain or loss on the amount invested, as a percentage of that amount, so a positive percentage means the final value is higher than the amount invested, and a negative percentage means it is lower.

Formula

Return equals final value minus invested amount, divided by invested amount, times 100.

How it works

A positive return means the final value is higher than the amount invested.

Worked example

Amount invested $10,000, final value $15,000, term 15 years. First, the gain is $15,000 minus $10,000, which is $5,000. Using the page formula, return is $5,000 divided by $10,000, times 100, which is 50.00%. That is the total return over the whole 15 years, not a yearly rate.

As an illustration only, not the output of this page, the annualised rate behind the same result is about 2.74% a year, because $15,000 divided by $10,000 is 1.50, and the 15th root of 1.50 is about 1.0274. Over 15 years the yearly pace looks modest, and that is the honest picture. A holiday fund held for this long will have seen several price rises, cancelled plans and changing tastes, so the total gain should be judged against what a trip costs now, not what it cost 15 years ago.

Who this term suits for a holiday saver: a family planning a big reunion or bucket-list trip about 15 years away, when children are grown and the whole family can travel together, checking whether a fund started long ago still covers a trip of that size.

FAQs

What is a good ROI?
There is no single good figure. Compare returns over the same period and with the same risk before deciding.

Is this holiday calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is 50.00% over 15 years still a win for a holiday fund?
It is a gain in money terms, but the win depends on travel prices. As an illustration only, about 2.74% a year over 15 years may trail a long run of rising fares and hotel rates, so check the cost of the planned trip today.

What holiday costs can eat into a 15-year return?
Fees, taxes, currency changes, and the cost of trips taken along the way. Any withdrawal for an earlier holiday reduces the amount left compounding, so the final figure alone does not tell the whole story.

Should a second holiday during the 15 years change how I read this figure?
Yes. If money was taken out for another trip, the 50.00% applies only to what stayed invested. Read it as the return on the money left in, not as a return on every pound or dollar ever set aside.
