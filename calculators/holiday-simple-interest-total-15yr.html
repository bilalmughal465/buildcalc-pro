Holiday Simple Interest Total Calculator (15 Years)

Calculate simple interest and the total amount to repay. This holiday version uses a 15-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Simple interest equals principal times rate times time, divided by 100.

How it works

Interest is charged only on the original principal, not on interest already added.

Worked example

Principal $10,000, annual rate 5%, term 15 years. Using the page formula, simple interest is $10,000 times 5 times 15, divided by 100, which is $7,500. The total amount is $10,000 plus $7,500, which is $17,500. That is the calculator's own output for these defaults.

After 15 years the interest, $7,500, is three quarters of the original sum, yet the yearly addition is still only $500. A holiday saver should notice the contrast: the total looks generous, but it took 15 years to build, and a holiday priced 15 years ago will almost certainly cost more than $17,500 by the time the trip is taken.

Who this term suits for a holiday saver: Parents saving over about 15 years for a big family holiday when the children are older, for example a first long-haul trip together, who want a fund that grows steadily while the children grow up.

FAQs

What is simple interest?
Simple interest is interest charged only on the original amount, not on accumulated interest.

Is this holiday calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

What does $7,500 of interest mean for a 15-year holiday plan?
It means the fund grows by $500 a year for 15 years, reaching $17,500 in total. That is a useful planning figure, but it is not a guarantee that $17,500 will buy the same holiday in 15 years that $10,000 buys today.

Should a 15-year holiday fund be split across several trips instead of one?
Often yes, because waiting 15 years for a single trip risks disappointment if prices or plans change. Taking smaller holidays along the way, while keeping the main fund growing, usually gives a family more travel for the same patience.

Does simple interest stay fair over 15 years when prices compound?
Not always, because holiday prices can compound while simple interest only adds a flat $500 a year. Over 15 years that gap matters, so compare this total with how travel prices have actually moved, not just with the interest rate.
