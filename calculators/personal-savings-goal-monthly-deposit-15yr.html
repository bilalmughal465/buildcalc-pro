Personal Savings Goal Monthly Deposit Calculator (15 Years)

Find the monthly deposit needed to reach a savings goal. This personal version uses a 15-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The Monthly deposit figure is the regular amount to set aside each month over the full 15 years, at the annual rate entered, in order to reach the goal amount. In practical terms, it turns a large distant target into a fixed monthly habit: paying that amount every month keeps the plan on track, while missed months or a lower rate would leave the total short of the goal.

Formula

Monthly deposit equals goal times monthly rate divided by one plus monthly rate to the power of months minus one.

How it works

Saving a fixed amount each month with interest reaches the goal faster than saving without interest.

Worked example

Goal $50,000, annual rate 5%, term 15 years. First, the monthly rate is 5% divided by 12, which is 0.0041667, and the number of payments is 15 times 12, which is 180. Using the monthly deposit formula, the monthly deposit is $187.06. Over 180 payments the deposits add up to $33,671.43, calculated from the unrounded deposit exactly as the calculator does, and interest adds $16,328.57 to reach the $50,000 goal. That interest is 32.7% of the goal.

Who this term suits for a person: A 15-year term suits a person building a house deposit or a long-term family fund who wants a balance between a manageable monthly amount and a reasonable wait, at $187.06 a month. It fits someone with steady work who can keep the habit through a house move or a job change, without tying the money up for a whole working life.

FAQs

How much should I save each month?
Divide the goal by the growth of regular deposits at the assumed rate to get the monthly amount.

Is this personal calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

How does a 15-year term balance the monthly amount and the interest?
At $187.06 a month, the payment is low enough for most household budgets, while interest still contributes $16,328.57, which is 32.7% of the goal. Total deposits are $33,671.43 over 180 payments. It is a middle path: cheaper than 5 or 10 years, but without the very long wait of 30 or 40 years.

What if the rate falls below 5% over 15 years?
If the rate falls, the same $187.06 a month would reach less than $50,000, because interest would add less than $16,328.57. This calculator assumes a fixed 5% rate for the whole term, which is unlikely in real life. Review the plan every few years and be ready to raise the monthly amount if the rate drops.

Can I use a 15-year personal fund for a house deposit?
Yes, a 15-year fund is often used for a house deposit, because 15 years gives time to save steadily without rushing. At $187.06 a month, the fund reaches $50,000 only if the 5% rate holds and every payment is made. House prices may also rise over 15 years, so check whether $50,000 will still be enough when you plan to buy.
