Home Future Value Monthly Savings Calculator (10 Years)

Estimate the future value of regular monthly savings. This home version uses a 10-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Future value uses the monthly rate and the number of monthly deposits to grow each deposit with interest.

How it works

Each monthly deposit earns interest from the month it is made until the end of the term.

Worked example

Monthly deposit $500, annual rate 6%, term 10 years. The monthly rate is 6% divided by 12, which is 0.5% a month, and the number of deposits is 10 times 12, which is 120 months. Using the page formula, the future value is $500 times one plus 0.005 to the power of 120, minus one, divided by 0.005, which gives $81939.67. That is the calculator's own output for these defaults.

The deposits themselves total $500 times 120, which is $60000. Interest therefore adds $81939.67 minus $60000, which is $21939.67. That interest is the reward for leaving every deposit invested for the full term; the earliest deposits earn interest for nearly the whole 10 years, while the last deposits earn for only a month.

Who this term suits for a home household: a home household building a solid home fund over a decade, for example saving toward a larger home, a major extension, or a mortgage deposit top-up in about 10 years — long enough for interest to add a real sum, but short enough that the goal still feels concrete.

FAQs

How does monthly saving grow?
Regular deposits grow because each deposit earns interest, and that interest then earns more interest.

Is this home calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is $500 a month realistic for a 10-year home goal?
For many home households it is a stretch but a workable one: $60,000 of deposits over 10 years, with $21,939.67 of interest on top. The plan should be checked against mortgage payments, utility bills, and home maintenance before it starts.

Why is the 10-year interest much larger than the 5-year interest?
Because deposits made early keep earning for up to 10 years, and the interest itself starts earning interest. Doubling the term more than quadruples the interest, from $4,885.02 to $21,939.67.

Should a home plan change if house prices rise over 10 years?
Yes. A $81,939.67 fund may buy less home in 10 years than it would today if prices rise faster than 6% a year. Review the goal amount every few years against real home prices, not just the calculator figure.
