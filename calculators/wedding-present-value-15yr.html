Wedding Present Value Calculator (15 Years)

Find how much a future amount is worth today. This wedding version uses a 15-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Present value equals future amount divided by one plus rate to the power of years.

How it works

A future amount is worth less today because money today can earn interest.

Worked example

Future amount $50,000, annual rate 5 percent, term 15 years. Using the page formula, the present value is $50,000 divided by 1.05 to the power of 15, which is $50,000 divided by 2.0789, giving $24,050.85. That is the calculator output for these defaults. The discount is $50,000 minus $24,050.85, which is $25,949.15, so the future wedding is worth 48.10 percent of its future amount in today's money.

Wedding use case: Parents building a wedding fund about 15 years ahead, who need to know how much today's money a $50,000 future wedding really represents, so the fund target stays honest.

FAQs

What is present value?
Present value is what a future sum is worth today after allowing for interest over time.

Is this wedding calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

How can a $50,000 wedding be worth only $24,050.85 today?
Because 15 years of discounting at 5 percent divides $50,000 by 2.0789, leaving just 48.10 percent of the future amount. More than half of the future budget, $25,949.15, is discount, not money you must have today.

Does a 15-year wedding fund need checking every year?
Yes. Over 15 years, returns, inflation and wedding prices all move, so a fund set from today's figure should be reviewed regularly and topped up if the assumed 5 percent return does not hold.

What if the return is 7 percent instead of 5 percent?
Then $50,000 in 15 years is worth $18,122.30 today, which is $5,928.55 less than at 5 percent. A higher assumed return lowers the amount needed today, but only if that return is actually earned.
