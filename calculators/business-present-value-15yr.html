Business Present Value Calculator (15 Years)

Find how much a future amount is worth today. This business version uses a 15-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Present value equals future amount divided by one plus rate to the power of years.

How it works

A future amount is worth less today because money today can earn interest.

Worked example

Future amount $50,000, annual rate 5%, term 15 years. First, the discount factor is 1.05 to the power of 15, which is 2.078928. Present value is $50,000 divided by 2.078928, which is $24,050.85. The discount is $50,000 minus $24,050.85, which is $25,949.15, or 51.9% of the future amount. At 15 years the value crosses half: less than half of the future sum, only 48.1%, is left in today's money.

Who this term suits for a business: A 15-year term suits a business signing a longer agreement, such as a 15-year franchise or fit-out deal with a payment due at the end. A $50,000 sum in 15 years is only $24,050.85 today, so any upfront cost, rent, or investment tied to that deal must be judged against $24,050.85, not against the full $50,000.

FAQs

What is present value?
Present value is what a future sum is worth today after allowing for interest over time.

Is this business calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why is $24,050.85 less than half of $50,000 after 15 years?
Because the discount factor after 15 years is 2.078928, which is more than 2. Dividing $50,000 by a factor above 2 must give less than $25,000, and here it gives $24,050.85. That is the point where waiting has removed 51.9% of the value, more than half.

What changes most if the rate is not 5% over 15 years?
The present value itself. At a higher rate the factor grows faster and $24,050.85 falls quickly, while at a lower rate it rises. Over 15 years a 1% change in the rate moves the result by thousands of dollars, so a business should test the calculation at its own low and high rate, not rely on 5% alone.

Can a 15-year present value be used for a lease decision?
Yes, as one input: a $50,000 payment due in 15 years should be compared with $24,050.85 today when weighing rent, fit-out costs, or an upfront payment. It cannot be the only input, because lease terms, tax, and the chance the payment changes must also be checked.
