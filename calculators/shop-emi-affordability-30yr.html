Shop EMI Affordability Calculator (30 Years)

Estimate a safe monthly EMI from income. This shop version uses a 30-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Safe EMI equals monthly income times safe share divided by 100.

How it works

Lenders often use a share of income as a guide, but other debts also matter.

Worked example

Monthly shop income $5,000, safe EMI share 30 percent, term 30 years. Using the page formula, safe EMI equals $5,000 times 30 divided by 100, which is $1,500 per month. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults, and it is the same on every page in this group because the term is not an input.

As an illustration only, paying $1,500 every month for 30 years would total $540,000 in monthly payments. That total is not the loan amount you can borrow, because this page does not include interest, fees, or a changing rate, and shop takings will not stay at $5,000 every month for 30 years.

Who this term suits for a shop owner: a shop owner comparing a very long premises commitment with a shorter one, where the EMI must be weighed against the risk that the shop, the street, or the owner will change long before the term ends.

FAQs

How much EMI can I afford?
A common guide is 30 percent of monthly income, but total debts and living costs must also fit.

Is this shop calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is a 30-year shop EMI the same as a premises mortgage?
No. This page only estimates a safe monthly EMI from income; it does not price a mortgage, include interest, or account for a deposit, fees, or property value. A real premises loan needs its own figures from the lender.

What happens if shop sales fall for several years in a 30-year plan?
The $1,500 guide stays the same on paper, but the shop's ability to pay falls. Several weak years can exhaust savings and force cuts to stock or staff, so a long plan needs a reserve built in from the start.

Should I borrow up to the full $1,500 for 30 years?
Borrowing to the full guide figure leaves no room for bad months, rent rises, or refits. Most shop owners are safer borrowing less than the guide allows, especially when the term is as long as 30 years.
