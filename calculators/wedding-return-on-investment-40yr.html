Wedding Return on Investment Calculator (40 Years)

Calculate return on investment as a percentage. This wedding version uses a 40-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The Return shown is the total gain on the amount invested over the whole period, expressed as a percentage of that invested amount, so a positive figure means the final value is above the amount invested and a negative figure means it is below it; it is not a yearly or annual rate.

Formula

Return equals final value minus invested amount, divided by invested amount, times 100.

How it works

A positive return means the final value is higher than the amount invested.

Worked example

Amount invested $10,000, final value $15,000, term 40 years. The gain is $15,000 minus $10,000, which is $5,000. Using the page formula, the return is $5,000 divided by $10,000, times 100, which gives 50.00%. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults, and it is the same on every page in this group because the term is not an input: only the amount invested and the final value are entered. Spread over 40 years, the same 50% total is equal to about 1.02% a year, because $10,000 compounded at 1.02% a year for 40 years reaches $15,000.

Who this term suits for a wedding: nobody as an ordinary wedding plan, because a 40-year horizon is a whole working life — its use is as a warning illustration that a 50% total spread over 40 years is only about 1% a year, so no wedding goal should be trusted for 40 years without regular review.

FAQs

What is a good ROI?
There is no single good figure. Compare returns over the same period and with the same risk before deciding.

Is this wedding calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is 1.02% a year over 40 years actually a wedding return?
Barely. A 50% total spread over 40 years is about 1.02% a year, which in many years would not keep up with rising wedding costs.

What does 40 years mean for wedding buying power?
It means today's $15,000 will buy far less at a wedding 40 years from now, so the fund must be judged on buying power, not on the nominal total.

What should a couple check before trusting a 40-year wedding figure?
They should check the yearly rate, the risk, the fees, and how wedding prices are expected to change, and they should review the plan regularly instead of trusting one 40-year figure.
