Wedding Savings Goal Monthly Deposit Calculator (5 Years)

Find the monthly deposit needed to reach a savings goal. This wedding version uses a 5-year planning term as its starting point.

—

This Monthly deposit is the regular amount to save every month for 60 months to reach the $50,000 goal, so saving steadily over this shorter term means a larger monthly amount than a longer plan would need.

Formula

Monthly deposit equals goal times monthly rate divided by one plus monthly rate to the power of months minus one.

How it works

Saving a fixed amount each month with interest reaches the goal faster than saving without interest.

Worked example

Goal amount $50,000, annual rate 5%, term 5 years. The monthly rate is 5% divided by 12, which is about 0.4167% a month, and the number of deposits is 5 times 12, which is 60 months. Using the page formula, the monthly deposit is $50,000 times the monthly rate divided by one plus the monthly rate to the power of 60, minus one, which gives $735.23. That is the calculator output for these defaults. Over 60 months the couple pays in $44,113.70 in total, so $5,886.30 of the $50,000 goal comes from interest. Totals use the calculator's unrounded figure, so the rounded monthly amount times 60 can differ by a dollar or two.

Who this term suits for a wedding: a couple with a wedding date roughly 5 years away, saving for the venue, catering, photography and dress, where $735.23 a month is a serious but reachable habit and the goal is close enough to plan in today's prices.

FAQs

How much should I save each month?
Divide the goal by the growth of regular deposits at the assumed rate to get the monthly amount.

Is this wedding calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is $735.23 a month realistic for a $50,000 wedding in 5 years?
It is a large monthly habit, but it is the honest figure for this goal and rate: $735.23 a month for 60 months pays in $44,113.70, and interest adds $5,886.30 to reach $50,000. If that monthly amount is too high, the wedding budget, the date, or both need to change, because the maths does not bend.

What if the couple can only save $500 a month for 5 years?
At 5%, $500 a month for 60 months reaches $34,003.04, which is $15,996.96 short of $50,000. The couple would need a smaller wedding budget, a later date, or a higher monthly deposit.

Should wedding prices be counted at today's prices for a 5-year plan?
Only roughly. Venue, catering and photography prices usually rise over 5 years, so a $50,000 wedding in today's prices may cost more by the wedding date. Review the goal every year and raise the deposit if prices move.
