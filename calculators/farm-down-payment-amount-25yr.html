Farm Down Payment Amount Calculator (25 Years)

Calculate a down payment from price and percentage. This farm version uses a 25-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Down payment equals price times down payment percentage divided by 100.

How it works

A larger down payment usually means a smaller loan and lower monthly payments.

Worked example

Price $300,000, down payment 20 percent, term 25 years. Using the page formula, down payment is $300,000 times 20 divided by 100, which is $60,000. The loan needed is $300,000 minus $60,000, which is $240,000. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults, and it is the same on every page in this group because the term is not an input. As an illustration only, not the output of this page, saving the whole $60,000 evenly over 25 years would mean about $2,400 a year, or about $200.00 a month, before interest, fees, taxes, or changes in the price. Over 25 years the monthly saving pace is only a fifth of the 5-year pace, so this term is really about succession and long-term ownership.

Who this term suits for a farm buyer: a farm family preparing the next generation to take over, saving slowly toward land or buildings that a son or daughter will farm.

FAQs

How much down payment do I need?
It depends on the lender and the purchase. A common planning figure is 20 percent, but requirements vary.

Is this farm calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is $200.00 a month for 25 years a serious farm plan?
Yes, if it is kept up for 300 months, but it only works if the saving is protected through good and bad seasons alike and reviewed as prices change.

How should a 25-year farm plan handle succession?
By naming who will farm the land and when, rather than saving in the abstract. The $60,000 is only useful if the purchase and the handover are planned together.

What if the farm is sold before 25 years?
The saved $60,000 is still the family's money, but the original purchase may no longer fit. A long plan should never lock savings into one inflexible use.
