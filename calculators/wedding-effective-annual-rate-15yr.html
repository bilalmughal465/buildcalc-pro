Wedding Effective Annual Rate Calculator (15 Years)

Convert a nominal annual rate to an effective annual rate. This wedding version uses a 15-year planning term as its starting point.

—

In plain terms, the Effective annual rate is the true yearly rate after compounding is taken into account, so it is slightly higher than the nominal rate when interest is compounded more than once a year.

Formula

Effective rate equals one plus nominal rate divided by periods, to the power of periods, minus one.

How it works

More frequent compounding raises the effective rate slightly above the nominal rate.

Worked example

Nominal rate 6%, compounded 12 times a year, term 15 years. The effective rate is one plus 0.06 divided by 12, to the power of 12, minus one, which gives 6.17%. That is the calculator's stated-formula result for these defaults, and it is the same on every page in this group because the term is not an input: only the nominal rate and the number of compounding periods are entered. On $10,000 for one year, the interest at the effective rate is $616.78, while at the nominal 6% it would be $600.00, so monthly compounding adds $16.78 in the first year. As an illustration only, $10,000 grown at the exact effective rate of 6.1678% for 15 years reaches $24,540.94, which is $14,540.94 of growth.

Who this term suits for a wedding: parents building a wedding fund for a child over about fifteen years, who need to know whether the headline rate on a loan or savings account is the rate they will really pay or earn.

FAQs

What is an effective rate?
The effective rate is the true yearly rate after allowing for compounding during the year.

Is this wedding calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does the effective rate matter more over 15 years for a wedding fund?
Because the compounding gap grows every year. At the exact effective rate of 6.1678%, $10,000 reaches $24,540.94 over fifteen years, which is $14,540.94 of growth. At a flat 6.00% it would reach only $23,965.58, so monthly compounding adds $575.36 over fifteen years on just $10,000.

Does the 6.17% figure stay the same for the whole 15 years?
Only if the nominal rate and the compounding frequency stay the same. The effective rate is fixed by the formula for a given nominal rate and number of periods, so 6% compounded monthly is 6.17% every year, but if the lender changes the nominal rate or the compounding frequency, the effective rate changes with it.

How should a couple use this figure when comparing 15-year wedding loans?
By comparing effective rates, not headline rates. A loan advertised at 6% compounded monthly costs 6.17% effective, while a loan advertised at 6.10% compounded yearly costs 6.10% effective, so the second loan is actually cheaper even though its headline rate looks higher.
