Car Future Value Monthly Savings Calculator (10 Years)

Estimate the future value of regular monthly savings. This car version uses a 10-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Future value uses the monthly rate and the number of monthly deposits to grow each deposit with interest.

How it works

Each monthly deposit earns interest from the month it is made until the end of the term.

Worked example

A car buyer sets aside $500 every month for 10 years at 6% a year. The monthly rate is 6% divided by 12, which is 0.5%, and there are 120 monthly deposits. Using the page formula, the future value is $500 times ((1.005 to the power of 120 minus 1) divided by 0.005), which is $81,939.67. The buyer paid in $60,000.00, so interest added $21,939.67, which is 26.8% of the final value.

Most of the result after only a few years is still the deposits themselves, because compounding has had little time to work, but as the years pass interest becomes a larger and larger share of the pot. That is why the later years add far more than the early years, even though the monthly deposit never changes. These figures assume a fixed 6% rate, with no fees, taxes, gaps, or changing rates, and they do not include car price rises, insurance, fuel, servicing, or registration, so they are planning estimates, not promises.

Who this term suits for a car buyer: a driver whose current car is reaching the end of its useful life and who wants the replacement paid for, or mostly paid for, in cash — for example someone running a ten-year-old hatchback who saves through its last years so the next car, a larger family car or a newer used model, does not have to go entirely on finance.

FAQs

How does monthly saving grow?
Regular deposits grow because each deposit earns interest, and that interest then earns more interest.

Is this car calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does 10 years more than double the 5-year result?
Because you deposit twice as much, $60,000.00 instead of $30,000.00, and every deposit earns interest for longer on average. Interest grows from $4,885.02 to $21,939.67, so the future value reaches $81,939.67, which is more than double $34,885.02. The extra $47,054.66 in the second five years comes from both new deposits and compounding on the first five years of saving.

Should I raise $500 a month as my pay rises over 10 years?
Yes, if you can afford it, because car prices also tend to rise over 10 years. This calculator holds the deposit fixed at $500, so raising it is the simplest way to keep pace with a more expensive replacement car. Even a small rise, kept up for the remaining years, adds more than the same rise made only in the final year.

Is a 10-year fund a good match for a car replacement cycle?
Often, yes. Many cars are kept for around ten years, so a ten-year saving habit lines up well with buying the next car. The risk is raiding the fund early for repairs or an upgrade you did not plan for, which leaves less for the replacement itself. Keeping the car fund separate from a repair budget helps avoid that.
