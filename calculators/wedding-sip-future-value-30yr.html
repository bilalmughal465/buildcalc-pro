Wedding SIP Future Value Calculator (30 Years)

Estimate the future value of a regular investment plan. This wedding version uses a 30-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Future value uses the monthly rate and number of monthly investments to grow each investment with interest.

How it works

Regular investing smooths timing and lets compounding build the value over time.

Worked example

Monthly investment $300, annual rate 8 percent, term 30 years. The monthly rate is 8 percent divided by 12, which is about 0.6667 percent a month, and the number of investments is 30 times 12, which is 360 months. Using the page formula, the future value is $300 times one plus 0.006667 to the power of 360, minus one, divided by 0.006667, which gives $447,107.83. That is the calculator output for these defaults. The couple pays in $108,000 in total, so $339,107.83 of the future value is interest.

Who this term suits for a wedding: a fund started a generation ahead, where the headline figure passes $400,000 but 30 years of price rises mean the wedding it buys may look very different from today's wedding.

FAQs

What is a SIP?
A SIP is a regular investment of a fixed amount, usually monthly, into a fund or plan.

Is this wedding calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does the 30-year figure pass $400,000?
Because compounding accelerates: the fund reaches $447,107.83 on $108,000 paid in, so $339,107.83 is interest. The last 10 years alone add far more than the first 10 years did.

Can a $447,107.83 fund still pay for a wedding after 30 years of price rises?
Only if wedding prices rise more slowly than the fund grows. At 3 percent inflation a $50,000 wedding today would cost far more in 30 years, so the fund should be judged against future prices, not today's prices.

What should a couple do if the money is needed early?
Use only what the plan allows and accept a smaller final figure. At 5 percent instead of 8 percent the same plan would give $249,677.59 over 30 years, so an early withdrawal or a lower return both cut the result sharply.
