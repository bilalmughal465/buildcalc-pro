Shop Retirement Corpus Calculator (15 Years)

Estimate a retirement corpus from monthly savings. This shop version uses a 15-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The Future value result is the total retirement fund built up after 15 years of monthly savings plus interest. A larger result means a bigger fund is ready at retirement, while a smaller result means the monthly saving or the rate needs to rise to reach the same goal.

Formula

Corpus uses the monthly rate and number of monthly savings to grow each saving with interest.

How it works

Starting earlier matters more than saving a little more later, because interest has longer to compound.

Worked example

Monthly saving $500, annual rate 7 percent, term 15 years. The monthly rate is 7 percent divided by 12, which is about 0.5833 percent a month, and the number of savings is 15 times 12, which is 180 months. Using the page formula, the future value is $500 times one plus the monthly rate to the power of 180, minus one, divided by the monthly rate, which gives $158,481.15. That is the calculator output for these defaults. The shop owner pays in $90,000 in total, so $68,481.15 of the corpus is interest.

Who this term suits for a shop owner: a shop owner planning a retirement about 15 years out, for example building a fund alongside the shop so that selling or closing the shop is a choice, not a forced sale at whatever price is offered.

FAQs

When should I start saving for retirement?
As early as possible. Time in the market allows compounding to do more of the work.

Is this shop calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

How can interest be $68,481.15 on $90,000 of savings?
Because each monthly saving compounds for up to 15 years. Over 180 months the interest on early savings becomes a large share of the $158,481.15 total.

Is a 15-year shop retirement plan too long to trust?
Not if it is reviewed. A 15-year plan is long enough for rates and shop takings to change, so the saving and the rate assumption should be checked every year or two, not set once and forgotten.

What if the shop pauses savings when takings drop?
A pause costs future compounding, especially on early savings. Pausing for a few months hurts less than stopping for years, but the missed savings are never made up by interest alone.
