Shop Credit Card Monthly Interest Calculator (35 Years)

Estimate monthly interest on a card balance. This shop version uses a 35-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Monthly interest equals balance times annual rate divided by 100 divided by 12.

How it works

Paying only interest leaves the balance unchanged. Paying more than interest reduces the balance.

Worked example

Balance $5,000, annual rate 18%, term 35 years. Using the page formula, monthly interest equals $5,000 times 18 divided by 100 divided by 12, which is $75.00 a month. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults, and it is the same on every page in this group because the term is not an input: only the balance and the rate decide the answer.

If only the $75.00 of interest is paid every month for 35 years, the total interest paid over 420 months is $31,500, and the $5,000 balance is still owed at the end, because interest-only payments never reduce the balance. That total is an illustration only, not a second output of this page: it assumes the rate stays at 18% and no new spending is added to the card.

Who this term suits for a shop owner: nobody as an ordinary shop plan, because a 35-year horizon is a lifetime warning, showing how a card balance can outlive the shop fittings, the lease, and the owner working life.

FAQs

Why is card interest high?
Card rates are usually higher than loan rates, so balances grow quickly if only minimum payments are made.

Is this shop calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does the total matter more than the $75 headline at 35 years?
Because $75 times 420 months is $31,500, more than six times the $5,000 borrowed, and that total is what the shop actually pays for borrowing $5,000.

Who repays a 35-year card balance if the original shop owner retires?
Whoever takes over the shop or the debt. A card balance does not end when the owner retires, so it must be cleared or passed on deliberately, not left running.

Is 35 years too long to judge from one card balance figure?
Yes. No shop, rate, or card balance stays still for 35 years, so the figure is a warning about interest-only borrowing, not a plan to follow.
