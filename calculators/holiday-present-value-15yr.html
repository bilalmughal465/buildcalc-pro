Holiday Present Value Calculator (15 Years)

Find how much a future amount is worth today. This holiday version uses a 15-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The "Present value" figure is the amount you would need to set aside today, at the rate you entered, for it to grow to your future amount over 15 years, so a lower figure than the future amount shows how much of that future cost is covered by growth over time rather than by money set aside today.

Formula

Present value equals future amount divided by one plus rate to the power of years.

How it works

A future amount is worth less today because money today can earn interest.

Worked example

Future amount $50,000, annual rate 5%, term 15 years. First, one plus the rate is 1.05, and 1.05 to the power of 15 is about 2.07893. Using the page formula, present value is $50,000 divided by 2.07893, which is $24,050.85. That is the calculator's own output for the page defaults. The discount is $50,000 minus $24,050.85, which is $25,949.15, so waiting 15 years removes $25,949.15 of value in present-day money at 5% a year.

After 15 years the present value is less than half of the future amount, because 1.05 raised to 15 more than doubles. If the amount set aside today grew at a steady 5% a year, it would reach $50,000 in 15 years.

Who this term suits for a holiday saver: a traveller planning a once-in-a-lifetime trip in about 15 years, such as a long safari, a round-the-world journey, or a return to a childhood holiday place with their own children, checking what a far-off dream trip is worth in present-day money.

FAQs

What is present value?
Present value is what a future sum is worth today after allowing for interest over time.

Is this holiday calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why is $24,050.85 less than half of $50,000?
Because 1.05 to the power of 15 is about 2.07893, more than 2. Dividing $50,000 by a factor above 2 must give less than half of $50,000.

How does the 15-year figure help with a once-in-a-lifetime trip?
It turns a distant $50,000 price into a present-day target of $24,050.85, so the saving needed now can be compared with other goals, instead of treating a far-off trip as if it cost $50,000 today.

Should I add inflation on top of this 5% rate for a 15-year holiday?
No, not on top of it here. The 5% rate is the single discount rate entered on this page. If holiday prices are expected to rise faster, enter a larger future amount or a different rate, and read the result as a new planning estimate, not as a forecast.
