Wedding Break Even Units Calculator (5 Years)

Find how many units must be sold to break even. This wedding version uses a 5-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Break even units equal fixed costs divided by price minus variable cost per unit.

How it works

At break even, total revenue exactly covers total costs, with no profit and no loss.

Worked example

Fixed costs $10,000, price per unit $50, variable cost per unit $30, term 5 years. The contribution per unit is $50 minus $30, which is $20. Dividing $10,000 by $20 gives 500 break even units. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults, and it is the same on every page in this group because the term is not an input: only fixed costs, price and variable cost are entered. At 500 units, revenue is 500 times $50, which is $25,000, and total cost is $10,000 plus 500 times $30, which is also $25,000, so profit is zero. As an illustration only, spreading 500 units over 5 years means about 100 units a year, or about 8.33 a month, on average.

Who this term suits for a wedding: a new wedding business finding its feet, such as a wedding photographer or videographer booking their first seasons, a florist building a local reputation, or a small catering team covering its start-up costs within about 5 years.

FAQs

What is break even?
Break even is the point where revenue covers all costs, so profit is zero.

Is this wedding calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

How many wedding packages a year cover 500 units in 5 years?
100 units a year, or about 8.33 a month on average, as an illustration only. The calculator itself only gives the 500-unit total; the yearly pace is 500 divided by 5.

Is 500 units a realistic target for a new wedding business in 5 years?
It depends on the business. A photographer selling 100 wedding packages a year is busy but plausible in a large city, while a venue selling 100 full weddings a year is a very large operation. Compare the pace with real bookings, not just the total.

What happens if the variable cost rises above $30?
The contribution per unit falls and break even rises. At a variable cost of $35, the contribution is $50 minus $35, which is $15, so break even becomes $10,000 divided by $15, which is about 667 units.
