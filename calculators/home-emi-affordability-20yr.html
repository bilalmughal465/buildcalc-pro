Home EMI Affordability Calculator (20 Years)

Estimate a safe monthly EMI from income. This home version uses a 20-year planning term as its starting point.

—

This amount is the largest monthly payment that fits the share of income you chose, leaving the rest of your income for living costs and other debts; it is a planning guide, not a loan approval, so check the total loan cost and your other commitments before borrowing.

Formula

Safe EMI equals monthly income times safe share divided by 100.

How it works

Lenders often use a share of income as a guide, but other debts also matter.

Worked example

Monthly home income $5,000, safe EMI share 30 percent, term 20 years. Using the page formula, safe EMI equals $5,000 times 30 divided by 100, which is $1,500 per month. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults, and it is the same on every page in this group because the term is not an input.

As an illustration only, not the output of this page, paying $1,500 every month for 20 years would total $360,000 in monthly payments. That total is not the loan amount you can borrow, because this page does not include interest, fees, or a changing rate, and home income will not stay at $5,000 every month for 20 years.

Who this term suits for a home buyer: a family taking a standard home mortgage for the house they expect to live in while raising children, where the EMI must still fit after school costs, car costs, and regular home maintenance are paid.

FAQs

How much EMI can I afford?
A common guide is 30 percent of monthly income, but total debts and living costs must also fit.

Is this home calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is $1,500 a month for 20 years realistic for a home?
It is realistic only if household income stays near $5,000 or grows, and if no large extra debt is added. Twenty years is long enough for jobs, health, and family size to change, so the plan should be reviewed every few years, not set once and forgotten.

What if interest rates rise during a 20-year home loan?
This page does not include interest or a changing rate, so a rise in rates would change the real monthly cost of a mortgage even though the $1,500 safe figure stays the same. A lender's quote, not this planning figure, shows what the actual EMI would be.

Should other home debts be counted before a 20-year commitment?
Yes. Car loans, card balances, and any second loan all compete with the same $5,000 of income. The safe EMI should be worked out after all other monthly debts, not before them.
