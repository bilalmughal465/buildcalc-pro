Travel Compound Amount Calculator (5 Years)

Calculate the amount after compound interest over time. This travel version uses a 5-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The Amount is the total you would have at the end of the 5-year term, including your original principal, while the Interest is the extra growth earned on top of that principal over the term.

Formula

Amount equals principal times one plus rate to the power of years.

How it works

Interest is added to the balance each year, so later interest is charged on a larger amount.

Worked example

Principal $10,000, annual rate 6 percent, term 5 years. Using the page formula, the amount is $10,000 times 1.06 to the power of 5, which is $13,382.26. That is the calculator output for these defaults.

The interest is $13,382.26 minus $10,000, which is $3,382.26. Because interest is added to the balance each year, the second year earns interest on $10,600, not just on the original $10,000, and the effect grows from there.

Who this term suits for a traveller: a traveller building a near-term trip fund, such as a round-the-world ticket, a sabbatical year, or a campervan deposit due in about 5 years, where the money must be ready while travel plans are still fresh.

FAQs

What is compound interest?
Compound interest is interest earned on both the principal and on interest already added.

Is this travel calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

How much would $10,000 grow to in 5 years at 6% for a travel fund?
It grows to $13,382.26, of which $3,382.26 is interest. That is the calculator output for a single $10,000 deposit at a fixed 6 percent, with no fees, taxes, or withdrawals counted.

Is 5 years long enough for compounding to matter for a traveller?
Yes, even over 5 years compounding adds $3,382.26 on $10,000, more than the $3,000 that simple interest at 6 percent would give. The gap widens every year the money stays invested.

Should a traveller count on 6% every year for 5 years?
No, 6 percent is only the entered assumption. Real returns vary year to year, and travel prices rise too, so treat $13,382.26 as a planning estimate and review the fund each year.
