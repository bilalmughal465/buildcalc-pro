Investment Loan EMI Calculator (20 Years)

Estimate the monthly EMI for a loan from amount, rate and term. This investment version uses a 20-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

EMI equals principal times monthly rate times one plus monthly rate to the power of payments, divided by that power minus one.

How it works

This assumes a fixed rate and equal monthly payments with no extra fees.

Worked example

Loan amount $200,000, annual rate 6.5%, term 20 years. The monthly rate is 6.5% divided by 12, which is about 0.5417% a month, and the number of payments is 20 times 12, which is 240 months. Using the page formula, the monthly EMI is $1491.15. Total paid over the term is $1491.15 times 240, which is $357875.11, so total interest is $357875.11 minus $200,000, which is $157875.11. That is the calculator's own output for these defaults.

Who this term suits for an investor: an investor building a long-term rental portfolio or funding a business premises, where twenty years spreads the cost thin enough for rent to cover the EMI with a margin for repairs and empty months.

FAQs

What is an EMI?
An EMI is a fixed monthly payment that covers both interest and principal over the loan term.

Is this investment calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is $1,491.15 a month realistic for a 20-year investment loan?
It is realistic if rent or business income covers it with a margin; over 20 years rents, rates and repair costs will all change, so the margin matters more than the starting figure.

What if interest rates rise during a 20-year investment loan?
This page assumes a fixed rate, so a rise would raise the real cost above $157,875.11 of interest; an investor should check whether the loan is fixed or variable before relying on this figure.

Should other investment debts be counted before a 20-year commitment?
Yes, because every other loan takes cash from the same rent or income, and a 20-year commitment leaves little room if another investment turns loss-making.
