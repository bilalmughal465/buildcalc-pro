Home Monthly Budget Split Calculator (10 Years)

Split monthly income into needs, wants and savings. This home version uses a 10-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Needs (50%) is the monthly amount for essentials such as housing and food, wants (30%) is the monthly amount for non-essentials, and savings (20%) is the monthly amount set aside, so the three figures together show whether the monthly income covers essentials while still leaving room to save.

Formula

Needs are 50 percent, wants are 30 percent and savings are 20 percent of income.

How it works

This 50-30-20 split is a planning guide, not a rule. Adjust it to your real costs.

Worked example

Monthly home income $4,000, term 10 years. Using the page formula, needs are 50 percent of $4,000, which is $2,000 a month; wants are 30 percent, which is $1,200 a month; and savings are 20 percent, which is $800 a month. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults, and it is the same on every page in this group because the term is not an input.

As an illustration only, not the output of this page, keeping that same $4,000 a month for 10 years (120 months) would mean total income of $480,000, split into $240,000 needs, $144,000 wants and $96,000 savings. In real life home income, rent or mortgage payments, and prices will not stay the same for 10 years, so these totals are a planning picture, not a promise.

Who this term suits for a home household: a family planning across a child's school years or a 10-year mortgage period, where keeping wants capped at $1,200 a month is what protects the $800 a month of savings while the mortgage is running.

FAQs

What is the 50-30-20 rule?
It is a simple budget guide: 50 percent needs, 30 percent wants and 20 percent savings.

Is this home calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does the same $800 a month look different over 10 years than over 5 years?
Because time turns a monthly habit into a large total. Over 10 years the same $800 a month adds up to $96,000 of savings before any interest, which is why a steady split matters more than any single month's result.

Should a home household keep the 50-30-20 split for the whole 10 years?
Not unchanged. Income, rent, school costs and mortgage payments all move over 10 years, so the split should be reviewed at least once a year and reset whenever a big home cost changes.

How should a 10-year home budget handle a new baby or a second child?
Needs almost always rise first, through food, clothing, childcare and a bigger home. The honest move is to let wants fall and to protect at least some savings, rather than pretending the old $2,000 needs figure still fits.
