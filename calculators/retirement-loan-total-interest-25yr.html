Retirement Loan Total Interest Calculator (25 Years)

Estimate total interest paid over a loan. This retirement version uses a 25-year planning term as its starting point.

—

In real terms, the Total interest is the extra amount paid on top of the amount borrowed over 25 years, and the Total paid is the borrowed amount plus that interest, so a higher Total interest means a larger share of the lifetime cost goes to the lender rather than repaying the original amount.

Formula

Total interest equals monthly EMI times number of payments minus the principal.

How it works

A longer term lowers the monthly payment but raises total interest.

Worked example

Loan amount $200,000, annual rate 6.5%, term 25 years. The monthly rate is 6.5% divided by 12, which is about 0.5417% a month, and the number of monthly payments is 25 times 12, which is 300 payments. Using the page formula, the monthly EMI is $1350.41, total paid is $1350.41 times 300, which is $405124.30, and total interest is $405124.30 minus $200,000, which is $205124.30. That is the calculator’s own result for these defaults.

By 25 years the interest, $205,124.30, is more than the amount borrowed, so the borrower pays back more than twice what was received.

Who this term suits for a retirement borrower: a borrower whose loan will outlive their working life by a long way, such as a property loan taken late in a career, where the debt will still be running deep into old age and may fall to the estate.

FAQs

Why does a longer loan cost more interest?
More payments mean interest is charged for longer, so total interest rises even if each payment is smaller.

Is this retirement calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why is the 25-year interest of $205,124.30 more than the amount borrowed?
Because the loan runs for 300 months, and interest is charged on the remaining balance every month, so over a long enough term the interest alone passes the original $200,000.

How should a retiree review a 25-year loan?
Every few years, checking pension income, remaining balance, and whether a lump sum from downsizing or savings could clear the loan early and save future interest.

What retirement risks grow over 25 years?
Health and care costs, changes to pension rules, and the chance that the borrower dies before the loan ends, leaving the debt to the estate.
