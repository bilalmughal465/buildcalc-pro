Travel Inflation Adjusted Value Calculator (25 Years)

Estimate what an amount will be worth after inflation. This travel version uses a 25-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Future amount equals amount times one plus inflation rate to the power of years.

How it works

Inflation means the same amount buys less over time, so future prices are higher.

Worked example

Amount 10,000 dollars, inflation rate 3 percent, term 25 years. Using the page formula, the future amount is 10,000 dollars times one plus 3 percent, to the power of 25, which is 20,937.78 dollars. That is the calculator output for these defaults. The increase over today's amount is 20,937.78 dollars minus 10,000 dollars, which is 10,937.78 dollars. That is a rise of about 109.38 percent in total over 25 years, so the price more than doubles, because prices at 3 percent a year double in about 23.45 years.

Who this term suits for a traveller: a traveller planning a trip a generation away, such as a golden-anniversary journey or a fund started for children to travel as adults, where today's 10,000 dollars buys less than half of what it buys today.

FAQs

What does inflation do to money?
Inflation reduces what money can buy, so a sum today will need to be larger in future to buy the same things.

Is this travel calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does 10,000 dollars more than double to 20,937.78 dollars over 25 years?
Because prices at 3 percent a year double in about 23.45 years, so by 25 years the doubling has already happened and a little more. Ten thousand dollars times 1.03 to the power of 25 is 20,937.78 dollars, an increase of 10,937.78 dollars.

Should a 25-year travel plan use today's prices at all?
Only as a starting point. Today's prices are useful for sizing the trip, but the fund must be grown for future prices, because 10,000 dollars in 25 years buys less than half of what it buys today at 3 percent a year.

How often should a 25-year travel inflation plan be recalculated?
At least every few years, and whenever flight, hotel, or fuel prices jump. A plan left alone for 25 years will almost certainly miss, because no single rate holds for a quarter of a century, so regular recalculation keeps the shortfall small and fixable.
