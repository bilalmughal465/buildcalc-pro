Holiday Loan Total Interest Calculator (10 Years)

Estimate total interest paid over a loan. This holiday version uses a 10-year planning term as its starting point.

—

In real terms, the Total interest is the extra cost of borrowing on top of the amount borrowed, and the Total paid is the amount borrowed plus that interest over 10 years.

Formula

Total interest equals monthly EMI times number of payments minus the principal.

How it works

A longer term lowers the monthly payment but raises total interest.

Worked example

Loan amount $200,000, annual rate 6.5%, term 10 years. The monthly rate is 6.5% divided by 12, which is 0.0054167, and there are 120 monthly payments. Using the page formula, the EMI is $2,270.96 a month, so the total paid is $272,515.15. Total interest is $272,515.15 minus $200,000, equal to $72,515.15, or 36.3% of the amount borrowed. That is about $7,251.52 of interest a year.

Doubling the term from 5 to 10 years cuts the monthly payment by $1,642.27, but it more than doubles the interest, from $34,793.78 to $72,515.15. That trade is the heart of this page: a lower monthly payment feels easier, yet the household pays for the holiday for twice as long and hands over far more interest in total.

Who this term suits for a holiday borrower: a household spreading the cost of a major holiday purchase, such as a holiday home, a motorhome, or a large family trip taken once and remembered for years, over a decade, so the monthly payment fits alongside normal living costs rather than dominating them.

FAQs

Why does a longer loan cost more interest?
More payments mean interest is charged for longer, so total interest rises even if each payment is smaller.

Is this holiday calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does interest more than double when the term doubles from 5 to 10 years?
Because interest is charged for twice as many months and the balance falls more slowly at first, so the total rises from $34,793.78 to $72,515.15 even though each monthly payment is smaller.

Is 10 years too long to pay for a holiday?
For a single trip, usually yes: the holiday itself lasts days or weeks, while the payments last a decade. For a holiday home or motorhome that is used year after year, a 10-year term is easier to defend, because the thing being paid for is still in use.

What if holiday prices rise during a 10-year holiday loan?
Rising prices do not change this fixed-rate example, but they do make future holidays dearer while the old loan is still being repaid, so the household should check that it can afford both the $2,270.96 payment and the holidays it still wants to take.
