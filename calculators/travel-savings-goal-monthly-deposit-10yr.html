Travel Savings Goal Monthly Deposit Calculator (10 Years)

Find the monthly deposit needed to reach a savings goal. This travel version uses a 10-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The Monthly deposit is the amount to save every month for 120 monthly deposits over 10 years to reach the goal amount, assuming the stated annual rate stays the same.

Formula

Monthly deposit equals goal times monthly rate divided by one plus monthly rate to the power of months minus one.

How it works

Saving a fixed amount each month with interest reaches the goal faster than saving without interest.

Worked example

Goal amount 50,000 dollars, annual rate 5 percent, term 10 years. The monthly rate is 5 percent divided by 12, which is about 0.4167 percent a month, and the number of deposits is 10 times 12, which is 120 months. Using the page formula, the monthly deposit is 50,000 dollars times the monthly rate, divided by one plus the monthly rate to the power of 120, minus one, which gives 321.99 dollars a month. That is the calculator output for these defaults. Over the full term the traveller deposits 38,639.31 dollars in total, so 11,360.69 dollars of the 50,000 dollar goal comes from interest. Without any interest, the same goal would need 416.67 dollars a month, so the assumed 5 percent return saves 94.67 dollars a month.

Who this term suits for a traveller: a traveller building a decade-long travel fund, such as saving for a family world trip, a retirement travel fund, or a second home abroad that will be bought in about 10 years.

FAQs

How much should I save each month?
Divide the goal by the growth of regular deposits at the assumed rate to get the monthly amount.

Is this travel calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is 321.99 dollars a month realistic for a 10-year travel goal?
For many travellers it is, because 321.99 dollars a month is less than half the no-interest figure of 416.67 dollars. The risk is not the monthly amount but keeping it going for 120 months, so setting up an automatic transfer on payday is the simplest way to stay on track.

What does the 11,360.69 dollars of growth actually mean here?
It means interest and compounding supply 11,360.69 dollars of the 50,000 dollar goal, while the traveller's own deposits supply 38,639.31 dollars. Without any interest, the same goal would need 416.67 dollars a month, so the assumed 5 percent return saves 94.67 dollars a month.

Should a traveller change the plan if flight and hotel prices rise over 10 years?
Yes, the goal itself should be reviewed. A 50,000 dollar trip in today's prices will cost more in 10 years if travel prices rise, so the traveller should either raise the monthly deposit as income grows or accept a shorter trip for the same fund.
