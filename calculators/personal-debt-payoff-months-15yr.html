Personal Debt Payoff Months Calculator (15 Years)

Estimate months to pay off a debt with fixed payments. This personal version uses a 15-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The "Months to pay off" figure is the number of months until the balance reaches zero at the monthly payment you entered, and if the payment does not cover the monthly interest, the calculator instead shows that the payment is too low to pay off this debt.

Formula

Months are found from the balance, monthly rate and monthly payment using the loan payoff formula.

How it works

If the payment is less than monthly interest, the balance will not fall.

Worked example

Balance $10,000, annual rate 12%, monthly payment $300. First, the monthly rate is 12% divided by 12, which is 0.01, so the first month's interest is $10,000 times 0.01, which is $100. The $300 payment covers that interest and leaves $200 for the balance in the first month. Using the loan payoff formula, the calculator shows 40.7 months to pay off the debt. That is about 3.4 years. Over the full payoff, the total paid is about $12,224.67, and total interest is about $2,224.67. That total is calculated from the unrounded 40.75 months exactly as the calculator does, not from 40.7 times $300, which would give $12,210. The last payment is a smaller, partial payment, not a full $300. Against a 15-year plan, that payoff uses only 22.6% of the 180 months, leaving 139.3 months, about 11.6 years, after the debt is gone, for planning comparison only.

Who this term suits for a person:A 15-year term suits a person who wants a long, steady discipline period around one debt, for example someone who has cleared cards before and slipped back into borrowing, or a household building a habit of living without that $300 payment going to a lender. The debt itself should not take 15 years; the 15 years are for proving the new habit holds.

FAQs

What if my payment is too low?
If the payment does not cover monthly interest, the debt will grow instead of shrinking.

Is this personal calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is a 15-year plan too long for a $10,000 debt?
As a payoff target, yes, because $300 a month at 12% clears it in 40.7 months, not 180 months. As a discipline period, a 15-year view can still be useful, for example to check that no new debt replaces the old one. Do not stretch the payment to fill 15 years, because that only adds interest.

How much interest do I save by paying $400 instead of $300?
At $400 a month, the same $10,000 at 12% clears in 28.9 months, with total paid of about $11,564.72 and interest of about $1,564.72. Compared with $300 a month, which costs about $2,224.67 in interest, the higher payment saves about $659.95 in interest and finishes about 11.8 months sooner. Check both payments in the calculator before choosing.

What if I miss a payment in the middle of the plan?
A missed payment does not change the formula, but it leaves the balance higher for longer, so interest keeps building and the payoff moves later than 40.7 months. One missed month is a delay, not a fresh start, so the planning fix is to resume the $300 payment as soon as possible and re-check the remaining months with the current balance.
