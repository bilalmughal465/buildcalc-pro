Business Present Value Calculator (5 Years)

Find how much a future amount is worth today. This business version uses a 5-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The Present value figure is what the future amount is worth in today's money at the rate and term entered, so it is the amount to compare with cash available today rather than with the future amount itself.

Formula

Present value equals future amount divided by one plus rate to the power of years.

How it works

A future amount is worth less today because money today can earn interest.

Worked example

Future amount $50,000, annual rate 5%, term 5 years. First, the discount factor is 1.05 to the power of 5, which is 1.276282. Present value is $50,000 divided by 1.276282, which is $39,176.31. The discount is $50,000 minus $39,176.31, which is $10,823.69, or 21.6% of the future amount. After only 5 years most of the value is still there, with 78.4% retained, so a short wait costs the business about one fifth of the sum in today's money.

Who this term suits for a business: A 5-year term suits a business deciding on a deferred payment, for example a supplier or client offering $50,000 in 5 years instead of cash now. At $39,176.31 today, the business can judge a cash discount: accepting anything well below $39,176.31 now would be worse than waiting, while a cash offer close to that figure may be better than waiting, once fees and risk are checked.

FAQs

What is present value?
Present value is what a future sum is worth today after allowing for interest over time.

Is this business calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is $39,176.31 a fair cash price for $50,000 in 5 years?
At a 5% rate, yes, as a planning figure: $50,000 divided by 1.05 to the power of 5 is $39,176.31. A cash offer well below that would be worse than waiting on these numbers alone, but fees, tax, and the risk that the payer may not pay must still be checked before accepting either option.

Why is the discount only 21.6% after 5 years?
Because discounting compounds slowly at first. The factor after 5 years is only 1.276282, so $50,000 divided by 1.276282 keeps 78.4% of its value. The discount grows much faster in later years, which is why a 5-year wait costs far less, in proportion, than a 20-year wait.

Should a business use 5% for a 5-year decision?
Only if 5% reflects what the business could otherwise earn or borrow at over those 5 years. If its real cost of money is higher, the present value falls below $39,176.31, and if it is lower, the present value rises. This calculator assumes a fixed 5% rate and no fees, so test the result at the business's own rate too.
