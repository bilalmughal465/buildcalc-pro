Home SIP Future Value Calculator (5 Years)

Estimate the future value of a regular investment plan. This home version uses a 5-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The Future value figure is the estimated total worth of your monthly investments at the end of 5 years, including the growth earned along the way, rather than only the sum of the monthly payments.

Formula

Future value uses the monthly rate and number of monthly investments to grow each investment with interest.

How it works

Regular investing smooths timing and lets compounding build the value over time.

Worked example

Monthly investment $300, annual rate 8%, term 5 years. The monthly rate is 8% divided by 12, which is about 0.6667% a month, and the number of monthly investments is 5 times 12, which is 60 months. Using the page formula, the future value is $300 times one plus the monthly rate to the power of 60, minus one, divided by the monthly rate, which gives $22,043.06. That is the calculator's own output for these defaults. The deposits actually paid in total $18,000, so growth is $22,043.06 minus $18,000, which is $4,043.06. All figures are estimates for planning only, not financial advice, and they assume a steady 8% every year with no fees, taxes, or missed deposits.

Who this term suits for a home household: a home household saving toward a near-term, known home cost, such as a home deposit top-up, a roof repair, or a kitchen refit due in about 5 years, where the monthly amount must be protected even when an unexpected home bill arrives.

FAQs

What is a SIP?
A SIP is a regular investment of a fixed amount, usually monthly, into a fund or plan.

Is this home calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Can a home household really afford $300 a month for 5 years?
Only if $300 fits after rent or mortgage payments, utility bills, groceries, and an emergency buffer. If it squeezes everyday home costs, start smaller and raise the amount when pay rises, rather than stopping altogether.

Why does growth add only $4,043.06 over 5 years?
Because compounding needs time. Over 60 months most of the $22,043.06 is the $18,000 actually paid in, and only $4,043.06 is growth. The same $300 a month earns far more growth over longer terms on this page.

What if a home repair comes up before the 5 years are done?
Do not raid the whole fund for a small repair if it can be covered from a separate emergency buffer. Taking money out early breaks the compounding run and leaves the home goal short.
