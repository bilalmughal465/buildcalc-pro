Business Down Payment Amount Calculator (10 Years)

Calculate a down payment from price and percentage. This business version uses a 10-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Down payment equals price times down payment percentage divided by 100.

How it works

A larger down payment usually means a smaller loan and lower monthly payments.

Worked example

Price $300,000, down payment 20%. Down payment is $300,000 times 20, divided by 100, which is $60,000. The loan needed is $300,000 minus $60,000, which is $240,000. The 10-year term does not change the calculator result; it is the planning horizon the business uses to build the deposit.

Saving illustration only, not the output of this calculator: setting aside $60,000 over 10 years means $500.00 a month for 120 months, with no interest counted, which is easier on monthly cash flow than the 5-year plan but ties the money up for twice as long.

Who this term suits for a business: Who this term suits for a business: a growing restaurant or retail business planning a second branch, where steady retained profit over a decade can fund the deposit without stopping the first branch from trading.

FAQs

How much down payment do I need?
It depends on the lender and the purchase. A common planning figure is 20 percent, but requirements vary.

Is this business calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

How does a 10-year saving plan change a growing business's cash flow?
At $500.00 a month the drag is smaller than the 5-year plan, but the money is still locked away, so the business needs a separate emergency fund and must not count the deposit as working capital.

Is a $60,000 down payment enough for a second branch?
It is only the deposit on a $300,000 purchase, so the business must also budget for fit-out, stock, staff, and the first months of trading, which this calculator does not include.

What happens if the business needs the deposit money halfway through 10 years?
Dipping into it delays the purchase and can break the lender's view of the business, so the deposit account should be kept separate from day-to-day trading cash.
