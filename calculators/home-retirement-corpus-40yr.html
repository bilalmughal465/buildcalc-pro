Home Retirement Corpus Calculator (40 Years)

Estimate a retirement corpus from monthly savings. This home version uses a 40-year planning term as its starting point.

—

The Future value is the total amount your monthly savings are expected to grow to by retirement after 40 years, including interest earned along the way, so it is the lump sum available to support living costs in retirement rather than only the money you paid in.

Formula

Corpus uses the monthly rate and number of monthly savings to grow each saving with interest.

How it works

Starting earlier matters more than saving a little more later, because interest has longer to compound.

Worked example

Monthly saving $500, annual rate 7%, term 40 years. The monthly rate is 7% divided by 12, which is about 0.5833% a month, and the number of deposits is 40 times 12, which is 480 months. Using the page formula, the corpus is $500 times one plus the monthly rate to the power of 480, minus one, divided by the monthly rate, which gives $1312406.70. That is the calculator's own output for these defaults. Deposits paid in total $240000, so growth from interest is $1312406.70 minus $240000, which is $1072406.70.

Who this term suits for a home household: a lifetime home illustration rather than an ordinary purchase plan, for example a household starting out today and saving across a whole working life toward retiring mortgage-free in about 40 years, where the real cost is 480 months of discipline, not any single deposit.

FAQs

When should I start saving for retirement?
As early as possible. Time in the market allows compounding to do more of the work.

Is this home calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is $500 a month for 40 years a realistic retirement plan at home?
As a habit it is realistic, but as a fixed amount it is not: $500 will mean far less in 40 years, so the deposit should rise with income and inflation over such a long plan.

Why is the 40-year growth figure so large?
Because $240,000 of deposits has 480 months to compound at 7%, growth reaches $1,072,406.70. That figure only holds if 7% is earned every single year with nothing withdrawn.

Should a 40-year home saver just set $500 and forget it?
No. Review the plan every few years, raise the deposit when pay rises, and recheck the rate assumption, because a 40-year plan left alone will drift away from real home costs.
