Shop Debt Payoff Months Calculator (15 Years)

Estimate months to pay off a debt with fixed payments. This shop version uses a 15-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Months are found from the balance, monthly rate and monthly payment using the loan payoff formula.

How it works

If the payment is less than monthly interest, the balance will not fall.

Worked example

Balance $10,000, annual rate 12%, monthly payment $300, term 15 years. The monthly rate is 12% divided by 12, which is 1% a month, so the first month of interest on $10,000 is $100. A $300 payment covers that $100 of interest and puts $200 toward the balance in the first month. Using the loan payoff formula, the months to pay off are 40.7. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults, and it is the same on every page in this group because the term is not an input: only the balance, the rate and the monthly payment are entered. Over 40.7 months at $300 a month, the total paid is about $12,225, so the interest paid is about $12,225 minus $10,000, which is $2,225. Against a 15-year planning window of 180 months, the debt is cleared about 139.3 months before the window ends, as a comparison only, not as a second output of this page.

Who this term suits for a shop owner: a shop planning a refit or expansion over a medium horizon, where the debt is cleared in about 41 months and the freed $300 a month can then be put into stock, repairs or a savings buffer for the rest of the 15 years.

FAQs

What if my payment is too low?
If the payment does not cover monthly interest, the debt will grow instead of shrinking.

Is this shop calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Does a 15-year shop plan change the 40.7-month answer?
No. The term is not an input on this page, so the payoff stays 40.7 months. The 15-year window only changes the comparison: the debt is cleared about 139.3 months before the window ends.

How should a shop use the freed $300 a month after month 41?
Once the debt is gone, the $300 a month can go to stock, shop repairs, a savings buffer or the next planned purchase, instead of interest. Planning that use in advance stops a new debt quietly taking the old one's place.

What if shop takings force a missed payment in year 3?
A missed payment leaves interest running, so the balance falls more slowly and the payoff moves past 40.7 months. One missed month is recoverable if later payments catch up, but repeated misses can turn a short debt into a long one.
