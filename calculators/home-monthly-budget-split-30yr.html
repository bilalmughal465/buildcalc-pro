Home Monthly Budget Split Calculator (30 Years)

Split monthly income into needs, wants and savings. This home version uses a 30-year planning term as its starting point.

—

This result shows how one month of household income is divided: the needs amount covers essential costs such as housing and food, the wants amount covers non-essential spending, and the savings amount is what remains set aside each month, so a 30-year plan stays affordable only if the needs amount continues to cover real essential costs as prices change.

Formula

Needs are 50 percent, wants are 30 percent and savings are 20 percent of income.

How it works

This 50-30-20 split is a planning guide, not a rule. Adjust it to your real costs.

Worked example

Monthly home income $4,000, term 30 years. Using the page formula, needs are 50 percent of $4,000, which is $2,000 a month; wants are 30 percent, which is $1,200 a month; and savings are 20 percent, which is $800 a month. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults, and it is the same on every page in this group because the term is not an input.

As an illustration only, not the output of this page, keeping that same $4,000 a month for 30 years (360 months) would mean total income of $1,440,000, split into $720,000 needs, $432,000 wants and $288,000 savings. In real life home income, rent or mortgage payments, and prices will not stay the same for 30 years, so these totals are a planning picture, not a promise.

Who this term suits for a home household: a household thinking across a full mortgage term, where the monthly split is less about one year's discipline and more about whether the home remains affordable for three decades of changing income, prices and family size.

FAQs

What is the 50-30-20 rule?
It is a simple budget guide: 50 percent needs, 30 percent wants and 20 percent savings.

Is this home calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is a 30-year home budget the same as a house mortgage plan?
No. A mortgage plan covers one loan, while this split covers the whole household: needs, wants and savings together. The mortgage payment sits inside the $2,000 needs figure, not beside it.

What happens if household income falls for several years in a 30-year plan?
Needs do not fall with it, so wants must fall first and savings may have to pause. A plan that assumes $4,000 a month for 30 years without interruption will break in the first long income dip.

Should wants stay at $1,200 a month for the whole 30 years?
No. Wants should move with income and with life stage: higher when children are young and costs are lower, lower when the mortgage or school costs rise, and never fixed for three decades.
