Wedding Credit Card Monthly Interest Calculator (30 Years)

Estimate monthly interest on a card balance. This wedding version uses a 30-year planning term as its starting point.

—

This monthly interest figure is the cost of carrying the balance for one month at the entered rate, before any payment toward the balance itself, so paying only this amount leaves the balance unchanged and the same charge can repeat each month.

Formula

Monthly interest equals balance times annual rate divided by 100 divided by 12.

How it works

Paying only interest leaves the balance unchanged. Paying more than interest reduces the balance.

Worked example

Balance $5,000, annual rate 18%, term 30 years. Using the page formula, the monthly interest is $5,000 times 18 divided by 100 divided by 12, which gives $75.00 a month. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults, and it is the same on every page in this group because the term is not an input: only the balance and the rate are entered. Paying only that $75 of interest leaves the balance unchanged, so over 360 months the interest paid is 360 times $75, which is $27,000, and the $5,000 is still owed at the end. This total is an illustration only, assuming the rate stays at 18% and no payments beyond interest are made; it is not a second output of this page.

Who this term suits for a wedding: a couple comparing a wedding card balance with a house mortgage over the same span, where the card rate makes the wedding debt far more expensive than the same amount borrowed as a mortgage.

FAQs

Why is card interest high?
Card rates are usually higher than loan rates, so balances grow quickly if only minimum payments are made.

Is this wedding calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is a 30-year wedding balance the same as a house mortgage?
No. A mortgage is repaid over 30 years and ends with the house owned. A card balance paid interest-only for 30 years costs $27,000 and still leaves $5,000 owed, with nothing owned at the end.

What happens if the couple stops earning for a time in a 30-year plan?
Missed payments usually add fees and penalty interest on top of the $75 a month, so the balance can grow even while no wedding spending happens. A fixed-term loan with a clear end date is less fragile than an open card balance.

Should a couple keep using the same card while repaying over 30 years?
No. New spending on the same card adds to the balance and to the monthly interest, so the $75 figure rises and the debt never falls. Repayment works only when new card spending stops.
