Wedding Loan Total Interest Calculator (35 Years)

Estimate total interest paid over a loan. This wedding version uses a 35-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

Total interest equals monthly EMI times number of payments minus the principal.

How it works

A longer term lowers the monthly payment but raises total interest.

Worked example

Loan $200,000, annual rate 6.5%, term 35 years. The monthly rate is 6.5% divided by 12, which is about 0.5417% a month, and the number of payments is 35 times 12, which is 420. Using the EMI formula, the monthly payment is $1,208.31. Over 420 payments the total paid is $507,489.63, so the total interest is $507,489.63 minus $200,000, which is $307,489.63. That is the calculator's own result for these defaults.

Who this term suits for a wedding: A couple on a lifetime household timeline, where a wedding loan has been stretched to thirty-five years for the lowest possible payment, $1,208.31 a month, even though the interest, $307,489.63, is more than one and a half times the loan.

FAQs

Why does a longer loan cost more interest?
More payments mean interest is charged for longer, so total interest rises even if each payment is smaller.

Is this wedding calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Why does the total matter more than the $1,208.31 headline at 35 years?
Because the headline hides the total: $507,489.63 is paid back on a $200,000 loan, so interest alone is $307,489.63. Judging a 35-year loan by its monthly payment alone badly understates its cost.

Who repays a 35-year wedding loan if the couple's circumstances change?
Whoever signed the loan is still liable, even after separation, job loss or moving abroad, unless the lender agrees otherwise. A 35-year commitment should be made only with that risk understood.

What if the wedding loan were only $100,000 instead of $200,000?
Halving the loan halves every figure at the same rate and term: the payment would be about $604.16 a month, the total paid about $253,744.82, and the total interest about $153,744.82.
