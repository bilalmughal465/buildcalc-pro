Business Compound Annual Growth Rate Calculator (5 Years)

Calculate compound annual growth over a period. This business version uses a 5-year planning term as its starting point.

—

Formula

CAGR equals ending value divided by starting value, to the power of one over years, minus one.

How it works

CAGR smooths growth into a single yearly rate, even if actual yearly growth varied.

Worked example

Starting value $10,000, ending value $20,000, term 5 years. The ratio is $20,000 divided by $10,000, which is 2.00. Raise 2.00 to the power of one over 5, subtract one, and multiply by 100: the compound annual growth rate is 14.87%. That means the business value would need to grow by about 14.87% every single year, steadily, to double in 5 years.

For a young business, 14.87% a year is fast growth: doubling in only 5 years means demand, pricing, or capacity grew quickly, and the next question is whether that pace can be staffed and funded without breaking quality. These figures are nominal, before inflation, fees, and tax, and actual yearly growth will have varied; CAGR smooths it into one rate.

Who this term suits for a business: a new cafe, online store, or small workshop judging its first 5 years: if revenue doubled this fast, the owner can test whether a second outlet, extra staff, or more stock is affordable, rather than guessing from one good year.

FAQs

What does CAGR mean?
CAGR is the steady yearly growth rate that would take a starting value to an ending value.

Is this business calculator exact?
No, it is an estimate for planning only, based on the numbers you enter.

Are these figures financial advice?
No, they are estimates for planning only, not financial advice. Check with a qualified adviser for decisions involving large sums.

Is 14.87% over 5 years a strong result for a small business?
Yes, for most small businesses it is strong, because doubling in 5 years needs steady growth every year, not one lucky year. Compare it with rising costs, though: if costs rose nearly as fast, profit may not have doubled.

Does CAGR hide a bad year inside the 5 years?
Yes. CAGR smooths growth into one steady rate, so a flat or falling year followed by a boom year still shows 14.87%. Check the yearly figures before hiring or borrowing on the back of this rate.

Should a 5-year CAGR decide whether to expand?
No, not alone. Use it with cash flow, margins, and repeat customers. A 5-year CAGR of 14.87% supports expansion only if the business can fund it and keep serving existing customers well.
