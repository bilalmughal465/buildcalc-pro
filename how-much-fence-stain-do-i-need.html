How Much Fence Stain Do I Need?

A simple guide with a worked example, a size table and answers to the questions people actually ask.

Short answer

A 100 ft long, 6 ft high fence, stained on both sides with 1 coat, needs 8 gallons of stain at 150 sq ft per gallon. For 2 coats, the same fence needs 16 gallons. Use the formula below with your own fence size and your own stain can's coverage number, or try the Fence Stain Calculator for an instant result.

The formula

Gallons = length × height × sides × coats ÷ coverage per gallon, rounded up. Always round up to a whole gallon, because you cannot buy part of a gallon and you will want a little left for touch-ups.

Worked example: 100 ft × 6 ft fence

Step 1: one side is 100 × 6 = 600 sq ft. Step 2: both sides are 600 × 2 = 1,200 sq ft. Step 3: for 1 coat, divide by the coverage: 1,200 ÷ 150 = 8 gallons. Step 4: for 2 coats, double it: 8 × 2 = 16 gallons. That is the same result the Fence Stain Calculator gives for these numbers.

How much stain for common fence sizes

Both sides, at 150 sq ft per gallon, rounded up to whole gallons. A 5-gallon bucket holds 5 gallons, so divide the gallons by 5 and round up to get the number of buckets.

How to measure your fence

Measure the length along the fence, including gates, and add the sections together. Measure the height from the ground to the top of the boards. If you are staining only one side, use 1 side in the formula instead of 2. If your neighbour's side is already stained, count only your side.

What changes the number

The coverage number on your stain can matters most. Rough or weathered wood drinks more stain than smooth wood, so the label's top coverage number may be too generous for a rough fence. A second coat also changes the total, because it doubles the coverage area. When in doubt, use the lower coverage number, round up, and keep a little extra for touch-ups.

Common mistakes

The most common mistake is counting only one side of the fence, which halves the answer. The next is forgetting the second coat. The third is using the label's maximum coverage on rough wood. Check all three before you buy.

Frequently asked questions

How much stain do I need for a 100 ft fence?

A 100 ft long, 6 ft high fence, stained on both sides with 1 coat, needs 8 gallons at 150 sq ft per gallon. For 2 coats, it needs 16 gallons.

Should I stain both sides of a fence?

Yes, if you can. Both sides face sun, rain and ground splash, so both sides need protection. Counting both sides doubles the area: a 100 ft x 6 ft fence is 1,200 sq ft, not 600 sq ft.

How many coats of fence stain do I need?

One coat is enough for a refresh. Two coats last longer on new or bare wood. Always count the coats in the formula, because 2 coats need twice the stain.

How much area does one gallon of fence stain cover?

It depends on the wood and the product. Rough fence wood is often around 150 sq ft per gallon, and smooth wood can cover more. Always use the coverage number on your own stain can.

How much fence will a 5-gallon bucket of stain cover?

At 150 sq ft per gallon, a 5-gallon bucket covers 750 sq ft with one coat. That is less than a 100 ft x 6 ft fence stained on both sides, which is 1,200 sq ft.

Do gates count in the fence length?

Yes. Measure gates as part of the total fence length, because they need stain too. Measure each section, add the lengths together, then use the total in the formula.
