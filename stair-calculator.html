How to calculate stairs

Stairs need equal risers. Divide the total rise by the max riser height and round up to get the number of risers, then divide the total rise by that number for the exact riser height. Treads are always one less than risers.

Formula: risers = total rise ÷ max riser height, rounded up, and riser height = total rise ÷ risers

Worked example: 105 inch rise, 10 inch treads

Risers: 105 ÷ 7.75 = 13.55, rounded up to 14 risers. Each riser: 105 ÷ 14 = 7.5 inches. Treads: 14 − 1 = 13 treads. Total run: 13 × 10 = 130 inches.

Keep risers equal

Uneven risers trip people. If your math gives uneven heights, adjust the riser count until every riser matches within a tiny fraction.

Frequently asked questions

How many steps do I need for a 105 inch rise?

A 105 inch rise needs 14 risers at 7.5 inches each, with 13 treads and a total run of 130 inches at a 10 inch tread depth.

How tall should each step be?

Risers are usually 7 to 7.75 inches. Keep every riser the same height, or the stair feels unsafe.

How deep should treads be?

About 10 inches for comfortable stairs. Building codes usually need at least 10 inches.

Why is total run less than risers times tread depth?

There is one less tread than risers, because the top floor itself is the last step.

Does this follow building codes?

It follows common US rules, but always check your local code for riser height, tread depth and headroom.
